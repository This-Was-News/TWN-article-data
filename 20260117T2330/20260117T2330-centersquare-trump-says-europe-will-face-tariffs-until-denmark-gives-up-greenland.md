## Article summary
President Donald Trump has announced he will impose tariffs on European countries until the U.S. acquires Greenland, citing strategic and resource interests. Trump believes only the U.S. can protect Greenland from China or Russia, highlighting the island's critical minerals and strategic location. He stated that Denmark and other European nations have benefited from U.S. subsidies without tariffs for years and it's time for Denmark to reciprocate. Trump intends to buy Greenland, but has not ruled out other acquisition methods, despite Denmark and Greenland officials stating the nation is not for sale and Greenlanders do not wish to join the U.S. The proposed tariffs, starting at 10% on February 1 and increasing to 25% on June 1, will remain until a deal for Greenland's purchase is reached. Trump claims this acquisition is vital for national security, especially with potential new shipping and military routes opening in the Arctic due to melting ice. This move follows previous U.S. attempts to acquire Greenland, dating back to 1867 and 1946, and comes amidst heightened U.S. ally troop presence in Greenland for unspecified reasons. Experts suggest these tariffs reveal the arbitrary nature of Trump's trade policies and the potential economic and geopolitical issues arising from unchecked executive power. The legality of Trump's tariffs is also being challenged in the Supreme Court, with a potential refund of over $133.5 billion at stake.
