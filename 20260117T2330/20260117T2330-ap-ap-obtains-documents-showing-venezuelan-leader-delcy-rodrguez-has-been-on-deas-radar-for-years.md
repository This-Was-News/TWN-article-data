## Article summary
Documents obtained by the Associated Press reveal that Delcy Rodríguez, Venezuela's acting president, has been a priority target for the U.S. Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) since at least 2022. The DEA has compiled an intelligence file on Rodríguez since 2018, detailing allegations of drug trafficking and gold smuggling. Confidential informants have linked her to money laundering operations and to Nicolás Maduro's alleged bagman, Alex Saab. While the U.S. government has not publicly accused Rodríguez of criminal wrongdoing, her name has appeared in numerous DEA investigations. This interest in Rodríguez persists even as President Trump has positioned her as a potential partner for stabilizing Venezuela post-Maduro. Opposition leader María Corina Machado stated that the American justice system possesses sufficient information about Rodríguez. Rodríguez previously worked to court American investment during the first Trump administration and was sanctioned by the White House in 2018 for her role in Maduro's government. The DEA's focus on Rodríguez, particularly through its Special Operations Division, highlights the long-standing U.S. scrutiny of power structures in Venezuela, a country ranked as the world's third most corrupt by Transparency International. The DEA records also indicate investigations into her potential involvement in corrupt government contracts and deals with associates.
