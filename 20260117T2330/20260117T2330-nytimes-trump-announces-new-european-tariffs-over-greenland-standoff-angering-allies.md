## Article summary
President Trump announced new tariffs on several European countries, including Denmark, Norway, Sweden, France, Germany, Britain, the Netherlands, and Finland, in an effort to pressure them into negotiating the sale of Greenland. The tariffs, starting at 10% and potentially rising to 25%, are intended to force these nations to the negotiating table. European leaders reacted with unified outrage, condemning the move as unacceptable coercion and a threat to European sovereignty. Lawmakers in Washington, including some Republicans, also criticized the decision, with some likening it to tactics used by Russia and warning it could harm U.S. national security and alliances. The tariffs also cast doubt on a previously struck trade deal between the U.S. and the European Union, potentially escalating into a trade war. The legality of these tariffs is further complicated by a Supreme Court case concerning the president's use of emergency economic powers. Despite the strong international backlash and domestic criticism, Trump argued that the U.S. needs control of Greenland for strategic reasons against China and Russia, despite existing agreements allowing for U.S. military presence. European officials emphasized the need for dialogue but warned of a dangerous downward spiral if the tariffs are confirmed, reaffirming solidarity with Denmark and Greenland.
