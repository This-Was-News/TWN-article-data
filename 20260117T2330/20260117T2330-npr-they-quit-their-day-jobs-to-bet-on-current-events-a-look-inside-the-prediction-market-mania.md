## Article summary
Prediction markets like Kalshi and Polymarket are experiencing a boom, attracting traders who quit their day jobs to bet on current events, with some making significant profits. These platforms allow users to wager on a wide range of outcomes, from political races to everyday occurrences, fostering a belief among traders that they represent a movement against established elites and that collective opinion reveals core truths. Mainstream media outlets like CNN and CNBC are integrating Kalshi markets into their coverage, and The Wall Street Journal's owner is partnering with Polymarket, indicating growing acceptance. However, critics, such as the CEO of Better Markets, liken these platforms to gambling sites, raising concerns about addiction and potential market manipulation. Kalshi argues it differs from traditional gambling by not having a 'house,' but federal lawsuits challenge this, claiming the company's trading entity acts as a house. The Trump administration has favored the growth of these markets, with Donald Trump Jr. involved in Polymarket and Kalshi, and Truth Social planning its own prediction market. This permissive environment has ignited debates about the industry's potential for insider trading and its influence on real-world events, especially concerning elections. The addictive nature of these gamified trading apps, with their slick design and constant alerts, is a significant worry, leading to lawsuits alleging they foster gambling addiction. Traders often employ sophisticated statistical models and specialized lingo to navigate these markets, with some admitting to compulsive behavior and the intense, anxiety-inducing experience of live trading. Regulatory bodies like the CFTC face challenges in overseeing these markets, particularly concerning elections, and there are ongoing concerns about insider trading, with some traders openly questioning the platforms' efforts to combat it. The potential for market manipulation, especially in political events, and the gamification of trading are central to the ongoing debate surrounding prediction markets.
