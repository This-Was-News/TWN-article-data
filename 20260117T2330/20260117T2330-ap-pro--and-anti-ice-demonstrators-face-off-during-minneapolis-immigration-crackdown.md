## Article summary
Protesters advocating for and against the Trump administration's immigration crackdown clashed in Minneapolis on Saturday. The governor's office announced the mobilization of National Guard troops to support state law enforcement, though they were not yet deployed. Daily protests have occurred since the Department of Homeland Security increased immigration enforcement in the Twin Cities, bringing in over 2,000 federal officers. A large group of pro-immigration protesters confronted a smaller pro-ICE rally, forcing the latter to disperse. Jake Lang, who organized the pro-ICE demonstration and intended to burn a Quran, sustained injuries. Lang, previously pardoned for Jan. 6-related charges, is now running for U.S. Senate in Florida. Amidst the protests, snowballs and water balloons were thrown before police intervened. A federal judge ruled that immigration officers cannot detain or tear gas peaceful protesters. A Liberian man, Garrison Gibson, who fled civil war as a child, expressed fear of leaving his home after being released from immigration detention following his arrest during the crackdown. His arrest and subsequent release were contentious, with allegations of White House involvement denied by the administration. DHS stated its commitment to deporting undocumented immigrants, while Gibson asserted his compliance with legal requirements.
