## Article summary
President Donald Trump announced that eight European nations will face a 10% import tariff starting in February due to their opposition to the United States controlling Greenland. The countries targeted are Denmark, Norway, Sweden, France, Germany, the United Kingdom, the Netherlands, and Finland. Trump stated that this tariff would escalate to 25% if a deal for the complete purchase of Greenland is not reached by June 1. He indicated that these tariffs are intended to pressure Denmark and other European nations into negotiations regarding Greenland's status, which he considers vital for U.S. national security. This move could strain relations within NATO, an alliance that has historically provided collective security for Europe and North America. European leaders, including the President of the European Commission and the head of the European Council, expressed concern that the tariffs would damage transatlantic relations and could lead to a dangerous downward spiral. There are also legal questions surrounding the implementation of such tariffs, particularly given the European Union's status as a single economic zone. Protests against Trump's policy occurred in Greenland's capital, Nuuk, and in Copenhagen, Denmark, with participants emphasizing that countries are not for sale. Despite Trump's claims of Russian and Chinese naval presence, Danish military officials have not observed such activity. The U.S. already has access to Greenland through a 1951 defense agreement, with a reduced military presence compared to previous decades.
