## Article summary
Black women are facing a disproportionate decline in career opportunities, with a significant rise in their unemployment rate compared to other demographic groups. This trend is particularly affecting college-educated Black women. Factors contributing to this downturn include a general slowdown in the job market, the impact of artificial intelligence, and a rollback of diversity and inclusion initiatives following the Supreme Court's ruling against affirmative action and the return of Donald Trump to the presidency. Many Black women professionals, who had seen increased recognition and opportunities after 2020, are now experiencing job losses and a chilling effect on their careers. In response, Black women are forming supportive communities, such as the "Black Women Rising" WhatsApp and Discord groups, to share job leads, offer emotional support, and strategize. Some are pivoting their careers, starting their own businesses, or altering their résumés to de-emphasize diversity and inclusion work. The emotional toll is significant, with many expressing anxiety and a sense of lost progress. The current environment is forcing many to re-evaluate their career paths and confront the challenges of a less inclusive professional landscape.
