## Article summary
Robert F. Kennedy Jr., as health secretary, announced plans in March 2025 to establish the Administration for a Healthy America (AHA) to address chronic disease and addiction by restructuring the Department of Health and Human Services (HHS). However, as of January 2026, the AHA does not yet exist, and there is little public information regarding its formation or inclusion of staff and programs. The process has been described as chaotic, with significant staff reductions and secretive planning meetings occurring within HHS. Unlike the creation of previous agencies, there has been no engagement with Congress for funding or authorization, leading former officials to express bafflement at the lack of a clear process. Despite promises to preserve core functions, many offices with specific congressional authorization have been eliminated. Some speculate that Kennedy may attempt to rebrand existing initiatives as AHA successes to appear to have met his goals without congressional support. The actual creation of a new agency would require congressional funding and authorization, which are currently absent.
