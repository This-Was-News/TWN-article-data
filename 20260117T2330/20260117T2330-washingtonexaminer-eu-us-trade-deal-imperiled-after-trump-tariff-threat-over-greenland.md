## Article summary
The trade deal between the United States and the European Union, finalized last summer, is now in jeopardy due to President Donald Trump's imposition of new tariffs. Trump has threatened additional 10% levies, escalating to 25% in June, on Denmark, Norway, Sweden, France, Germany, the United Kingdom, the Netherlands, and Finland. These tariffs are in response to the European nations' increased military presence in Greenland and their support for the island's autonomy, as Trump seeks to acquire Greenland from Denmark for national security reasons. A key European Parliament leader, Manfred Weber, has stated that the approval of the EU-U.S. trade deal is now impossible given Trump's threats. The trade agreement had already partially taken effect, with the EU facing a 15% tariff on most exports, while negotiations on steel, aluminum, and copper duties were ongoing. These negotiations are now likely to collapse as Europe stands firm with Greenland and its ties to Denmark. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen warned that further tariffs would damage transatlantic relations and risk a downward spiral. French President Emmanuel Macron also asserted that Europe would respond unitedly and that European sovereignty would be upheld. EU ambassadors are scheduled to hold an emergency meeting to discuss Trump's tariff threat.
