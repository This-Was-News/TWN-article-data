## Article summary
White House press secretary Karoline Leavitt warned CBS anchor Tony Dokoupil that President Trump would "sue your ass off" if his 13-minute interview was not aired in full. While some CBS personnel perceived the threat as jest, CBS News stated it had always planned to air the interview unedited and in its entirety. This incident highlights a pattern of President Trump pursuing legal or regulatory action against media outlets he deems displeasing, including past lawsuits and settlements with news organizations. The White House has also taken steps to control information flow, such as searching a reporter's home and barring certain journalists. Powerful media companies are navigating a delicate balance, with past settlements like ABC's $16 million payment to Trump over remarks by anchor George Stephanopoulos setting a precedent. The decision by CBS's owner, Paramount, to settle a previous lawsuit with Trump for $16 million has influenced CBS News's editorial decisions. The subsequent sale of Paramount to Skydance, run by David Ellison, and the hiring of Bari Weiss as CBS News's editor in chief have raised questions about potential pro-Trump bias. Critics view Weiss's editorial judgment skeptically, especially after she postponed a segment about the Trump administration, which some saw as censorship. The power of Leavitt's threat underscores how a president's willingness to sue and a news outlet's willingness to settle can fundamentally alter journalistic practices. The full interview, when aired, included discussions on various topics and concluded with Trump making remarks about the anchor's job security.
