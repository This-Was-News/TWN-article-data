## Article summary
Rep. Mary Miller (R-IL) has introduced the American Family Housing Act, a bill aimed at restricting large corporations, companies, and investors from purchasing single-family homes. The legislation seeks to amend the Investment Company Act of 1940 to prohibit these entities from buying single-family residences, thereby preventing them from pricing out ordinary working-class families. Miller's objective is to counter Wall Street firms that are driving up housing prices and excluding American families from the market. This initiative follows President Donald Trump's executive order, 'Stopping Wall Street From Competing With Main Street Homebuyers,' which also prioritized housing affordability for Americans over investment firms. Trump's directive highlighted that neighborhoods once owned by middle-class families are now controlled by corporate interests. Data from the National Association of Realtors indicates that the average first-time homebuyer is now 40 years old, and first-time buyers constitute only 21% of the market. A 2024 Government Accountability Office study estimated that institutional investors collectively own approximately 300,000 homes nationwide, with significant concentration in metropolitan areas. Miller's bill would require the Securities and Exchange Commission to monitor and enforce these restrictions, applying only to future purchases by corporations with over $100 billion in assets under management. The bill is designed to take effect 100 days after approval to avoid market destabilization.
