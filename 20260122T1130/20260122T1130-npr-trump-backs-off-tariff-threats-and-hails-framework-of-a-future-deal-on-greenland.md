## Article summary
President Trump announced at the World Economic Forum in Davos that he has formed the "framework of a future deal" regarding Greenland and the Arctic, leading him to back off threatened tariffs on regional countries. He explicitly stated he "won't use force" to acquire Greenland, despite earlier rhetoric suggesting it. Trump also criticized European values, suggesting they were inferior to American ones and that many European nations were unrecognizable. He reiterated his belief that U.S. ownership of Greenland is vital for national security, referring to it dismissively as a "piece of ice." The negotiations for Greenland will be led by Vice President JD Vance and other officials. Trump's approach to Greenland had previously caused antagonism with allies, with Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney criticizing his policies for disrupting the international order. Trump responded to Carney, suggesting Canada should be more grateful for U.S. assistance. Beyond Greenland, Trump discussed his "Board of Peace" initiative aimed at ending the Israel-Hamas conflict, which has faced criticism for its structure and membership requirements, including an invitation to Russian President Vladimir Putin. He also addressed economic issues, calling for legislation to lower housing costs, cap credit card interest rates, and regulate the crypto market, while highlighting efforts to reduce prescription drug prices.
