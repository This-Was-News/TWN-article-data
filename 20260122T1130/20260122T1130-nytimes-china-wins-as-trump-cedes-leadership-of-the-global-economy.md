## Article summary
In a speech at the World Economic Forum in Davos, President Trump declared an end to American leadership in the global economy, asserting that the United States would no longer provide markets and military protection to allies perceived as "freeloaders." He characterized tariffs as the cost of entry for American consumers and vowed to continue his trade war. Trump's remarks were seen as a significant departure from the post-World War II liberal democratic order established by the U.S. and its allies. This stance contrasted sharply with Chinese President Xi Jinping's 2017 speech at the same venue, where Xi positioned himself as a proponent of economic globalization and international cooperation. Trump's address, described as bombastic and aggrieved, also included a temporary reversal on using tariffs to acquire Greenland. The overall message signaled a shift in American global economic policy, potentially ceding leadership to China.
