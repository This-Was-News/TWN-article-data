## Article summary
President Donald Trump launched his Board of Peace at the World Economic Forum in Davos, Switzerland, aiming to address global conflicts, though details about its mandate and operations remain scarce. The initiative, initially conceived for the Gaza ceasefire, has expanded in scope, with 35 countries agreeing to join and 60 invited. Some traditional U.S. allies have expressed skepticism, concerned the board might supersede the United Nations, a body Trump has criticized. The U.S. Secretary of State, Marco Rubio, praised the board as action-oriented and emphasized its initial focus on the Gaza peace deal. Meanwhile, Europe expressed relief as Trump reversed his threat to impose tariffs on European nations over Greenland. German Chancellor Friedrich Merz supported talks regarding Greenland, affirming Danish sovereignty. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy also arrived in Davos to discuss recovery efforts. Tech billionaire Elon Musk is set to speak at the forum, amidst a public dispute with Ryanair's CEO. The Board of Peace logo, however, primarily features North and South America, aligning with Trump's "Donroe Doctrine" focus on the Western Hemisphere. Russia's President Vladimir Putin is scheduled to meet with Trump's envoys, Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, to discuss potential peace in Ukraine.
