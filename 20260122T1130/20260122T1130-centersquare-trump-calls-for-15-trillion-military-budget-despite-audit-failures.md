## Article summary
President Donald Trump has proposed a $1.5 trillion budget for the Department of War, aiming to build what he calls the 'Dream Military' to ensure national safety and security. Trump suggested that tariff revenue, along with tariff rebate checks and funds to reduce federal debt, could finance this significant increase in defense spending. An analysis by the Committee for a Responsible Federal Budget estimated this plan would boost defense spending by $5 trillion over the next decade, potentially reaching $5.8 trillion with interest, and concluded that tariff revenue would not be sufficient to cover the costs. Senate and House Armed Services Committee leaders, Sen. Roger Wicker and Rep. Mike Rogers, endorsed the plan, citing intensifying global threats from China, Russia, Iran, and narco-terrorists, and emphasizing the need to rebuild American military leadership. They believe the investment will lead to accelerated production of ships and aircraft, a modernized arsenal, and advanced technologies. Secretary of War Pete Hegseth stated the budget would send a strong message globally. However, some Democrats, including Reps. Ilhan Omar and Mark Pocan, criticized the proposal, highlighting the Pentagon's ongoing failure to pass an annual audit and its inability to accurately account for its spending. They pointed out that the Department of War failed its eighth consecutive audit in December 2025 and questioned the justification for increasing the budget without accountability and transparency, noting that no other federal agency would be permitted to operate in such a manner. The Department of War had previously informed Congress of its intention to achieve full accounting of spending and assets by 2028, a target later revised to 2027.
