## Article summary
The Justice Department has stated that there is currently no basis for a criminal civil rights investigation into the fatal shooting of Renee Good by a U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement officer in Minneapolis. This decision marks a departure from previous administrations, which often quickly probed such incidents. While the FBI is conducting its own investigation, the Civil Rights Division will not be involved at this time. Federal officials have indicated the ICE officer acted in self-defense, and the woman was engaged in domestic terrorism. However, this quick pronouncement before a thorough investigation has raised concerns. Minnesota officials are also alarmed, as federal authorities have reportedly blocked state investigators from accessing evidence and asserted Minnesota lacks jurisdiction. The situation has led to the resignation of several federal prosecutors in Minnesota and supervisors within the Civil Rights Division in Washington. Minnesota lawmakers have criticized these departures, citing concerns about political pressure on Justice Department officials. Historically, the Civil Rights Division has played a significant role in investigating law enforcement shootings, aiming to ensure thorough reviews and soothe community tensions, as seen in the George Floyd case.
