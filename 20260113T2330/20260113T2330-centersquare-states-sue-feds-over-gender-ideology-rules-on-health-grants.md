## Article summary
Twelve states, led by New York, California, and Oregon, are suing the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services over new grant conditions. The lawsuit, filed in the U.S. District Court for Rhode Island, challenges policies that the states' Democratic attorneys general claim are discriminatory. Oregon Attorney General Dan Rayfield stated that the new policy demands recipients of health, education, and research funding certify compliance with an executive order that allegedly denies the existence of transgender people and imposes rigid definitions of sex. This policy, according to Rayfield, uses federal money to interfere with personal medical decisions. The other states involved in the lawsuit are Colorado, Delaware, Illinois, Michigan, Minnesota, Nevada, Rhode Island, Vermont, and Washington. The plaintiffs argue that the new policy forces states to discriminate against transgender individuals or forfeit vital HHS grants. California Attorney General Rob Bonta described the changes as an effort to target transgender, nonbinary, intersex, and gender nonconforming individuals, asserting that HHS has overstepped its constitutional authority. The lawsuit contends that the Trump administration cannot change laws via executive order and is attempting to rewrite Title IX, a law prohibiting sex-based discrimination in federally funded educational programs. New York Attorney General Letitia James characterized the administration's directive as cruel and unjust, forcing states to choose between their values and essential funding.
