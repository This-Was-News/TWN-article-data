## Article summary
The U.S. Supreme Court heard arguments in two cases challenging state laws that ban transgender people from participating in women's sports. Advocates on both sides of the issue urged lawmakers to take action. Supporters of the bans, like Carrie and Taylor Peters, emphasized the need to protect spaces for cisgender women and argued that biological differences give transgender women an unfair advantage. They believe this is a matter of biology, not ideology, and that Title IX should be used to protect women's sports. Conversely, advocates for transgender athletes, such as Lambda Legal fellow Pelecanos, argued that transgender athletes like Becky Pepper-Jackson do not have significant competitive advantages and that Title IX is meant to provide equal access to educational opportunities for all students, including transgender individuals. Evelyn Ullman, representing Democrats for an Informed Approach to Gender, stated that while she supports Democrat causes, transgender inclusion in sports undermines the definition of woman and the women's rights movement. She also noted that support for transgender inclusion in sports is a vocal minority within the Democratic party. The debate centers on the interpretation of Title IX and the definition of womanhood in the context of sports.
