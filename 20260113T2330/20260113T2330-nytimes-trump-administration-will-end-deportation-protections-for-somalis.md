## Article summary
The Trump administration is ending deportation protections for over 2,000 Somali nationals, a program known as Temporary Protected Status (TPS), which is set to expire on March 17. This decision comes amid an intensified immigration enforcement operation in Minnesota, home to the largest Somali population in the U.S. Homeland security officials stated that conditions in Somalia have improved, making the continued stay of Somalis in the U.S. contrary to national interests. TPS was initially granted in the early 1990s due to Somalia's civil war and has been extended multiple times. Legislators from the Minneapolis region criticized the move, arguing it puts legal residents at risk and disrupts families and communities. The Trump administration has previously sought to end TPS for hundreds of thousands of migrants from various countries. U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement urged Somalis to leave the country voluntarily before March 17. President Trump had previously expressed a desire to end these protections for Somalis. The administration's focus on Minnesota is partly due to a fraud scheme involving individuals of Somali descent. While many Somalis in Minnesota are U.S. citizens or legal residents, federal agencies are also reviewing refugee cases and investigating fraud in the state.
