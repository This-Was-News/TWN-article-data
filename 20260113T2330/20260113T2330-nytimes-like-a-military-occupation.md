## Article summary
Videos depict an escalating federal crackdown in Minneapolis, characterized by aggressive tactics and confrontations between heavily armed federal agents and residents. These incidents, occurring in various public spaces, have heightened tensions following a recent killing. State Representative Michael Howard expressed that the community feels under siege by the federal government, noting that U.S. citizens were detained and later released. The Department of Homeland Security stated that arrests were made in connection with assaulting federal officers, though charges were unclear. Federal officers claim their presence is to apprehend undocumented immigrants with criminal backgrounds, especially in areas where local governments do not cooperate. However, residents and business owners condemn these tactics, alleging racial and ethnic profiling and indiscriminate sweeps. The situation evokes memories of the 2020 George Floyd protests, with current fears focused on federal government overreach. The federal actions have also led to the resignation of six federal prosecutors in Minnesota. Videos show agents demanding immigration status, breaking car windows, and confronting bystanders. Minneapolis City Council President Elliott Payne described patrols as a "military occupation," with agents using obscenities and displaying weapons. President Trump stated the agents are removing dangerous criminals, while also warning of a "day of reckoning and retribution" for Minnesota. Federal agents are reportedly questioning people about their citizenship without warrants, which critics argue does not enhance safety. Community resistance, including documenting agent activity, has emerged as empowering for residents. However, concerns remain about potential escalation and accidental shootings. A lawsuit has been filed by the state and cities to block the federal surge, alleging "militarized raids and dangerous, illegal and unconstitutional stops and arrests."
