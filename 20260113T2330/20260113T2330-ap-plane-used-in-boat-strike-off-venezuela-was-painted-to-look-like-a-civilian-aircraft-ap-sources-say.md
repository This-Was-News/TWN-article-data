## Article summary
The U.S. military used a plane painted to resemble a civilian aircraft during a strike on a suspected drug-smuggling boat off the coast of Venezuela. This action reportedly violates the Pentagon's manual on the laws of war, which prohibits feigning civilian status for attack, a practice known as perfidy. The plane also carried munitions internally, deviating from standard practice. These details emerged as the U.S. Senate prepares to vote on a war powers resolution to limit further military action in Venezuela without congressional authorization. President Trump is actively trying to dissuade Republican senators from supporting this resolution. The administration has justified past boat strikes by asserting an armed conflict with drug cartels. However, military guidelines explicitly warn against disguising oneself as a civilian during combat. The strike in question occurred on September 2nd, marking the beginning of a series of U.S. military actions against suspected drug boats. Concerns have also been raised about a subsequent strike that killed survivors of the initial boat attack, which legal experts suggest may have violated laws of war. Meanwhile, senators have reviewed the White House's legal justification for using military force to oust Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro, who has since been captured and faces charges in the U.S. Some lawmakers have expressed doubts about the legality and long-term implications of this operation and the U.S. intention to manage Venezuela.
