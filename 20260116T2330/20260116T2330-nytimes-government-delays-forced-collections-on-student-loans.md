## Article summary
The Trump administration has announced a temporary delay in forced collections from student loan defaulters, including the seizure of tax refunds and wage garnishment. This pause is intended to allow more time for the overhaul of the student loan repayment system. Meanwhile, U.S. lawmakers visited Denmark to discuss Greenland, with President Trump suggesting tariffs to pressure countries regarding its annexation. In Latin America, the FAA issued advisories for increased military activity in the Pacific Ocean near several countries. A student deported to Honduras is hoping to return to the U.S. after a prosecutor admitted her deportation was a mistake. President Trump appointed allies to a commission reviewing his White House ballroom plans. The administration is also working with states to prevent the AI boom from increasing electricity costs. Mexico's president defended her nation's anti-drug efforts amidst U.S. criticism and the looming threat of U.S. military action. President Trump pardoned a convicted fraudster for the second time, along with other individuals with ties to his orbit. Trump softened his threat to invoke the Insurrection Act for Minneapolis, while Democrats criticized federal overreach in immigration enforcement. Venezuela's opposition leader, María Corina Machado, met with President Trump and expressed faith in his support for democracy in Venezuela. New polling indicates Trump's base remains largely supportive, though discontent exists regarding his handling of the economy. A Trump store in suburban Philadelphia is closing due to faltering sales. The CIA director met with Venezuela's interim president, reinforcing the U.S. message of stability. Canada is lowering tariffs on Chinese electric vehicles and China will do the same for Canadian canola products, signaling a shift in policy and a break with the U.S.
