## Article summary
President Donald Trump has indicated he may impose tariffs on countries that do not support the U.S. acquiring control of Greenland. This statement was made as a bipartisan U.S. congressional delegation met with Danish and Greenlandic lawmakers in Copenhagen to de-escalate tensions surrounding Trump's desire for Greenland. Trump has repeatedly asserted the U.S. should control Greenland, citing national security and concerns about Chinese and Russian interests, and has not ruled out using force. European leaders, however, maintain that decisions about Greenland are solely for Denmark and Greenland to make. During meetings in Copenhagen, U.S. senators emphasized the strong, long-standing alliance between the U.S. and Denmark, with Senator Lisa Murkowski stating Greenland should be viewed as an ally, not an asset. Greenlandic politician Aaja Chemnitz criticized the U.S. administration's claims as exaggerated threats. Greenland's prime minister has stated a preference for Denmark and NATO if forced to choose. The chair of the Inuit Circumpolar Council expressed concern that the White House's persistent statements reflect a dismissive view of Indigenous peoples and smaller nations, highlighting that Inuit in Greenland do not wish to be recolonized.
