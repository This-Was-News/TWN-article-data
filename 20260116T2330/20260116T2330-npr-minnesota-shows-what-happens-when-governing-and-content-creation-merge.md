## Article summary
The Trump administration has increasingly integrated social media content into its governing strategies, using viral videos, memes, and influencer content to shape narratives and justify policies, particularly concerning immigration. This approach was evident in Minnesota, where a controversial ICE operation and a fatal shooting were amplified and framed through social media by the administration and its allies. The White House has also employed social media to promote actions like the raid in Venezuela and has shared graphics with QAnon slogans and white supremacist-linked songs. This strategy is characterized by high volume, real-time agenda-setting, and provocative messaging designed to provoke reactions and target specific audiences. Experts describe this as a shift from policy-driven governance to narrative-driven politics, where online content is used to energize supporters and demonize opponents. The administration views influencers and partisan journalists as key audiences, granting them special access and briefings. While this "always-online" focus has been central since the beginning of Trump's second term, it has also led to controversies and, in some cases, backfired, straining his coalition. Critics argue this fusion of posting and policy, termed a "clicktatorship," leads to more extreme behavior and a coarsening of political discourse, potentially alienating the public. However, the administration maintains its communication style is authentic and led by President Trump, the "greatest communicator." The extensive use of social media, while effective in mobilizing a base, may also be what ultimately exposes and challenges the administration's actions as more Americans witness and document events through their own devices.
