## Article summary
White House National Economic Council Director Kevin Hassett revealed that the Trump administration is in discussions with major banks regarding the voluntary issuance of "Trump cards." These cards would feature an interest rate cap of 10%, a proposal stemming from President Donald Trump's recent call for such a cap on credit card interest rates to address affordability concerns. Hassett explained that instead of legislative action, the administration is exploring this voluntary approach with banks. The "Trump cards" would be targeted at individuals with stable incomes who are currently underserved by traditional credit markets. This initiative is part of a broader affordability agenda the president plans to promote at the World Economic Forum. The agenda also includes proposals for savers to use retirement funds for home down payments and Trump's "Great Healthcare Plan" aimed at significantly reducing prescription drug prices. Additionally, discussions are ongoing about a tariff rebate check for low- and middle-income households. While some lawmakers have proposed mandatory interest rate caps, industry groups have warned against such measures, citing potential negative impacts on affordability.
