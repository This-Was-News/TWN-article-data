## Article summary
The U.S. Education Department's Office for Civil Rights, once a strong enforcer of sexual violence cases in schools, has seen its investigations dwindle significantly. This decline is attributed to mass layoffs under the Trump administration, which left the office with fewer lawyers and a backlog of over 25,000 cases. Before the layoffs, the office opened dozens of sexual violence investigations annually; since then, fewer than 10 nationwide have been initiated. Concurrently, Trump officials have increased investigations into schools accommodating transgender students, opening nearly 50 such cases. Critics argue the office is understaffed and slow, leading some firms to stop filing complaints altogether, viewing it as a "dead end." An Education Department spokesperson stated they are working through the caseload and blamed the Biden administration for rewriting Title IX rules. The reduction in investigations leaves students with few options, primarily lawsuits or walking away from seeking justice. Historically, the federal office has held schools accountable for mishandling sexual assault cases, but current staff report that many past cases are now idle. While dozens of laid-off workers are being brought back, the department intends to pursue further layoffs. The decrease in the Office for Civil Rights' capacity is seen by some as a rollback of progress for survivors of sexual assault.
