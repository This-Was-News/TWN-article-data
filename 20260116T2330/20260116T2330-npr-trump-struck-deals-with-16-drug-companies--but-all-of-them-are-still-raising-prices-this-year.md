## Article summary
Despite striking deals with the Trump administration to lower prices, all 16 major drug companies that inked agreements have still raised prices for some of their drugs in 2026. These price hikes, occurring in January, a typical time for such increases, affected 872 brand-name drugs. The median price increase was 4%, mirroring the previous year. While the administration claimed these deals would benefit American consumers and push other countries to pay more, drug price researchers note that market behavior remains consistent with previous years. The details of these deals, nicknamed "most favored nation" agreements, remain largely undisclosed, but they reportedly include commitments for Medicaid discounts and future drug pricing strategies. Companies like Pfizer and Merck have defended their price increases, citing investments in new medicines and modest adjustments. However, the effectiveness of these deals in impacting the prices paid by most Americans is questioned by health policy researchers. In contrast, some drugs did see price reductions, with 18 significant cuts reported. These include insulin products and medications for diabetes and heart failure, with some price drops attributed to Medicare drug price negotiations initiated by the Biden administration. The overall impact of these various policies on drug pricing presents a complex and mixed picture.
