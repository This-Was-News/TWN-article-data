## Article summary
President Trump has issued a series of pardons and commutations, including freeing convicted fraudster Adriana Camberos for a second time. Camberos was previously released by Trump in 2021 but was convicted again in 2024 for a new fraud scheme. The pardons also extended to former Puerto Rican Governor Wanda Vázquez and a former F.B.I. agent, who had pleaded guilty in a political corruption case. These clemency grants, many of which were not previously reported, were supported by individuals with close ties to Trump's orbit. This pattern suggests Trump has used his clemency power to reward allies and donors, contrasting with Justice Department guidelines. The pardons, some for white-collar crimes by affluent individuals, occurred while the administration announced efforts to root out fraud in programs for the poor. A White House official defended the grants as rectifying excessive or politically motivated prosecutions against Trump supporters, asserting decisions were merit-based. The case involving Vázquez and banker Julio Herrera Velutini highlights large donations to a Trump-backed super PAC by Velutini's daughter shortly before a lenient plea deal was negotiated. Camberos's supporters and the White House official claimed she was unfairly targeted. Trump also pardoned businessman Terren S. Peizer, convicted of insider trading, and commuted the sentence of Jacob Deutsch, involved in mortgage fraud. Lawyers for some recipients stated their clients were victims of government overreach or unfair processes.
