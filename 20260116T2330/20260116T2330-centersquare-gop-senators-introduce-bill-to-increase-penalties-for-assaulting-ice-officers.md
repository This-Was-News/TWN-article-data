## Article summary
Republican U.S. senators, led by Sen. John Cornyn, have introduced the ICE Protection Act to increase penalties for assaulting U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) officers. This bill comes in response to an unprecedented rise in assaults against ICE officers during their duties. The proposed legislation aims to double the statutory maximum prison sentence for assaulting, resisting arrest, or impeding law enforcement while using a deadly weapon, from 20 to 40 years. It also introduces mandatory minimum sentences for vehicular attacks on officers, ranging from five to 10 years based on the severity of injury. These increased penalties are intended to send a clear message that attacks on law enforcement will not be tolerated, with Cornyn attributing the surge in violence to inflammatory rhetoric from the Left. The bill was introduced following an incident where a Cuban national rammed his vehicle into ICE vehicles, injuring an officer. Data indicates a significant increase in assaults, vehicular attacks, and death threats against ICE officers over the past year. Texas Governor Greg Abbott supports stricter enforcement and has bolstered resources for ICE officers. This legislative effort underscores a growing concern over the safety of federal agents performing immigration enforcement.
