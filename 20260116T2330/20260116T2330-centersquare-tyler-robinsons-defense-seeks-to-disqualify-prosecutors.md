## Article summary
Attorneys for Tyler James Robinson, who is accused of murdering conservative leader Charlie Kirk, are seeking to disqualify the prosecution team due to a conflict of interest. The defense argues that one of the prosecutors has an adult child who was in close proximity to Kirk when he was fatally shot at a rally in Orem, Utah. Robinson's lawyer, Richard Novak, requested that the Utah Attorney General's Office represent the prosecution in this matter, citing legal precedents. Utah County Attorney Jeff Gray dismissed this as a delaying tactic. The defense claims the prosecutor's child was 85 feet from Kirk and that the County Attorney's Office did not properly screen the prosecutor. The names of the prosecutor and their child have not been publicly disclosed. Judge Tony Graf expressed concern about closing the entire hearing on the conflict of interest, stating that the prosecutor's name is a relevant fact that cannot be omitted. The defense plans to call the County Attorney, the prosecutor in question, and their child as witnesses. Robinson faces charges including aggravated murder, witness tampering, and obstruction of justice, with the prosecution intending to seek the death penalty if he is convicted.
