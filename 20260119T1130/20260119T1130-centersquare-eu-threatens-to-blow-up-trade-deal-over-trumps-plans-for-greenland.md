## Article summary
A top European official has stated that President Donald Trump's comments regarding Greenland could jeopardize a trade deal between the U.S. and the European Union. Manfred Weber, president of the European People's Party, indicated that his party would not support a deal under these circumstances and called for a halt to proposed 0% tariffs on U.S. products. This statement follows Trump's announcement of potential tariffs on European countries if a deal to annex Greenland is not reached. European Union Commission President Ursula von der Leyen expressed solidarity with Denmark and Greenland, warning that tariffs could worsen transatlantic relations and lead to a "dangerous downward spiral." Trump intends to impose tariffs starting at 10% on goods from Denmark, Norway, Sweden, France, Germany, the United Kingdom, the Netherlands, and Finland, increasing to 25%, until a deal for Greenland is finalized. He cited historical attempts to acquire the island and the strategic importance of "The Golden Dome, and Modern Day Weapons Systems" as reasons for the current push. European allies have reportedly sent a symbolic number of troops to Greenland amid these escalating discussions. Trump had previously announced a trade deal with the EU in July that would impose a 15% tariff on imports from the bloc.
