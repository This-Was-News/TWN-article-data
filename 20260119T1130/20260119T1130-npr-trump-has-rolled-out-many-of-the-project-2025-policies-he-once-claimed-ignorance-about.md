## Article summary
Donald Trump, who initially claimed ignorance about Project 2025, has since implemented many of its policies. This right-wing policy plan, developed by the Heritage Foundation, aims to remake the government. Concerns about Project 2025 gained public attention during the 2024 presidential campaign. Despite dismissing the plan as "ridiculous" and distancing himself from its creators, Trump appointed key figures involved in Project 2025 to significant positions after his election. His administration has since enacted policies such as ending diversity, equity, and inclusion programs, strengthening immigration enforcement, and reshaping the federal workforce. California's Attorney General, Rob Bonta, stated that Project 2025 effectively outlined the administration's agenda. Bonta and other Democratic attorneys general have used the plan to challenge Trump's policies in court, successfully blocking some initiatives. The White House, however, dismisses these concerns, asserting that Trump is implementing the agenda voters supported. Presidential historian Tevi Troy suggests that Trump's initial rejection of Project 2025 may have inadvertently amplified its controversial nature, contrasting it with typical policy proposals from think tanks. Trump later acknowledged Project 2025, even boasting about meeting with its architect, Russell Vought, while threatening to dismantle federal agencies.
