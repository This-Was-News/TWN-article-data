## Article summary
European Union officials are leaning towards negotiation rather than retaliation in response to President Trump's threat of tariffs over his desire to purchase Greenland. Trump had demanded a deal to buy Greenland, warning that failure to comply would result in a 10 percent tariff in February, escalating to 25 percent in June. Ambassadors from the 27-nation bloc met in Brussels to discuss the situation, emphasizing a preference for negotiation while remaining committed to protecting Greenland's autonomy. The Trump administration, through Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, indicated no intention to back down, asserting that American control of Greenland would be beneficial for all parties involved. Trump also claimed on social media that NATO had for years urged Denmark to address a Russian threat near Greenland, suggesting it was time for action. The EU's preliminary talks highlighted their desire to avoid retaliatory measures but also their commitment to respecting the will of the people of Greenland.
