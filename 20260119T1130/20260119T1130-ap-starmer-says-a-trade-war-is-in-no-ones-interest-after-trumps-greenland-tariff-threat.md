## Article summary
British Prime Minister Keir Starmer has stated that U.S. President Donald Trump's threat of tariffs against European allies over Greenland is "completely wrong" and that a trade war serves no one's interest. Trump's proposed 10% import tax on goods from eight European nations, including the U.K., is a response to their opposition to American control of Greenland. Trump claims the U.S. needs Greenland for national security, citing potential designs by China and Russia on the island's critical mineral reserves, and has not ruled out military action. Starmer affirmed Britain's support for Greenland and Denmark's right to decide the Arctic island's future, while emphasizing the importance of the U.K.-U.S. relationship. He stressed that pragmatism does not equate to passivity and that partnership does not mean abandoning principles. Britain is not planning retaliatory tariffs and will work with allies to keep dialogue open. European Council President Antonio Costa indicated the EU leaders are ready to defend themselves against coercion. Meanwhile, Denmark and Greenland's foreign minister are scheduled to meet with NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte to discuss increased NATO activity in the Arctic.
