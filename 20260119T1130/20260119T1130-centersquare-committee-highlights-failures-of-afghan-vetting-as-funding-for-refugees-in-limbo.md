## Article summary
A Senate Judiciary Committee hearing revealed significant failures in the vetting process for Afghan parolees admitted to the U.S. under Operation Allies Welcome. Officials admitted that many of the nearly 100,000 Afghan evacuees were admitted without complete biographic data, and some were admitted before any biometrics were collected for background checks. Several agencies lost track of many evacuees after resettlement, and a concerning number, including potentially thousands with ties to terrorist organizations, remain unaccounted for. Senator John Cornyn highlighted that 18,000 individuals were known or suspected terrorists, with 2,000 actively being investigated. Senator Josh Hawley raised concerns about tens of millions of dollars potentially going to pro-terrorist organizations that were supposed to assist in the refugee process. This comes as Congress is set to vote on nearly $6 billion in continued funding for refugee programs, which saw a substantial increase under the Biden administration, rising from less than $2 billion in FY2021 to nearly $9 billion in FY2022. Despite the vetting issues, many refugees continue to qualify for numerous taxpayer-funded benefits. Afghan refugees, in particular, face challenges with lower English proficiency, educational attainment, and labor force participation, leading to higher poverty rates and continued reliance on government assistance. Total federal spending on refugee and migrant assistance programs has reached approximately $30 billion over the last four years.
