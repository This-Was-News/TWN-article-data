## Article summary
A high-speed train collision in southern Spain on Sunday has resulted in at least 39 fatalities, with rescue efforts ongoing. The incident occurred near Cordoba when the tail end of a train traveling from Malaga to Madrid derailed and struck a train en route from Madrid to Huelva. Approximately 300 passengers were on the first train, and 75 were hospitalized. Spanish authorities are investigating the cause of the "strange" accident, which happened on a recently renovated, flat stretch of track. The train that derailed belonged to a private company, Iryo, while the other was operated by the public company Renfe. The collision caused the first two carriages of the Renfe train to fall down a 4-meter slope, sustaining significant damage. Spain boasts the largest high-speed rail network in Europe, a popular and generally safe mode of transport. Train services between Madrid and Andalusia were suspended on Monday. The country's worst train accident this century occurred in 2013, claiming 80 lives due to excessive speed.
