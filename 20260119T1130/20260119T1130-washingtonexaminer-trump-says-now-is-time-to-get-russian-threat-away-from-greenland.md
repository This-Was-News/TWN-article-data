## Article summary
President Donald Trump stated on social media that it is time to address the Russian threat in Greenland, asserting that Denmark has been unable to resolve the issue despite NATO's warnings for two decades. This statement comes amidst Trump's ongoing efforts to acquire Greenland from Denmark, a proposal met with firm rejection by Danish officials. Trump has previously offered to purchase the Arctic island and even suggested military intervention, causing stress within NATO, of which both countries are members. Danish Foreign Affairs Minister Lars Løkke Rasmussen acknowledged heightened security concerns in the Arctic but denied China posed an imminent threat, emphasizing significant Danish investment in Greenland's capabilities. Despite acknowledging shared concerns about the Arctic's changing security landscape, Rasmussen reiterated that the U.S. acquiring Greenland is unnecessary. In a related development, Trump announced a 10% tariff on several European nations, including Denmark, until an agreement for the purchase of Greenland is reached. Danish diplomats met with U.S. officials to discuss the situation, but no resolution was found, though discussions are set to continue.
