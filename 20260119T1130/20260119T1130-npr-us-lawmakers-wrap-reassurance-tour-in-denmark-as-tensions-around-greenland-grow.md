## Article summary
A bipartisan U.S. congressional delegation concluded a visit to Denmark aimed at de-escalating tensions surrounding President Trump's desire to acquire Greenland. Trump intensified his efforts by announcing tariffs on Denmark and other European nations opposing his plan. Lawmakers, including Senators Lisa Murkowski and Chris Coons, met with Danish and Greenlandic officials, emphasizing the importance of dialogue and reassuring allies of U.S. support. Danish and Greenlandic politicians expressed concerns that Trump's actions threaten the NATO alliance and undermine deep-seated relationships. Senator Thom Tillis criticized the administration's approach, calling coercive actions against an ally "beyond stupid." Greenlandic politicians highlighted the existential nature of the threat and the need for face-to-face diplomacy. The delegation also acknowledged Denmark's historical sacrifices as an ally. Protests against Trump's proposal took place in Copenhagen, with demonstrators emphasizing that Greenland is not for sale. Lawmakers expressed worry that Trump's actions could embolden adversaries like Russia and China. The visit underscored the significant role lawmakers can play in international diplomacy, especially during periods of unpredictable foreign policy.
