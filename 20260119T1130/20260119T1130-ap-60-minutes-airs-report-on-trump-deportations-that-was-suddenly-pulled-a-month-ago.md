## Article summary
The news program '60 Minutes' has aired a report on Trump administration deportations that was previously removed from its schedule a month prior. This removal had sparked an internal conflict within CBS News regarding political pressure. The report, focusing on deportees sent to El Salvador's CECOT prison, was initially pulled by CBS News editor-in-chief Bari Weiss, who cited insufficient representation of the administration's viewpoint. Correspondent Sharyn Alfonsi contended that the decision was political, not editorial, and that the administration's refusal to grant on-camera interviews was a tactic to suppress the story. CBS News stated that the piece was always intended to be aired once ready, emphasizing its commitment to airing the story and its editorial independence. The initial decision to postpone the report had drawn criticism, with some suggesting it was an attempt by new corporate leadership to appease Trump. An earlier version of Alfonsi's story was mistakenly made available online, allowing viewers to compare it with the eventually aired version. The aired report included updated statements from the White House and Department of Homeland Security, as well as a brief clip of President Trump and White House press secretary Karoline Leavitt. The relationship between CBS News and the Trump administration has seen increased visibility, with officials appearing in interviews, including one with President Trump himself. This follows previous instances where Trump has objected to the editing of his interviews.
