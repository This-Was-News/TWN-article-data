## Article summary
The U.S. Supreme Court is set to hear arguments in Trump v. Cook, a case concerning President Donald Trump's authority to fire Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook. Trump accused Cook of mortgage fraud prior to her appointment, citing it as cause for removal. However, a lower court judge reinstated Cook, arguing she is entitled to notice and a hearing before termination, likening her rights to those of a civil servant. The Trump administration contested this, asserting the president only needs 'cause' for removal, not a hearing. This case could potentially overturn a nearly 90-year-old precedent that limited presidential power to remove members of federal boards. The Supreme Court has already heard arguments in a similar case, Trump v. Slaughter, challenging the removal of a Federal Trade Commission member. The outcome of these cases could redefine the president's relationship with independent agencies like the Federal Reserve, which Trump has publicly criticized. The court is expected to issue a decision by July. The Federal Reserve has recently faced scrutiny, including a Department of Justice investigation into its chair, Jerome Powell, over interest rate decisions. The Supreme Court's ruling will determine whether the order to keep Cook on the board remains in place.
