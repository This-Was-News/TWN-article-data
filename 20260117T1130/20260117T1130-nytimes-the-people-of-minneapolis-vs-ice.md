## Article summary
Federal agents are conducting immigration crackdowns in Minneapolis, leading to confrontations with local activists. These activists use their vehicles, phones, and social networks to monitor and disrupt ICE operations, aiming to document alleged abuses and waste agents' time. The city's defiance is evident in protests, school walkouts, and street-level confrontations following incidents like the shooting death of Renee Good by an ICE agent. Many white volunteers feel a particular responsibility to protect vulnerable neighbors, motivated by recent events. The Trump administration claims the crackdown is necessary to combat fraud, especially in the Somali community, but a New York Times analysis contradicts official accounts of an ICE agent's fatal shooting of Ms. Good. Activists are instructed not to obstruct arrests but to record interactions, identify ICE vehicles, and make agents aware they are being watched. These encounters can become heated, with reports of ICE agents detaining or using pepper spray on protesters, though a federal judge has issued an order limiting such actions. Despite the risks and escalating tensions, including the use of tear gas and sting ball grenades against protesters, the local monitoring and confrontation efforts continue, reflecting a deep-seated resistance to federal immigration tactics.
