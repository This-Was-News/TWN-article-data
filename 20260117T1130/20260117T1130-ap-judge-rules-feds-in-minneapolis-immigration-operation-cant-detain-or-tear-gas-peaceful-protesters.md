## Article summary
A federal judge in Minnesota has ruled that federal officers involved in an immigration operation in Minneapolis cannot detain or use tear gas on peaceful protesters. The ruling, issued by U.S. District Judge Kate Menendez, addresses a case filed by six activists who have been observing Immigration and Customs Enforcement and Border Patrol activities. The judge stated that safely following agents at an appropriate distance does not constitute reasonable suspicion for a stop. Furthermore, agents cannot arrest individuals without probable cause that a crime has been committed or that they are obstructing the officers' work. This decision comes amidst clashes between federal agents and demonstrators since the immigration crackdown began, which escalated after an agent fatally shot a woman. The Department of Homeland Security stated that its agency is taking appropriate measures to uphold the law and protect officers, citing instances of assault, vandalism, and obstruction by protesters. The judge is also presiding over a separate lawsuit filed by the state and cities of Minneapolis and St. Paul seeking to suspend the enforcement crackdown, though she declined to grant an immediate temporary restraining order in that case.
