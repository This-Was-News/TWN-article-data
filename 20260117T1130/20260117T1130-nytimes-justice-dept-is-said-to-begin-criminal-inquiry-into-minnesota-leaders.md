## Article summary
The Trump administration has initiated a criminal investigation into elected Democratic officials in Minnesota, including Governor Tim Walz and Minneapolis Mayor Jacob Frey. This action is seen as a significant escalation in the conflict between federal authorities and local leaders regarding an immigration crackdown in the city. The investigation reportedly centers on allegations that Walz and Frey conspired to obstruct federal agents sent to Minneapolis. Both officials have strongly condemned the investigation, denouncing it as a "weaponized use of law enforcement power" and an "authoritarian tactic." This development follows the fatal shooting of Renee Good, an unarmed woman, by a federal agent, which has sparked protests and criticism from local leaders. While federal officials have indicated they are unlikely to charge the agent involved, they may investigate Ms. Good's partner and potential connections to activists. The Justice Department has vowed to prosecute anyone impeding federal agents, and the new investigation aims to determine if senior Democrats obstructed law enforcement. This probe was announced shortly after a deputy attorney general posted accusations of "terrorism" against the governor and mayor on social media, though no public evidence supports these claims. The situation has also involved threats from President Trump to invoke the Insurrection Act, which he has since appeared to back away from. A federal judge has imposed restrictions on federal agents' conduct during protests, a move supported by the Minnesota attorney general. The investigation signals a hardening stance from federal authorities, with officials emphasizing their commitment to prosecuting those who obstruct or attack federal officers.
