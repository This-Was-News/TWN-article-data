## Article summary
San Francisco is set to receive $39.9 million in state funding to address homelessness and mental health issues. Governor Gavin Newsom announced the funding at an event focused on "Treatments, Not Tents," highlighting a recent 9% reduction in unsheltered homelessness statewide. This funding is part of the state's Homeless Housing, Assistance and Prevention (HHAP) program, which aims to support local efforts in ending homelessness through housing and services. The funds supplement those from Proposition 1, a voter-approved initiative for housing and mental health. San Francisco Mayor Daniel Lurie stated that the city is already shifting its approach with the "Breaking the Cycle plan," which integrates health, social services, law enforcement, and emergency responders. This plan has already led to a 40% increase in shelter placements and the opening of 600 new treatment-focused beds. Lurie also mentioned new legislation to house families living in RVs and efforts to clean public spaces. He noted a 44% decrease in encampments in December compared to the previous year. Lurie expressed gratitude for the state's financial support, emphasizing its role in funding tangible solutions for those experiencing homelessness.
