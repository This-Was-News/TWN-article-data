## Article summary
X, formerly known as Twitter, has announced that its AI chatbot, Grok, will be blocked from editing images to depict real people in revealing clothing in regions where such actions are illegal. This decision follows a significant global backlash and investigations by several governments, including California, into the proliferation of nonconsensual sexually explicit material generated by Grok. Initially, X's response to media inquiries was dismissive, stating "legacy media lies." However, xAI, Elon Musk's AI company, has now implemented technological measures to prevent Grok from facilitating the creation of such content, including images in bikinis or underwear. Image creation and editing features are now restricted to paid subscribers to ensure accountability. Countries like Malaysia and Indonesia have already taken legal action and blocked access to Grok, while others like the U.K. and the European Union are investigating potential violations of online safety laws. Technology Secretary Liz Kendall emphasized the need for social media platforms to meet legal duties and provide safe services. California Attorney General Rob Bonta also urged xAI to prevent further harassment of women and girls through Grok's editing functions, highlighting a zero-tolerance policy for AI-based creation and dissemination of nonconsensual intimate images or child sexual abuse material.
