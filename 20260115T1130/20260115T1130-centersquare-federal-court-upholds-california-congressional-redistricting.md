## Article summary
A federal court in Los Angeles has upheld California's congressional redistricting plan, dismissing a lawsuit that accused the state of racial gerrymandering. The three-judge panel ruled 2-1 in favor of Governor Gavin Newsom and the state, finding that the challengers failed to prove racial gerrymandering occurred. The lawsuit, filed by Republicans and the U.S. Department of Justice, sought to stop mid-decade redistricting, alleging violations of the Voting Rights Act. Defendants argued the redistricting was political-partisan, which is legal. The court referenced a Supreme Court ruling stating that partisan gerrymandering is a political question beyond federal court reach. The ruling affirmed the will of California voters who approved Proposition 50, the measure enabling the redistricting, with nearly 65% voting in favor. Governor Newsom and Attorney General Rob Bonta praised the decision, emphasizing that all challenges to Proposition 50 have thus far failed. However, one judge dissented, suggesting that racial gerrymandering was likely predominant in at least one district, citing the mapmaker's public statements about bolstering Latino voting power. The dissenting judge argued that the court should not solely consider voter intent but also other "more probative evidence." The case may potentially proceed to the U.S. Supreme Court.
