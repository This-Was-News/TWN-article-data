## Article summary
Following the capture of Nicolas Maduro, two Venezuelan women, Maria Corina Machado and Delcy Rodriguez, are vying for U.S. support to lead the country. President Trump initially indicated support for Vice President Delcy Rodriguez, a move reflecting a pragmatic geopolitical approach and a behind-the-scenes effort by both women to gain favor. Machado, an "Iron Lady" and idealist, has a history of opposing socialist leaders and has been a vocal proponent of U.S. intervention. She gained significant popularity, winning 90% of the vote in an opposition primary, but was banned from running. Despite endorsing a candidate who won the election, the results were overturned. Machado has faced setbacks in securing Trump's full support due to perceived missteps. Rodriguez, in contrast, comes from a Marxist background and has risen through the ranks of the Chavista government, holding various ministerial positions. She gained respect for her pragmatic economic reforms and handling of the oil industry, even making a past attempt to influence Trump's inauguration. Despite initial denouncements of U.S. actions, Rodriguez has adopted conciliatory policies towards the U.S., including an oil deal and releasing political prisoners. Trump has expressed optimism about their partnership. Machado, however, warns that Rodriguez is untrustworthy and aligned with adversarial nations. The dynamic between these two strong-willed women and their differing visions for Venezuela will likely shape the country's future, with Trump's decision proving decisive.
