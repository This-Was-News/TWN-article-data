## Article summary
President Donald Trump has ordered new mediation for a stalled contract dispute between the New York Metropolitan Transportation Authority (MTA) and union leaders representing Long Island Rail Road workers. This intervention aims to prevent a potentially disruptive strike. Trump signed an executive order establishing a second "emergency" board to assess the good-faith negotiations between the MTA and five labor unions. This board, appointed by the president, will investigate the dispute and has the power to recommend a resolution. The White House's involvement, requested by labor leaders, grants both sides an additional 120 days to reach an agreement before any strike action can occur. A previous Presidential Emergency Board had proposed a 14% raise over four years, which the MTA rejected, offering instead a 9.5% raise over three years. Union officials accuse the employer of delay and obstruction, while Governor Kathy Hochul has previously criticized the unions for not negotiating in good faith and rejected binding arbitration. The new panel will have 60 days to make non-binding recommendations, followed by a 60-day cooling-off period. If no agreement is reached after 120 days, a strike or lockout could commence. The governor's office had no immediate comment on the executive order.
