## Article summary
The recent death of Renee Macklin Good, fatally shot by an ICE officer in Minneapolis, has prompted scrutiny of the Department of Homeland Security's use-of-force policy. This incident draws parallels to the 1992 Ruby Ridge standoff, which led to the establishment of a uniform, written deadly force policy by the Department of Justice. This policy emphasized the paramount value of human life and restricted the use of deadly force to situations where there was no safe alternative. Retired FBI agent John Cox explained that this policy was stricter than the constitutional standard, prioritizing the preservation of life. He suggested that under such a policy, an officer might be expected to avoid using deadly force if a safer alternative, like stepping aside, was available. While the DHS updated its policy in 2023 to include similar principles, it was an administrative update. In contrast, White House adviser Stephen Miller asserted that ICE officers have federal immunity. Democratic representatives Delia Ramirez and Seth Magaziner have introduced the DHS Use of Force Oversight Act to codify a baseline policy for the department, aiming to ensure consistent adherence regardless of administration. Ramirez expressed a desire for a similar bipartisan discussion on policy that occurred after Ruby Ridge, but noted that the administration's characterization of Good's actions as "domestic terrorism" has complicated dialogue. Currently, no Republicans have co-sponsored the bill.
