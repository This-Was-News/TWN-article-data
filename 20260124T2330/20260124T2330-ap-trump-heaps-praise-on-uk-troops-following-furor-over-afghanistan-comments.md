## Article summary
U.S. President Donald Trump has praised British soldiers who fought in Afghanistan, following criticism in the U.K. for comments he made suggesting other NATO nations did not support the U.S. on the front lines. In a social media post, Trump described the British servicemen and women who died and were injured in Afghanistan as "among the greatest of all warriors," emphasizing the strong military bond between the U.S. and the U.K. This statement came after a conversation with British Prime Minister Keir Starmer, who had called Trump's earlier remarks "insulting and frankly appalling." Starmer's office confirmed that the prime minister raised the sacrifices of British and American soldiers in Afghanistan during his discussion with Trump. Trump's previous comments, made in an interview, questioned the commitment of other NATO nations to supporting the U.S. in potential future conflicts. The U.S. led an international coalition in Afghanistan in 2001, with significant troop contributions from numerous countries, including the U.K., which had the largest contingent after the U.S. The Italian and French governments also deemed Trump's earlier remarks unacceptable.
