## Article summary
The Supreme Court is hearing arguments in a case that could impact the independence of the Federal Reserve. At issue are President Trump's efforts to fire Lisa Cook, a member of the Federal Reserve's governing board, which critics argue would break with 112 years of law and precedent. The case stems from accusations that Cook falsified documents for loan applications, which her lawyers deny, stating the applications were legal and "cherry-picked" to appear nefarious. The Trump administration argues that if the president determines there is cause to fire a Fed board member, that decision should not be reviewable by courts, granting the president unrestricted power. This argument is countered by legal scholars and economists who warn that undermining the Fed's independence could destabilize the economy, potentially leading to increased inflation and borrowing rates. The Federal Reserve was established in 1913 to shield monetary policy from political manipulation by giving governors limited terms and barring them from being fired except for malfeasance. Presidents of both parties have historically desired lower interest rates, especially in election years, which can destabilize the economy. The conservative majority on the Supreme Court has previously allowed presidents to remove agency leaders, but the unique structure of the Federal Reserve might lead to a different outcome. If Trump prevails, he could seek to replace other Fed governors, potentially gaining a majority of his own appointees on the board. Prominent figures from business and politics, with the exception of Fed Chair Jerome Powell, have urged the court to preserve the Fed's independence. Paul Clement, former solicitor general under George W. Bush, will argue the case against Trump, and Fed Chair Jerome Powell is expected to attend the arguments.
