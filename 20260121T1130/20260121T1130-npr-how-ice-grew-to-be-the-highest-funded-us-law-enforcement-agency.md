## Article summary
Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) has become the highest-funded U.S. law enforcement agency, with its budget skyrocketing to $85 billion, a significant increase from its less than $6 billion budget a decade ago. This dramatic funding surge is attributed to the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, enacted last July, which provided ICE with a $75 billion supplement. This new funding makes ICE's budget larger than all other federal law enforcement agencies combined. The agency is now leading President Trump's immigration crackdown, deploying thousands of agents and facing criticism for its operations, including recent detentions and a fatality in Minneapolis. ICE's growth follows two decades of modest funding since its creation in 2003. Border encounters with migrants have fluctuated, spiking under President Trump's first term, decreasing during the COVID-19 pandemic, and then soaring under President Biden before decreasing again by late 2024. Upon his return to the White House in 2025, Trump prioritized empowering immigration authorities for swift migrant removal, with ICE at the forefront. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act also allocates $45 billion for ICE to expand its detention capacity, aiming to hold up to 100,000 people daily. Fueled by its increased budget, ICE significantly expanded its workforce in 2025, more than doubling its officers and agents from 10,000 to 22,000, offering substantial incentives for new hires. This rapid expansion occurred during the same year the Trump administration reduced the overall federal workforce. Democrats in Congress are now calling for changes to ICE's operations amidst a spike in deaths within ICE custody. Proponents of the funding increase argue it is commensurate with the agency's task, while critics point out that the funds are heavily concentrated on enforcement, arrest, detention, and deportation, with insufficient support for immigration hearings or judges.
