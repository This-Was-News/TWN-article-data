## Article summary
Nevada's tourism numbers experienced a significant decline in 2025, with a nearly 7.4% drop compared to the previous year. Over the first 11 months of 2025, Las Vegas welcomed just over 35.4 million visitors, marking a cooling off from its post-pandemic rebound. This downturn is seen as a reflection of broader U.S. economic shifts, with Las Vegas often being an early indicator of changes in consumer spending and behavior. The decline has also impacted hotel revenue, with average daily room rates falling by 5% and revenue per available room decreasing by 8.5%. A core issue identified by the Las Vegas Convention Visitors Authority (LVCVA) is the steep drop in international travel, particularly a 24% estimated decrease in Canadian visitors. The LVCVA also pointed to the potential impact of a $250 entrance Visa Integrity Fee on international travelers from certain countries, suggesting it sends an unwelcoming message. Despite these challenges, the LVCVA expressed optimism for 2026, citing a strong trade-show calendar, major events like WrestleMania 42 and the Las Vegas Grand Prix, and anticipated increased international interest due to the 2026 World Cup. However, they remain realistic about ongoing headwinds such as cautious consumer sentiment and new federal policies affecting international visitors. The LVCVA did not comment on the rise of online gambling, which is projected to nearly double in market size by 2030.
