## Article summary
World shares experienced mixed trading on Wednesday as markets steadied ahead of a speech by U.S. President Donald Trump at the World Economic Forum in Davos, Switzerland. Gold prices surged to a record high, surpassing $4,800 for the first time, as investors sought safe-haven assets amid market uncertainty. U.S. stock futures showed gains, with the S&P 500 future rising 0.4% and the Dow Jones Industrial Average future up 0.2%, recovering from a significant drop the previous day. European markets, however, were mostly down, with Germany's DAX shedding 0.4% and France's CAC 40 slipping 0.2%. Investors are anticipating Trump's address, where he is expected to highlight his administration's accomplishments. U.S. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick indicated that the U.S. message would be that globalization has failed. Trump's recent threats to impose tariffs on eight European countries over their opposition to his stance on Greenland have caused market fluctuations and strained relations with allies. In Asian trading, Tokyo's Nikkei 225 declined 0.4%, while South Korea's Kospi gained 0.5% and Hong Kong's Hang Seng rebounded 0.4%. The Federal Reserve is expected to maintain its benchmark interest rate at its upcoming meeting, while Japan's central bank will conclude its monetary policy meeting on Friday. In other markets, U.S. crude oil prices dipped, and the U.S. dollar weakened against the Japanese yen and the euro.
