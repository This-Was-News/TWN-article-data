## Article summary
The Department of Homeland Security (DHS), established in November 2002 in response to the 9/11 attacks, was intended to protect the U.S. from external threats by consolidating agencies like Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) and Customs and Border Protection. President George W. Bush stated its purpose was to analyze threats, guard borders, protect infrastructure, and coordinate emergency responses, focusing government resources on American safety. However, over two decades later, the deployment of thousands of ICE and Border Patrol officers in U.S. cities has led some Democratic leaders to question if the DHS's role has shifted. They argue that the department's enforcement tools are now being used against domestic critics rather than external threats. The presence of officers in tactical gear confronting residents and protesters in cities like Minneapolis, Los Angeles, Portland, Chicago, and Washington has been likened to an occupation. These confrontations, widely shared on social media, create an image of a government in conflict with its own cities. Mayor Keith Wilson of Portland expressed concern, stating that the DHS, designed to protect Americans, is in some cases being turned on them, which he finds deeply unsettling.
