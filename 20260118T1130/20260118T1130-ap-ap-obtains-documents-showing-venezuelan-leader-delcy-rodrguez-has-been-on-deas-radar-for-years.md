## Article summary
Acting President Delcy Rodríguez of Venezuela has been a priority target for the U.S. Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) since at least 2022, according to documents obtained by The Associated Press. The DEA has compiled an extensive intelligence file on Rodríguez, detailing allegations of drug trafficking and gold smuggling, with one informant claiming she used hotels as a front for money laundering. Her name has appeared in numerous DEA investigations across various field offices, indicating sustained interest from the agency. Despite these findings, the U.S. government has not publicly accused Rodríguez of any criminal wrongdoing. President Trump, however, has recently praised Rodríguez, positioning her as a potential partner for stabilizing Venezuela after Nicolás Maduro's capture. This stance is viewed with skepticism by experts and opposition leaders, who describe the Venezuelan government as a "criminal-hybrid regime." Rodríguez's rise to power is linked to her loyalty to Maduro and her socialist background, though she previously attempted to court American investment during the first Trump administration. She was sanctioned by the White House in 2018 for her role in Maduro's authoritarian rule, but these sanctions did not involve corruption allegations. The DEA's interest in Rodríguez highlights the pervasive corruption within Venezuela's government, with experts suggesting the U.S. has leverage over her due to these ongoing investigations.
