## Article summary
President Donald Trump has announced that he will impose tariffs on European countries until the U.S. acquires Greenland. He stated that the U.S. is the only nation capable of protecting Greenland from China or Russia, highlighting the island's strategic importance due to its mineral reserves and proximity to key shipping lanes. Trump expressed that it's time for Denmark to "give back" Greenland, asserting that "World Peace is at stake!" He plans to implement a 10% tariff on goods from Denmark, Norway, Sweden, France, Germany, the United Kingdom, the Netherlands, and Finland starting February 1, increasing to 25% by June 1, until a deal for Greenland's purchase is reached. Officials from Denmark and Greenland have stated the nation is not for sale, and public opinion in Greenland indicates a lack of desire to join the U.S. This move by Trump follows previous attempts by U.S. presidents to acquire Greenland, dating back to 1867. The U.S. currently operates Pituffik Space Base in Greenland under an agreement with Denmark. Trump's use of tariffs has been a consistent element of his presidency, and his recent actions are being scrutinized by trade policy experts regarding executive power and trade law. The legality of his tariffs is also facing a challenge before the Supreme Court.
