## Article summary
A top European official has stated that President Donald Trump's comments regarding Greenland could jeopardize a trade deal between the U.S. and the European Union. Manfred Weber, president of the European People's Party, indicated that his party would not support a deal under these circumstances and called for a halt to proposed 0% tariffs on U.S. products. This statement follows Trump's announcement of potential tariffs on European countries if a deal to annex Greenland is not reached.  Trump aims to acquire the island, but Denmark and Greenland have stated the territory is not for sale, and public opinion in Greenland does not favor joining the U.S. European Union Commission President Ursula von der Leyen expressed solidarity with Denmark and warned that tariffs could damage transatlantic relations and lead to a "dangerous downward spiral."  She emphasized the EU's commitment to unity, coordination, and sovereignty. Trump initially proposed a 10% tariff on goods from Denmark, Norway, Sweden, France, Germany, the United Kingdom, the Netherlands, and Finland, set to increase to 25% if Greenland is not acquired. He cited historical attempts to acquire Greenland and the island's strategic importance due to modern weapons systems.  This situation arises as U.S. allies have sent a symbolic troop presence to Greenland amid Trump's intensified discussions about acquiring the island.  Previously, Trump had announced a trade deal requiring a 15% tariff on imports from the 27-nation EU bloc.
