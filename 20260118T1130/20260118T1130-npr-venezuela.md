## Article summary
Following the capture of Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro by U.S. special forces, hopes for a democratic transition have been tempered by the continued influence of Diosdado Cabello, Maduro's key enforcer. Cabello, Venezuela's Interior Minister, remains in power under interim President Delcy Rodríguez and has publicly condemned the U.S. operation, urging Venezuelans to defend their sovereignty. He and Maduro are both indicted in the U.S. on drug trafficking and narco-terrorism charges, with Washington offering a significant reward for Cabello's arrest. Despite these charges, Cabello, a close confidant of Hugo Chávez, wields considerable power within the Venezuelan government. He previously served as interim president for a brief period in 2002 and, after leading Venezuela's National Assembly and the ruling Socialist Party, was brought back into Maduro's cabinet in 2024 to quell protests following allegations of election fraud. As interior minister, Cabello oversaw a crackdown that resulted in deaths and arrests. Human rights activists and former prosecutors argue that Cabello's continued presence, along with his control over police forces and pro-government gangs, hinders legal stability and the release of political prisoners. Opposition figures consider it too dangerous to return to Venezuela while Cabello and other hardliners remain in positions of power. The future for Cabello involves potential deals for exile, lying low, or cooperating with the interim government, though his long-held ambitions may persist.
