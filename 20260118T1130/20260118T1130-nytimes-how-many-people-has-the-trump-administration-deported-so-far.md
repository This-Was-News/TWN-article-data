## Article summary
Over the past year, President Trump's administration has deported approximately 230,000 immigrants arrested inside the country and another 270,000 at the border, according to a New York Times analysis of federal data. The number of deportations from interior arrests since Trump took office surpasses the total for the entire Biden administration. Despite this, the number of people attempting to cross the Southwest border has fallen to record lows, resulting in fewer border arrests and deportations compared to previous years. An additional 40,000 individuals opted for self-deportation through a government-provided stipend program. Counting deportations is complex due to the lack of a specific legal definition, with estimates encompassing actions by Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) and Customs and Border Protection (CBP). While deportations of border-crossers still constitute about half of the total, ICE's "at-large arrests" on streets and in homes have quadrupled, becoming the majority of immigration arrests in many states. The rate of deportations has increased for individuals with criminal records and those without. Experts generally agree that Trump's policies are beginning to reduce the undocumented immigrant population, though the impact on the overall immigrant population is debated, with some reports indicating a decrease in the undocumented population while the foreign-born population continues to grow.
