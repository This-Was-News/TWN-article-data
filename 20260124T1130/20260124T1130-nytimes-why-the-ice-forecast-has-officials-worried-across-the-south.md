## Article summary
A historic winter storm is anticipated to bring crippling amounts of ice, potentially causing widespread power outages and significant infrastructure damage. Meteorologists are particularly concerned about two main areas: one stretching from Texas to Tennessee with a bullseye over Mississippi, and another along the Appalachians affecting the Carolinas and northern Georgia. Even a quarter of an inch of ice can lead to power outages and falling tree limbs, while near one inch of accumulation could cause prolonged power disruptions and downed trees. Freezing rain is considered the most dangerous precipitation due to its adhesive and accumulating properties, adding significant weight to surfaces. Memphis is in a critical transition zone where a slight shift in the storm could result in a dangerous mix of ice and snow. Residents in this zone may experience sleet, which can form a persistent icy slush on roads. The severity of the ice accumulation is attributed to unusually cold surface temperatures in the South, allowing precipitation to freeze on contact. Forecasters predict temperatures in the mid- to upper 20s, which are cold enough to make ice a significant problem, even on roadways.
