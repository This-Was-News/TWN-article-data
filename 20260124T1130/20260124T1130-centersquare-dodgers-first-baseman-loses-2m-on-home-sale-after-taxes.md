## Article summary
Los Angeles Dodgers first baseman Freddie Freeman recently experienced a $2 million loss on his home sale due to a real estate transfer tax known as Measure ULA. This tax, approved by voters in 2022, applies to properties valued at $5 million or more and charges a 4% tax on the sale price, regardless of whether there was a capital gain or loss. Susan Shelley of the Howard Jarvis Taxpayers Association criticizes the tax, stating it was written by special interest groups and has negatively impacted the Los Angeles real estate market, particularly for apartment buildings and commercial properties. The tax, intended to fund homelessness and housing initiatives, is also criticized for potentially being intercepted by administrative costs rather than directly helping those in need. Steven Greenhut of the Pacific Research Institute echoes these concerns, highlighting that the tax has discouraged building and selling apartments, exacerbating Los Angeles's housing shortage. Media reports and a UCLA study confirm a decline in new apartment construction since the implementation of Measure ULA. The tax's passage with 57.7% approval is also debated in the context of California's requirement for a two-thirds vote on special taxes, which was circumvented through a citizens' initiative loophole.
