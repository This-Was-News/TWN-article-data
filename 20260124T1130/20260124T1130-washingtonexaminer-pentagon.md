## Article summary
The Pentagon's newly released National Defense Strategy indicates that Iran's leaders intend to rebuild their conventional military forces and remain open to the possibility of obtaining a nuclear weapon, despite recent U.S. airstrikes. These strikes, part of Operation Midnight Hammer, targeted three Iranian nuclear facilities in June. While U.S. officials initially claimed the facilities were obliterated and the program set back by one to two years, a preliminary report suggested less damage than publicly stated. The U.S. is increasing its military presence in the Middle East amidst Iran's crackdown on nationwide protests, which the Iranian government claims have ended with over 3,000 deaths. President Trump had threatened military intervention but ultimately decided against it, citing Iran's decision to halt executions. The U.S. has deployed an aircraft carrier and fighter jets to the region. Iran's Foreign Minister stated that Iran's armed forces would retaliate if attacked again. Following the earlier U.S. strikes, Iran launched missiles at a U.S. base in Qatar, but evacuated personnel ahead of time. The National Defense Strategy reiterates President Trump's commitment to preventing Iran from acquiring nuclear weapons and also addresses the U.S. focus on homeland defense and its relationship with China.
