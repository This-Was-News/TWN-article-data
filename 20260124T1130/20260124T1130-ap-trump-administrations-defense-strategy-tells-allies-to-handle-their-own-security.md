## Article summary
The Pentagon has released a new National Defense Strategy that shifts priorities, urging U.S. allies to assume greater responsibility for their own security. This strategy reasserts the Trump administration's focus on dominance in the Western Hemisphere, prioritizing U.S. interests over a long-standing goal of countering China. The document criticizes European and Asian partners for over-reliance on U.S. defense subsidies and calls for a significant change in approach. Allies are now expected to bear more of the burden in deterring threats from nations like Russia and North Korea. This shift comes amid a period of tension between the Trump administration and traditional allies. The strategy also emphasizes the importance of U.S. military and commercial access to key regions like Greenland and the Panama Canal. While seeking cooperation with neighbors in the Americas, it issues a stern warning that the U.S. will decisively act to protect its interests if necessary. Regarding China, the strategy views it as a settled force in the Indo-Pacific, requiring deterrence rather than confrontation, and aims for stable peace and fair trade. Notably, the document makes no mention of or guarantee to Taiwan. In Europe, while acknowledging Russia as a persistent threat, it asserts that NATO allies are powerful enough to take primary responsibility for conventional defense, even as the U.S. reduces its troop presence on NATO's borders with Ukraine.
