## Article summary
The U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) has announced a ban on the use of taxpayer dollars for research that utilizes human fetal tissue obtained from elective abortions. This new policy prohibits researchers and institutions from receiving National Institutes of Health (NIH) funding for studies involving primary human fetal tissue, cells, and derivatives from elective abortions. However, existing human fetal cell lines, such as HEK 293, are exempt from this ban. HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. stated that this shift is supported by science and ethics, and will be applied consistently across the department. HHS Press Secretary Emily Hilliard clarified that elective abortions refer to those on demand, not for medical necessity. This announcement coincided with the National March for Life, where Vice President J.D. Vance praised the administration's decision to reinstate a ban on fetal tissue in federal research. Additionally, the administration has initiated a fraud investigation into 38 Planned Parenthood affiliates, alleging they may have unlawfully received $88 million in COVID-19 era Paycheck Protection Program funds. Small Business Administration Administrator Kelly Loeffler stated that these affiliates were ineligible for such funds and that steps will be taken to recover the money. Planned Parenthood has denied these accusations, labeling them as politically motivated.
