## Article summary
The U.S. House of Representatives is set to vote on the final four government funding bills, totaling $1.2 trillion, just ten days before the fiscal year 2026 deadline. These bills cover the departments of Defense, Labor-Health and Human Services-Education, Transportation-Housing and Urban Development, and Homeland Security. The Defense bill, the largest at $839 billion, includes a 3.8% pay raise for military personnel and funding for President Trump's Golden Dome project. The Transportation-HUD bill allocates $102 billion, with $25 billion dedicated to transportation and border security, and $77 billion for HUD programs. The Labor-HHS-Education bill, at $221 billion, supports various bipartisan priorities but faces opposition due to over $5 billion for migrant and refugee assistance. Democrats express concerns about the $64 billion Homeland Security bill, arguing it does not sufficiently restrict Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) authority, particularly after a recent fatal incident. Despite these disagreements, House leadership aims to pass these bills by Friday to allow the Senate to finalize them next week, avoiding a partial government shutdown.
