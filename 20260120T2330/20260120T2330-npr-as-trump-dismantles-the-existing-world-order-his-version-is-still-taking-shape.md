## Article summary
President Trump's second term is marked by a foreign policy that dramatically diverges from traditional U.S. approaches, dismantling the post-World War II global order established by Harry Truman. This new approach, characterized by an "America First" philosophy, emphasizes unilateral action and a return to 19th-century tenets like the Monroe Doctrine, which Trump rebrands as the "Donroe Doctrine." While his supporters argue this "shock therapy" is necessary to jolt allies out of complacency, critics express concern that it erodes the credibility of alliances. In contrast to his first term, where he aimed to keep the U.S. out of foreign conflicts, Trump has increasingly employed military force in his second term, conducting operations in the Middle East and Africa, and intervening in countries like Venezuela. His foreign policy has shown effectiveness in coercing weaker states but has been less successful in influencing powerful rivals like Russia and China. Allies are increasingly questioning Washington's reliability, leading them to play defense and hedge their long-term strategies to reduce dependence on the U.S. As the old world order breaks down, Trump's alternative vision for global engagement is still in the process of being defined.
