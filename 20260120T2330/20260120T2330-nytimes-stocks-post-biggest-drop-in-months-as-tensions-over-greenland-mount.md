## Article summary
Stocks experienced their largest decline in months on Tuesday, with the S&P 500 dropping over 2 percent, as President Trump's escalating dispute with European leaders over Greenland intensified. This led to a significant increase in Wall Street's fear gauge, the Vix volatility index. The market's reaction reflected growing investor concern about the stability of the global order, a departure from recent geopolitical events that had little impact on corporate profits. Investors had grown accustomed to such upheavals, but Trump's threats of higher tariffs on European allies unless they supported his Greenland plans triggered a sell-off. Notably, both the U.S. dollar and government bonds also lost value, indicating a broader move away from American assets. Financial markets contradicted Trump's optimistic remarks about the U.S. economy. Experts advised investors to focus on diversification and defensive strategies amidst the lingering uncertainty.
