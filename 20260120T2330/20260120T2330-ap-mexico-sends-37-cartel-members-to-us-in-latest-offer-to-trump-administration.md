## Article summary
Mexico has transferred 37 alleged cartel members to the United States as part of an ongoing effort to cooperate with the Trump administration's pressure to crack down on drug smuggling. This marks the third such transfer in less than a year, bringing the total to 92 individuals. The transferred individuals include high-ranking members from prominent cartels such as the Sinaloa Cartel and Jalisco New Generation Cartel, many of whom have pending cases in the U.S. Mexican Security Minister Omar García Harfuch stated that these individuals posed a significant threat to Mexico's security. The transfers come amid increasing rhetoric from President Trump regarding potential military action against cartels operating in Mexico. Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum has communicated with Trump, assuring him of continued collaboration while asserting that U.S. intervention is not necessary. These actions are seen by analysts as a measure to appease the U.S. administration and demonstrate Mexico's commitment to combating organized crime. Previous transfers have included significant figures like drug lord Rafael Caro Quintero. Mexico aims to prevent cartel members from continuing illicit activities from within its prisons and to provide U.S. prosecutors with opportunities to build cases.
