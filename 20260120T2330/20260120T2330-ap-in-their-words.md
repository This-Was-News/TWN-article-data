## Article summary
Leaders at the World Economic Forum in Davos, Switzerland, expressed grave concerns and outrage over President Donald Trump's threats regarding Greenland and potential trade tariffs. California Governor Gavin Newsom used strong language, urging European leaders to "stand tall and firm" and stop being "complicit" with Trump's actions, likening diplomacy with him to dealing with a "T-Rex." French President Emmanuel Macron warned against a "new colonial approach" and "imperial ambitions" that undermine international law and weaken Europe. Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney emphasized the need for middle powers to cooperate to avoid being "on the menu" and affirmed support for Greenland and Denmark's right to self-determination. U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent attempted to downplay the rift, stating that relations remain close and that disagreements over Greenland do not negate NATO alliance or cooperation on other global issues. Belgian Prime Minister Bart De Wever expressed that "so many red lines have been crossed" and warned that backing down would lead to a loss of dignity, suggesting a need to press for a return to the old military alliance. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen called the proposed tariffs a "mistake" between allies and stated that a "diplomatic downward spiral" would only embolden adversaries, suggesting Europe must "build a new independent Europe."
