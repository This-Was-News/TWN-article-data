## Article summary
Federal prosecutors have issued grand jury subpoenas to Minnesota officials, including Governor Tim Walz and Attorney General Keith Ellison, as part of an investigation into potential obstruction of federal immigration enforcement. The probe is reportedly focused on public statements made by these officials regarding a recent immigration operation in the Minneapolis-St. Paul area. Mayors Jacob Frey of Minneapolis and Kaohly Her of St. Paul also received subpoenas. Officials have criticized the investigation as a political tactic to suppress opposition. The subpoenas were issued after the government urged a judge to reject a state lawsuit challenging the immigration enforcement surge. The Justice Department argued that Minnesota is attempting to assert a veto over federal law enforcement. Meanwhile, U.S. Border Patrol reports over 10,000 arrests in Minnesota over the past year, including dangerous offenders. Tensions have been high following the fatal shooting of a civilian by an ICE officer, leading to protests and confrontations between federal agents and the public. Church leaders have also condemned disruptions to religious services by anti-ICE protesters. Legal experts suggest that the state's arguments against federal immigration enforcement may not succeed due to the clear federal authority in this area.
