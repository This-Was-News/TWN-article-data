## Article summary
The official split of TikTok into separate entities for America and the rest of the world signifies a major capitulation for the popular social media app in the United States after a six-year struggle. This outcome highlights the challenging decisions faced by Chinese technology companies aiming for global reach. In the U.S., these companies must navigate evolving geopolitical tensions, lengthy legal disputes, and general distrust towards entities with Chinese origins. An alternative path involves pursuing international markets with potentially fewer obstacles, albeit without access to the U.S. economy. Over the past six years, as TikTok became a focal point in U.S.-China relations, some Chinese tech firms attempted to appear more acceptable to American authorities and investors. Strategies included relocating headquarters to places like Singapore, investing heavily in marketing to build consumer familiarity, and restricting access for users in China to bolster claims of not being Chinese companies. Recently, the artificial intelligence startup Manus appeared to successfully sidestep geopolitical issues when Meta acquired it for an estimated $2 billion. Despite being founded by Chinese engineers with ties to China, Manus had moved its headquarters to Singapore and restricted access in China, seemingly following a successful strategy.
