## Article summary
President Trump, in his second term, is exercising his power to an unprecedented degree, largely due to a staff and administration that prioritizes executing his vision over challenging it. Unlike his first term, where he encountered more resistance from aides and advisors, Trump has surrounded himself with loyal lieutenants, such as Chief of Staff Susie Wiles, who see their role as facilitating his agenda. This shift in approach means fewer "guardrails" and less internal friction, allowing the administration to move at "light speed" to enact policies. Trump is leveraging Article 2 executive power extensively, signing more executive orders in his first year of the second term than in his entire first term. He has taken actions such as sending the National Guard into cities, ordering prosecutions of perceived enemies, imposing tariffs, and taking military action without congressional approval. Political scientists note that Trump is pushing the boundaries of presidential power further than ever seen before. While the Supreme Court may curb some of these actions, Trump has already achieved significant victories. However, this approach may come at a political cost, as his approval ratings have dipped, with a majority of voters believing he has focused on the wrong priorities, though his base remains supportive.
