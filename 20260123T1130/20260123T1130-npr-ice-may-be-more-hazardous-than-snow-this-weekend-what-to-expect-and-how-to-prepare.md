## Article summary
A significant winter storm is expected to impact over 200 million people across the United States starting Friday, bringing a mix of sleet, ice, snow, and extreme cold. Forecasters are particularly concerned about the hazardous effects of freezing rain and ice, which can lead to crippling conditions, long-duration power outages, extensive tree damage, and dangerous travel. Unlike snow, which can be plowed, ice, especially black ice, is difficult to remove and poses a severe risk. Ice accumulations can significantly increase the weight on tree branches and power lines, causing them to break and fall. The National Weather Service has issued warnings for widespread hazardous travel conditions and power outages, urging people to prepare and avoid unnecessary travel. Experts recommend preparing emergency kits with at least three days of supplies for a "Go-Kit" and two weeks for a "Stay-at-Home Kit." If travel is necessary, it's advised to stock your car with emergency supplies, fill your gas tank, check tire inflation, and drive slowly with increased following distances. State officials and meteorologists emphasize that ice storms are often more dangerous than snowstorms due to the unique challenges they present.
