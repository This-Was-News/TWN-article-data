## Article summary
TikTok has struck a deal with a group of non-Chinese investors to create a new U.S. entity, effectively loosening its ties to its Chinese owner, ByteDance. This agreement concludes a six-year legal saga that saw the app face potential bans in the United States due to national security concerns. Investors like Oracle, MGX, and Silver Lake will collectively own over 80 percent of the new U.S. venture, with Adam Presser set to be its chief executive. The deal aims to address worries that Beijing could use the app for surveillance or manipulation of its over 200 million U.S. users. This resolution averts the app's potential exit from the American market, which would have occurred if it hadn't separated from ByteDance. The app's future has been a subject of debate since 2019, with various U.S. government branches and political figures attempting to ban it. Influencers and users actively lobbied and protested to save the app, which became entangled in the broader U.S.-China trade war over technological dominance. The prolonged nature of the situation led some to believe the threat of a ban had diminished.
