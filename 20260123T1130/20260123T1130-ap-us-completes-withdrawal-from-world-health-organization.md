## Article summary
The United States has officially completed its withdrawal from the World Health Organization, a year after President Donald Trump announced the decision. This departure marks the end of a 78-year commitment, despite the U.S. owing over $130 million to the global health agency. Trump cited the WHO's alleged mishandling of the COVID-19 pandemic and its perceived lack of independence as reasons for the withdrawal. Public health experts, however, express strong concerns that this move will significantly hamper global responses to future outbreaks and hinder U.S. scientists and pharmaceutical companies in developing crucial vaccines and medicines. The exit means the U.S. has ceased participation in WHO committees and global health initiatives, including those crucial for tracking flu strains. While the administration claims to be establishing direct data-sharing agreements with other countries, experts doubt the efficacy and scope of these arrangements. Furthermore, there is a legal debate regarding whether Congress's approval was necessary for withdrawal, as it was for joining the organization. The WHO states the U.S. has outstanding dues for 2024 and 2025, totaling over $133 million, though an administration official disputes the obligation to pay prior to withdrawal.
