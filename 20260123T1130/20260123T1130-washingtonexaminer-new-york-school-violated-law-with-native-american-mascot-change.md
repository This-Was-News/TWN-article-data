## Article summary
The U.S. Department of Education has determined that the Connetquot Central School District in New York violated federal civil rights law by changing its Native American mascot, the Thunderbirds, to T-Birds. This change was made to comply with a 2023 New York state law that banned Native American-derived mascots in public schools. The Department of Education's Office for Civil Rights concluded that the district engaged in discrimination based on race and national origin, violating Title VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964. Assistant Secretary for Civil Rights Kimberly Richey stated that the state law created discriminatory classifications by targeting only Native American culture, while other racial or ethnic groups were not subject to such bans. The department views this as an attempt by "ideologues" to ban certain mascots while allowing others. The investigation was opened after the school district affirmed compliance with the state law and changed its mascot as part of a settlement. The Trump administration is proposing a resolution agreement that requires the district to reinstate the Thunderbirds name and associated imagery. The administration emphasizes that equal treatment under the law is non-negotiable and vows to ensure all communities are treated equally.
