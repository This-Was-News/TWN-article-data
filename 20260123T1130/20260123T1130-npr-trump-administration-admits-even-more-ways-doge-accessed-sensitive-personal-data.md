## Article summary
Staffers involved in the Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE) effort improperly accessed and shared sensitive personal data on millions of Americans. The Trump administration has struggled to quantify the extent of the data at risk, its usage, or the necessity of its data consolidation efforts. The Social Security Administration (SSA) revealed that DOGE employees secretly and improperly shared sensitive personal data last year, with an inability to verify the full scope of these violations. This admission came in a court filing that corrected previous testimony regarding DOGE's alleged illegal access to SSA data. Two SSA DOGE employees were referred to a federal watchdog for potentially violating the Hatch Act by discussing a request to match Social Security data with state voter rolls to find evidence of voter fraud and overturn election results. DOGE team members also bypassed IT rules to share data on external servers and sent a password-protected file of private records to DOGE affiliates. A whistleblower, Chuck Borges, alleged that DOGE staffers violated policies and federal laws by copying a dataset of over 300 million Americans' sensitive information into a virtual database without proper security protocols. The data accessed by DOGE has been used to support claims of fraud influencing Trump administration policies on immigration, benefits, and election security. For instance, the Department of Justice sued states for refusing to turn over voter rolls containing sensitive data, which voting rights groups argue violates federal privacy laws. DOGE engineer Aram Moghaddassi sought access to the Department of Homeland Security's SAVE system to check foreign-born individuals' immigration status for fraud detection and also requested Florida's voter data to check for voter fraud. Additionally, Antonio Gracias, an ally of DOGE leader Elon Musk, claimed to have found noncitizens voting based on a sample review of Social Security data and voter rolls, though the number of cases referred for prosecution was small. Activist groups like True the Vote, which have made unsupported claims of election fraud, have publicly appealed to DOGE for access to federal databases to audit voter rolls. The administration's data consolidation efforts have also extended to immigration enforcement, with plans to share sensitive Medicaid and IRS data with the Department of Homeland Security.
