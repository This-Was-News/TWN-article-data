## Article summary
Texas and Oklahoma are preparing for a significant winter storm that is predicted to bring heavy snow and crippling ice across the eastern two-thirds of the U.S. Forecasters warn that the storm could cause catastrophic damage and extensive power outages, with some areas facing ice accumulation comparable to a hurricane. Approximately 160 million people are under winter storm or cold weather watches and warnings. Cold air from Canada has already led to school closures in Chicago and Des Moines, with dangerous wind chills posing a frostbite risk. The storm system is expected to produce heavy snow from Oklahoma to the Northeast and a final blast of extreme cold in the upper Midwest. Governors in Georgia and Mississippi have declared states of emergency, and residents in affected areas are stocking up on supplies and preparing for potential prolonged power outages. Airlines have canceled hundreds of flights, and transportation departments are pre-treating highways. Texas officials are confident their power grid is stronger than during a severe cold snap five years ago that caused widespread outages and deaths. The unpredictable nature of winter storms makes precise forecasting challenging, with the most severe impacts often becoming clear as the event unfolds.
