## Article summary
Six state attorneys general, led by Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier, have called on the nonprofit climate company Ceres, Inc. to cease activities they allege violate antitrust and consumer protection laws. The attorneys general contend that Ceres pressures companies into ESG investments and net-zero emissions initiatives, aiming to transform industries and achieve systemic change. Founded after the Exxon Valdez oil spill, Ceres states its mission is to work with companies to acknowledge environmental impacts and act more responsibly. However, the attorneys general accuse Ceres of coordinating pressure through 'stakeholder engagement,' shareholder resolutions, and 'investor campaigns to replace directors,' impacting the oil and gas industry by decreasing demand and investment in new supplies. They argue these efforts to artificially alter markets and suppress competition are anticompetitive and violate the Sherman Antitrust Act. Will Hild of Consumers Research echoed these concerns, calling Ceres a leader of a 'climate cartel' pushing radical political agendas and ESG policies that harm consumers and the economy. Jason Isaac of the American Energy Institute accused Ceres of using 'dark money' and collusive pressure campaigns. The attorneys general assert that companies targeted by Ceres risk greenwashing claims if they fail to meet unrealistic goals. Uthmeier stated that Ceres' 'Global World War' to achieve net zero must stop, as it assaults American families and businesses.
