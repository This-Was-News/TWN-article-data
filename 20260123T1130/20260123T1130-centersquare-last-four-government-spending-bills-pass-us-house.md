## Article summary
The U.S. House of Representatives has passed the final four of its fiscal year 2026 government funding bills, sending them to the Senate for approval by January 30. These bills total $1.2 trillion and cover appropriations for the departments of Defense, Labor-Health and Human Services-Education, Transportation-Housing and Urban Development, and Homeland Security. House Speaker Mike Johnson highlighted the bipartisan effort in passing all 12 appropriations bills. The Defense bill allocates $839 billion, including a 3.8% pay raise for servicemembers. The Labor-HHS-Education bill funds various programs like Pell Grants and rural initiatives with $221 billion. The Transportation-HUD bill provides $102 billion for transportation, border security, and housing assistance. The Homeland Security bill, however, passed with only seven Democratic votes due to concerns about Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) authority, allocating $64 billion and maintaining ICE funding at $10 billion. Some Democrats argued the bill lacks sufficient oversight and accountability for ICE. The Senate will vote on these four bills, along with two others already passed by the House, upon its return. Three appropriations bills have already become law, and three more await presidential signature.
