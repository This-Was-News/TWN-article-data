## Article summary
Calls are mounting for Congress to reclaim its trade policy authority following President Donald Trump's imposition of a 10% tariff on nations supporting Greenland. Trump's persistent desire to acquire Greenland, which he deems strategically vital for U.S. national security, has escalated since his return to office, even suggesting forceful annexation. This approach, coupled with his use of executive actions to bypass Congress on trade matters, has prompted economic experts and lawmakers to urge Capitol Hill to assert its Article 1 powers. Jason Furman, former Obama administration economic advisor, advocates for Congress to set fiscal policy rather than leaving it to presidential whims. Daniel Hannan, a UK House of Lords member, suggested impeachment as a recourse for Trump's "madness" and his "imperial posturing," citing a letter to the Norwegian prime minister as evidence of his erratic behavior. Republican Representative Don Bacon indicated he would lean towards impeachment if an invasion of Greenland were authorized, calling the president's rhetoric "ridiculous" and an "attack on an ally." Senator Ed Markey has called for invoking the 25th Amendment. House Speaker Mike Johnson, however, maintains a more muted stance, emphasizing diplomatic channels. Republican strategist Dennis Lennox warns that Trump's "Greenland fantasy" is damaging the GOP's credibility abroad. A bipartisan congressional delegation visited Denmark to reassure leaders that Trump's rhetoric does not represent the entire U.S. Congress, with Senators Thom Tillis and Lisa Murkowski expressing concerns about any forceful acquisition of sovereign territory. Senator Rand Paul noted a lack of Republican support for acquiring Greenland. The document also touches upon Congress's weakening power under Trump and a recent debate over AI chip sales to China, where some Republicans are pushing back against administration policies.
