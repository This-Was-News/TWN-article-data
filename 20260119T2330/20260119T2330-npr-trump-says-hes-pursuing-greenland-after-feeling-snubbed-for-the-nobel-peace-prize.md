## Article summary
President Donald Trump stated his pursuit of U.S. control over Greenland is a consequence of not receiving the Nobel Peace Prize last year, leading him to feel less obligated to prioritize peace and more focused on American interests. He conveyed this sentiment to Norway's Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre, criticizing Norway for the perceived snub and asserting that complete control of Greenland is essential for global security. The Prime Minister clarified that Norway does not award the Nobel Peace Prize, which is handled by a separate committee. This exchange occurred amidst rising tensions between the U.S. and Europe over Greenland, following Trump's threat to impose tariffs on eight European countries for deploying military personnel to the region for a Danish-led exercise. The European Union is planning an emergency summit to address these threats, with leaders from Denmark, Finland, France, Germany, the Netherlands, Norway, Sweden, and the United Kingdom issuing a joint statement condemning the proposed tariffs as damaging to transatlantic relations. Trump's justification for acquiring Greenland includes its strategic location and resources, questioning Denmark's historical claim to the territory.
