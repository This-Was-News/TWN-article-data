## Article summary
A new report from the Kiel Institute for the World Economy indicates that Americans are bearing the cost of nearly all of President Donald Trump's foreign tariffs, contradicting his claims that other nations absorb this financial burden. The German think tank's research suggests that for every $100 in tariff revenue collected by the U.S. government, $96 originates from American importers and consumers, with only $4 impacting foreign exporter profits. This finding aligns with previous studies from U.S. institutions and financial entities. The report posits that tariffs function as a consumption tax on Americans rather than a tax on foreign producers. One reason foreign exporters may not be lowering prices is their belief that the tariffs could be temporary. Other analyses, including projections from Goldman Sachs and studies on European wines, have also indicated that American consumers and businesses pay a significant majority of tariff costs. However, some, like former Trump trade adviser Peter Navarro, argue that the actual payer is determined by market bargaining power. The Congressional Budget Office has adjusted its projections to reflect that foreign businesses might bear approximately 5% of the tariff costs through reduced prices. In response to concerns about rising grocery prices, Trump previously exempted over 200 food products from tariffs. Tariffs have been a key policy tool for Trump, with various adjustments and legal challenges ongoing, including a case heard by the Supreme Court.
