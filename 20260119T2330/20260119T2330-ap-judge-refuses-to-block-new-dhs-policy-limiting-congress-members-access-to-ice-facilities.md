## Article summary
A federal judge has declined to temporarily block a new Department of Homeland Security policy that requires a week's notice before members of Congress can visit Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) facilities. U.S. District Judge Jia Cobb ruled that the plaintiffs, representing several Democratic members of Congress, used the wrong legal procedure to challenge the policy. She stated that the January 8 policy is a new agency action distinct from a previous policy she had temporarily blocked. The challenge arose after three Minnesota Democratic representatives were denied access to an ICE facility shortly after a fatal shooting involving an ICE officer. Last month, Cobb had temporarily blocked a prior policy requiring a week's notice for such visits, deeming it likely illegal. However, DHS Secretary Kristi Noem issued a new memorandum reinstating a similar notice requirement, which was not disclosed until after the representatives were turned away. Cobb's ruling on Monday emphasized that she was not judging the legality of the new policy itself, but rather the procedural method used to challenge it. The plaintiffs argued that the policy obstructs congressional oversight, which is crucial for ongoing funding negotiations for DHS and ICE. They also contended that the administration has not proven that appropriated funds are not being used to implement the new notice policy, which they claim violates a law barring DHS from using general funds to prevent congressional access for oversight. The judge had previously noted that requiring a week's notice makes it difficult to reconstruct the actual conditions within ICE facilities on the day of a requested visit.
