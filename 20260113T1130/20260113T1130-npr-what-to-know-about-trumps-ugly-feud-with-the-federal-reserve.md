## Article summary
Federal Reserve Chairman Jerome Powell has publicly responded to a Justice Department criminal probe into the Fed, accusing the Trump administration of using federal prosecutors to interfere with the central bank's decisions on interest rates. Powell stated that this unprecedented action should be viewed in the context of the administration's ongoing pressure campaign. President Trump has long desired lower interest rates and has publicly criticized Powell, even threatening to fire him and attempting to remove a Fed board member. While the Fed has cut rates recently, Trump believes they should be lower to stimulate the economy and reduce government borrowing costs. The administration has also attacked the Fed over cost overruns in its headquarters renovation project, which Powell attributes to inflation and unforeseen issues like lead and asbestos, not extravagance. Powell views the Justice Department probe as a pretext to undermine the Fed's independence, asserting that monetary policy should be based on evidence, not political pressure. Economists generally agree that central bank independence is crucial for effective economic management. Powell has vowed to continue his job with integrity despite the threats. The upcoming nomination of Powell's successor as Fed chair is expected to keep the focus on the central bank's independence, with some senators vowing to oppose any nominee until the Justice Department's legal matter is resolved.
