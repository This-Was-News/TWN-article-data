## Article summary
Minnesota officials have filed a lawsuit against the Trump administration to halt an intensified immigration crackdown in the Minneapolis region. The lawsuit, lodged in the U.S. District Court for the District of Minnesota, follows an incident where an Immigration and Customs Enforcement agent fatally shot a civilian. State Attorney General Keith Ellison alleges that federal agents have unlawfully arrested bystanders, detained U.S. citizens, and used chemical irritants against demonstrators. The lawsuit describes the operation as "militarized raids" and "dangerous, illegal, and unconstitutional stops and arrests in sensitive public places." Homeland Security spokesperson Tricia McLaughlin refuted these claims, accusing Ellison of prioritizing politics and asserting that law enforcement acts on reasonable suspicion. Over 2,000 federal immigration agents are reportedly in Minnesota, with plans for further increases. Incidents described include agents questioning individuals about their immigration status in public parking lots, with some individuals fearing for their safety when speaking to the media. There have also been reports of agents engaging in vehicle pursuits and collisions with residents. Similar legal action has been taken by Illinois and the city of Chicago against the Trump administration's immigration enforcement tactics, alleging unlawful interrogations, arrests without warrants or probable cause, and the use of chemical agents.
