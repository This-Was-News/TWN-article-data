## Article summary
Tensions are high in Minnesota as federal agents and protesters repeatedly clash following the fatal shooting of Renee Good, a Minneapolis woman, by an immigration enforcement officer. Federal authorities deployed tear gas to disperse crowds protesting the incident and the broader immigration crackdown. In response, Minnesota, along with Minneapolis and St. Paul, has filed a lawsuit against the Trump administration, alleging violations of constitutional protections and free speech rights. The Department of Homeland Security plans to send over 2,000 immigration officers to the state for what ICE calls its largest enforcement operation ever. Homeland Security spokesperson Tricia McLaughlin defended the operation, stating President Trump's duty is to protect Americans and enforce the law. The lawsuit aims to halt or limit the surge, with Minnesota Attorney General Keith Ellison calling the operation a "federal invasion." The Trump administration maintains that Good and her vehicle posed a threat, a claim disputed by state officials based on video evidence. Similar federal crackdowns have led to lawsuits in other states, such as Illinois, where residents reported fear of leaving their homes. Meanwhile, in Portland, Oregon, federal authorities pressed charges against a Venezuelan national involved in an incident where a truck struck a Border Patrol vehicle.
