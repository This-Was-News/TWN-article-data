## Article summary
Iran has eased some restrictions, allowing mobile phone calls abroad after days of internet and communication blackouts amid escalating protests. Despite this, internet access to the outside world and texting services remain down, with the death toll from the protests reportedly reaching at least 646. International bodies, including the UN Human Rights chief and foreign ministers from Finland and the Netherlands, have condemned the violence and called for the immediate restoration of internet access. Finland and the Netherlands have summoned Iranian ambassadors to protest the crackdown. Meanwhile, Iran claims to have arrested "Israel-linked terrorist groups" in Zahedan. Nobel laureate Malala Yousafzai expressed solidarity with the Iranian protest movement, emphasizing the struggle for girls' and women's autonomy. The German chancellor believes the Iranian government is nearing its end due to its reliance on force. Israel stated it remains on alert due to the situation in Iran but has not altered civilian guidelines. The U.S.-based Human Rights Activists News Agency reported the death toll, with a significant number of casualties among both protesters and security forces, and over 10,700 detentions.
