## Article summary
Arizona Governor Katie Hobbs delivered her State of the State address, prioritizing affordability for the state's residents. She highlighted job creation and reduced costs for childcare, prescription drugs, and housing, while acknowledging that more needs to be done to address the financial struggles of families. Hobbs proposed tax relief for the middle class and suggested that billionaires and large corporations should contribute more in taxes. She also criticized the federal government's denial of emergency funding for flood-stricken communities in Globe and Miami and called for the booming data center industry to contribute more to water usage costs. Regarding border security, Hobbs stated her administration is actively working to secure the border, combat drug trafficking, and apprehend criminals, seeking reimbursement for Arizona's significant border security expenses. Furthermore, she advocated for increased oversight of the Education Savings Account program to prevent waste, fraud, and abuse. In response, Senate Republicans, including Senate President Pro Tem T.J. Shope and Senate Majority Leader John Kavanagh, argued that the governor's policies have worsened the situation, leading to higher costs and unresolved problems. The Republican Governors Association also criticized Hobbs, claiming she lacks solutions and has mismanaged the state budget. Gubernatorial elections are scheduled for November 3, 2026, with several Republicans vying to challenge Hobbs in the general election.
