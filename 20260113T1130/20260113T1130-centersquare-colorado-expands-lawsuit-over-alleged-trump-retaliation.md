## Article summary
Colorado's Attorney General Phil Weiser has expanded a lawsuit against the Trump administration, alleging a "campaign of punishment" against the state. The lawsuit, initially filed in October, was updated with additional retaliatory actions taken by the administration. Weiser stated that the U.S. Constitution does not permit the president to single out states for punishment based on their sovereign powers. The original lawsuit was prompted by President Trump's decision to move the U.S. Space Command Headquarters from Colorado Springs to Alabama, which Colorado alleges was a punishment for its vote-by-mail system. The updated lawsuit includes further actions such as terminating $109 million in transportation funding, ending $615 million in Department of Energy funding, dismantling the National Center for Atmospheric Research, threatening SNAP funding, and denying disaster relief requests. Colorado argues these actions violate the Tenth Amendment, the Elections Clause, state sovereignty, separation-of-powers principles, and federal laws. The state is seeking a declaration that these actions are unconstitutional. Colorado has been a frequent litigant against the Trump administration, joining or filing approximately 50 lawsuits in the past year.
