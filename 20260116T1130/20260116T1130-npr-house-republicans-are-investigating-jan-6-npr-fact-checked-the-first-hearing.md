## Article summary
A new Republican-led congressional subcommittee investigating the January 6, 2021, attack on the U.S. Capitol has held its first hearing, which NPR fact-checked, identifying multiple false and misleading claims. These claims coincide with a broader effort by the Trump administration to rewrite the history of the attack. The hearing focused on the investigation into pipe bombs discovered outside the Republican and Democratic national committees' headquarters on January 6, a case that had stalled under the Biden administration, fueling conspiracy theories. Conservative podcaster Dan Bongino had previously suggested the bombs were placed by an anti-Trump insider or as an inside job, but later, as Deputy Director of the FBI, announced the arrest of a suspect, Brian Cole Jr., who had pleaded not guilty. Representative Clay Higgins claimed the Biden FBI had undercover agents and confidential informants embedded within rally crowds and conspired to entrap Americans, but the facts show Joe Biden was not president at the time, and an inspector general report found no evidence of FBI undercover employees in the crowds or directing illegal acts. Representative Barry Loudermilk stated that Trump could not send the National Guard unless requested by the legislative branch and that requests were made only after shots were fired, but the president has direct control of the D.C. National Guard, and Capitol Police requested assistance prior to the building breach, though troops did not arrive until hours later. Representative Troy Nehls criticized police officers who testified before the previous Jan. 6 committee as "Trump haters" with scripted testimonies, but one officer, Michael Fanone, who suffered a traumatic brain injury, had previously voted for Trump in 2016.
