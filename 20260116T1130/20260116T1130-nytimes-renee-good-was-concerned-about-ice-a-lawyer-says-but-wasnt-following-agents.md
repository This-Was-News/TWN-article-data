## Article summary
Renee Good was concerned about the actions of immigration agents in Minneapolis but was not following them on the morning she was fatally shot by an agent, according to her family's lawyer, Antonio M. Romanucci. The incident sparked protests and highlighted differing interpretations of President Trump's immigration policies, with allies defending the shooting as self-defense and many local officials defending Good. Romanucci stated that Good and her partner, Becca Good, had stopped to observe activity near their home, exercising their right to assemble. The couple, who had moved to Minneapolis to raise their child, were reportedly engaged with neighbors about immigration enforcement. Romanucci's office has opened a civil investigation, anticipating legal action against the U.S. and the agent for excessive force and negligence. He questioned the escalation of the situation, noting that Good had stated she was not angry at the agent. The family has been deeply hurt by descriptions of Good as engaging in domestic terrorism, with Romanucci emphasizing her character and the family's suffering. The FBI is investigating the shooting, but the family's lawyer expressed concerns about the investigation's impartiality. Federal prosecutors have reportedly looked into Good's ties to activist groups, and some prosecutors resigned over a push to investigate Becca Good, whom Romanucci believes is being unfairly targeted.
