## Article summary
The Insurrection Act is a law that allows the U.S. President to deploy troops domestically to quell civil unrest. President Trump has threatened to invoke it in response to protests in Minnesota following the fatal shooting of a woman by an ICE agent. This act differs from National Guard deployments as it permits armed forces to perform law enforcement functions like making arrests. The Insurrection Act has been invoked infrequently since its enactment in 1807, with the last use during the 1992 Los Angeles riots. There are three ways to invoke the act: at the request of a state, when federal law enforcement is obstructed, or to suppress an insurrection hindering federal law execution. Unlike other military deployments, the Insurrection Act is an exception to the Posse Comitatus Act, which generally prohibits the military from acting as domestic police. Legal experts express concern over the act's broad and loosely defined terms, granting significant discretion to the president. Invoking the Insurrection Act for purposes beyond quelling insurrections, such as immigration enforcement, would be a departure from historical precedent and likely face legal challenges.
