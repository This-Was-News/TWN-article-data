## Article summary
Hundreds of students from high schools in St. Paul, Minnesota, walked out of class to protest the presence of Immigration and Customs Enforcement agents in the Twin Cities. The Saint Paul Public Schools district stated that students who missed class for the protest would be marked absent or tardy, emphasizing that the walkout was not district-sponsored. The district respects the right to protest but does not accompany or supervise students who leave campus. In response to safety concerns related to federal agents, the superintendent announced a temporary virtual learning option for students starting January 22. School will also be closed on Monday and Tuesday for Martin Luther King Jr. Day and a subsequent day. The district aims to ensure students remain connected to school, whether in-person or virtually. This follows a report that other Minneapolis Public Schools students could opt for remote learning after an ICE officer shooting. The district has not yet received a response from the SPPS parents advisory council regarding the matter.
