## Article summary
Canada has agreed to reduce its 100% tariff on Chinese electric vehicles (EVs) as part of a deal that includes lower tariffs on Canadian farm products. Prime Minister Mark Carney announced the agreement following meetings with Chinese leaders in Beijing, aimed at improving bilateral relations. Under the new terms, there will be an initial annual cap of 49,000 Chinese EVs exported to Canada, increasing to approximately 70,000 over five years. In exchange, China will lower its tariff on Canadian canola seeds from 84% to about 15%. This move signifies a significant shift, as Canada had previously aligned with the U.S. on imposing tariffs on Chinese EVs and other goods. The agreement also includes provisions for China to invest in the Canadian auto industry within three years. Prime Minister Carney emphasized the importance of focusing on areas of alignment to improve global governance, which he described as being under strain, partly due to an 'America-first' approach. This visit marks the first by a Canadian prime minister to China in eight years and aims to re-establish dialogue and a framework for cooperation, particularly in light of global trade disruptions. The deal is seen by some as a way for Canada to build an economy less reliant on the U.S. and to bring more affordable vehicles to Canadian consumers.
