## Article summary
Venezuelan opposition leader María Corina Machado met with President Donald Trump at the White House, presenting him with her Nobel Peace Prize medal. Trump confirmed the gesture on social media, calling it a "wonderful gesture of mutual respect." The White House later posted a photo of Trump holding the framed medal, with a note expressing gratitude for his actions securing a free Venezuela. Despite this symbolic exchange, the White House maintained skepticism about Machado's ability to lead the country. Machado also met with a bipartisan group of senators, emphasizing the need for progress towards a transition of power in Venezuela within months. The meeting occurred as U.S. forces seized another sanctioned oil tanker linked to Venezuela, part of an effort to control the country's oil following the capture of former President Nicolás Maduro. Machado had been in hiding before traveling to Norway to receive her prize and subsequently to Washington. The White House press secretary stated that the meeting was a "frank and positive discussion" but did not change Trump's opinion on Machado's leadership capabilities. Senators expressed concern about the lack of a clear timeline for elections in Venezuela. Machado's visit coincided with the release of several Americans detained under Maduro's government, a move that followed cooperation from Venezuela's interim authorities.
