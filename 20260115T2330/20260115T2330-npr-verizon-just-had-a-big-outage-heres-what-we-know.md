## Article summary
Verizon, the largest mobile network in the United States, experienced a significant outage on Wednesday, leaving many customers without service for a considerable part of the day. While Verizon has stated the outage is resolved and attributed it to a "software issue," a precise explanation for the cause remains unclear. The company reported receiving a massive number of outage reports, though the exact number of affected customers is unknown. Such widespread disruptions, while concerning, are not uncommon in the telecommunications industry, with competitors like AT&T also facing large-scale outages previously. Experts suggest that the increasing complexity of cloud-based and software-dependent networks, a shift from older, hardware-reliant systems, introduces more vulnerabilities for glitches and attacks. The consequences for users included a complete loss of connectivity, with only "SOS" mode being accessible. Verizon has initiated damage control, offering credits to affected customers and advising them to restart devices. The Federal Communications Commission is actively investigating the situation. Experts believe that such outages could happen again to any major telecommunications firm due to the inherent nature of modern, cloud-based networks. They also advise consumers to consider having a backup connectivity plan, such as a landline or a secondary phone on a different network, highlighting the evolving vulnerabilities in the U.S. wireless grid.
