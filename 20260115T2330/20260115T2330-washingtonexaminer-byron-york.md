## Article summary
A recent shooting incident in Minneapolis involving an Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) officer and an undocumented immigrant highlights a growing tension between state and federal law enforcement in Minnesota. The incident, which resulted in injuries to both individuals, was described by the Department of Homeland Security as a defensive action by the officer who was attacked by the immigrant and two others. This event underscores a broader sentiment in parts of Minnesota where there is significant opposition to ICE operations. Minnesota Attorney General Keith Ellison has filed a lawsuit against ICE and DHS officials, characterizing ICE's presence as a "federal invasion" driven by political motives and arguing that the 10th Amendment grants the state sovereign authority to protect its residents, thus restricting federal enforcement. Legal analysts, however, deem this argument "novel" and "crazy," noting the lack of legal precedent for a state barring federal law enforcement from enforcing federal laws within its borders. A federal judge recently declined Minnesota's request for a temporary restraining order against ICE, indicating that the legal challenge may not succeed despite the strong rhetoric from state officials. The core of the dispute centers on whether a state can prevent federal agencies from enforcing federal law within its jurisdiction, a fight that continues in Minneapolis.
