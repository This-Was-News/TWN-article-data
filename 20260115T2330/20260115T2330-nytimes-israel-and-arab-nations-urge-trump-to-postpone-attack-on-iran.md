## Article summary
The fatal shooting of Renee Good by an ICE agent in Minneapolis has intensified scrutiny on the training and preparedness of federal agents enforcing President Trump's deportation agenda. Internal ICE documents emphasize minimal force and de-escalation tactics, yet the Trump administration has publicly supported more aggressive actions and offered "absolute immunity" to agents like the one who killed Ms. Good. Video analysis suggests Ms. Good may not have been attempting to run the agent over, contradicting initial administration claims. This incident highlights a potential disconnect between official guidelines, which prioritize de-escalation and a use-of-force continuum, and the administration's rhetoric, which appears to tacitly approve lethal force in self-defense. Furthermore, ICE agents, historically focused on targeted arrests, may lack adequate crowd-control training for large-scale sweeps, a mission they are increasingly undertaking with expanded funding and expedited training programs. The Justice Department's decision to investigate Ms. Good's widow and protest groups, rather than the agent's actions, further underscores the administration's priorities. Despite the internal training materials stressing de-escalation, the message from Trump administration officials to agents seems to be to disregard established training and legal advice, raising concerns about future confrontations.
