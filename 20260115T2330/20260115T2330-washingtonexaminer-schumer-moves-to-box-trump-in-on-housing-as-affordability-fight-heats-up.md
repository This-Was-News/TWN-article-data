## Article summary
Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer is launching a Democratic offensive on housing costs, aiming to put President Donald Trump on the defensive. Schumer framed housing as the starting point of a broader Democratic push on affordability, emphasizing it as the largest unavoidable expense for families. He highlighted the crisis of rising rents, the increasing age of first-time homebuyers, and the shortage of millions of housing units nationwide. Schumer outlined a four-goal Democratic housing agenda: reducing rents, expanding homeownership, curbing predatory corporate behavior, and increasing housing construction. He proposed expanding rental relief and Section 8 vouchers, lowering upfront costs for homebuyers, easing mortgage access, and addressing rising homeowners insurance premiums. Schumer also targeted corporate landlords and institutional investors, vowing to crack down on pricing algorithms and "rental junk fees." He stressed the need for increased housing construction, advocating for federal incentives and pressure on states and localities to reform restrictive zoning laws. Schumer accused House Republicans of blocking the bipartisan ROAD to Housing Act and criticized Trump for not intervening. While Trump has also targeted housing affordability, proposing to block large institutional investors from buying single-family homes, Schumer dismissed this as "stolen" from Democrats and accused Trump of inaction. Schumer also criticized Trump's policies for potentially driving up construction prices through tariffs and destabilizing housing finance. He seized on the DOJ investigation into Federal Reserve Chairman Jerome Powell, arguing that politicizing housing finance would worsen affordability by keeping interest rates higher. Democrats aim to tie housing to a broader cost-of-living message, with Schumer calling it the first step in an agenda called "Opportunity Starts at Home."
