## Article summary
International media have descended upon Greenland's capital, Nuuk, to cover the geopolitical attention President Donald Trump has brought to the Arctic island. Trump's expressed desire to control Greenland, a semi-autonomous territory of Denmark, has sparked significant international interest, with journalists from major outlets like the AP, Reuters, CNN, BBC, and Al Jazeera flocking to the region. Local Greenlanders, however, emphasize that their island is not for sale and that decisions about its future rest with its own people. Politicians and community leaders report being overwhelmed with interview requests as media crews capture the island's unique landscape and daily life. Trump has cited national security and the alleged designs of China and Russia on Greenland's vast mineral resources as justifications for his ambitions. Despite these claims, Greenlanders like Maya Martinsen express bewilderment at Trump's obsession, viewing his interest as a pretext to exploit their untapped resources rather than a genuine concern for their home. The media's presence highlights the growing international awareness of Greenland's strategic importance, even as its residents assert their right to self-determination.
