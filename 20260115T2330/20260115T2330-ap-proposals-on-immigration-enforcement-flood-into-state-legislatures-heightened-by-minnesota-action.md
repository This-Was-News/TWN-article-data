## Article summary
Following the shooting death of a protester in Minneapolis by an Immigration and Customs Enforcement officer, Democrats in several states are proposing new laws to restrict federal immigration enforcement. These proposals range from allowing individuals to sue federal officers for constitutional rights violations to prohibiting state law enforcement cooperation with federal immigration efforts. New York, Oregon, New Jersey, and California are among the states where such measures are being introduced or have been passed. In contrast, Tennessee Republicans, with White House backing, are advancing bills to bolster the state's support for President Trump's immigration crackdown, including measures to verify the legal status of residents seeking public benefits, licenses, and driver's licenses. The Trump administration has actively opposed efforts to limit ICE, initiating lawsuits against states and cities with sanctuary policies. Legal experts anticipate significant federal litigation over these state laws, as they raise complex constitutional questions. States like Minnesota and Illinois are also suing the Trump administration, alleging violations of free speech rights and overreach in federal immigration enforcement actions. The escalating conflict highlights a deep divide between federal immigration policy and state-level initiatives, with legal battles expected to shape the future of immigration enforcement.
