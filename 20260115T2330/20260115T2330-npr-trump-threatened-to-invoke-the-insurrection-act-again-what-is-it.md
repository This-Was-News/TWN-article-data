## Article summary
President Trump has threatened to invoke the Insurrection Act to suppress protests in Minnesota following the fatal shooting of Renee Macklin Good by an ICE agent and a subsequent shooting of a Venezuelan immigrant. The Insurrection Act allows the president to send troops to states to restore law and order, and unlike National Guard deployments, it permits armed forces to carry out law enforcement functions like making arrests. This act, signed into law in 1807, has been invoked around 30 times, with the last instance occurring over three decades ago. There are three ways to invoke the act: at the request of a state legislature or governor, or when unlawful obstructions make enforcing federal laws "impracticable," or when an insurrection "hinders the execution of the laws." Before invoking it, the president must order insurgents to disperse. Deployments under the Insurrection Act are an exception to the Posse Comitatus Act, which generally prohibits federal military personnel from acting as police. The act's terms like "insurrection" are loosely defined, granting broad discretion to the president. Legal experts suggest that using the Insurrection Act for purposes beyond quelling violent uprisings, such as enforcing immigration laws, would be a departure from historical precedent and likely face legal challenges.
