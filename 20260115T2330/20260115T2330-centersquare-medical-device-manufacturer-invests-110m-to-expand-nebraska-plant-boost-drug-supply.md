## Article summary
Becton, Dickinson and Company (BD), a major medical device manufacturer, is investing $110 million to expand its plant in Columbus, Nebraska. This expansion aims to increase the production of prefillable syringes crucial for biologic drugs, including popular GLP-1 weight loss medications. The investment is projected to create approximately 120 new jobs in the state. This initiative aligns with broader efforts to bolster domestic pharmaceutical manufacturing and strengthen U.S. supply chains, a priority highlighted by the Trump administration. BD's commitment extends beyond Nebraska, with plans to invest over $2.5 billion in U.S. manufacturing over the next five years. The Nebraska expansion includes roughly $100 million for the production of its BD Neopak glass prefillable syringe platform and an additional $10 million for cannula manufacturing. These syringes are designed for injectable biologic drugs and are compatible with autoinjectors, seeing increased demand due to the rapid growth of diabetes and obesity medications. The company emphasizes that localizing production of these drug delivery components will enhance reliability and speed to market. This expansion builds upon a previous $35 million investment at the same facility, which added about 50 jobs. Nebraska officials, including Sen. Pete Ricketts and Gov. Jim Pillen, have praised the investment for its economic benefits and its contribution to keeping critical manufacturing within the state.
