## Article summary
The U.S. Supreme Court has agreed to hear a second election law case, Watson v. Republican National Committee, which will determine if federal laws prohibit states from accepting mail-in ballots after Election Day. This follows a recent ruling in Bost v. Illinois State Board of Elections, where the court affirmed an Illinois congressman's right to sue over post-Election Day ballot counting. Election integrity advocates, like Jason Snead of the Honest Elections Project, have expressed concerns about the ease with which liberal candidates and groups can challenge election laws compared to conservatives seeking to uphold them. Sixteen states and the District of Columbia currently allow mail-in ballots to be counted for up to two weeks after Election Day, provided they are postmarked by Election Day. The Watson case specifically challenges Mississippi's law, which permits ballots to be counted for up to five days after Election Day if postmarked by the deadline. Snead urged state lawmakers to adjust statutes to block delayed mail-in ballot receipt deadlines before the upcoming midterm elections, anticipating a potential Supreme Court ruling against such practices. While some voting rights advocates worry about the timing of the decision, Snead believes a ruling by June or July would allow sufficient time for public education, emphasizing the importance of voters mailing their ballots early.
