## Article summary
Iran's top judge has signaled that detained protesters may face swift trials and executions, as activists report the death toll from nationwide demonstrations has surpassed 2,571. These protests, initially sparked by economic hardship, have evolved to target the ruling theocracy. State television has acknowledged a high number of casualties, attributing the delay in reporting to the severity of injuries. Amidst the crackdown, Iran has eased some restrictions, allowing international phone calls but maintaining internet and texting limitations. The U.S. has warned of potential military action over the crackdown, while Middle Eastern governments are reportedly advising against conflict. Starlink is now offering free satellite internet service within Iran, providing a crucial communication channel. Activists also report that Iranian state media has aired at least 97 coerced confessions from protesters, often after alleged torture. India has urged its nationals to leave Iran due to the evolving situation, and Pakistani students studying in Iran are returning home. Meanwhile, President Trump's proposed tariffs on countries trading with Iran could impact global markets and U.S. consumers. In Berlin, two individuals attempted to tear down the Iranian flag at the embassy. The scale of the current death toll is unprecedented in decades, drawing comparisons to the 1979 Islamic Revolution.
