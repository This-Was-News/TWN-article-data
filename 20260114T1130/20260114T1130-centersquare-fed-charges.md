## Article summary
Haitian and Yemeni immigrants have been charged with stealing tens of millions of dollars from the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) during the Biden administration. U.S. Senators Ted Cruz and John Cornyn have proposed legislation that would allow for the revocation of citizenship privileges and deportation for foreign nationals who defraud the federal government. The proposed bill could impact two Haitian individuals who owned Boston-area bodegas and were charged in a $7 million SNAP fraud scheme. Antonio Bonheur and Saul Alisme, both Haitian, were accused of food stamp fraud, with their stores allegedly receiving up to $500,000 a month in SNAP benefits despite their small size and limited inventory. Undercover operations revealed that SNAP benefits were allegedly trafficked for cash, and liquor was sold in exchange for SNAP benefits. They also allegedly sold MannaPacks, food intended for humanitarian relief, for $8 each. In a separate case, a bodega in Brooklyn, New York, was the center of an alleged scheme by Yemeni immigrants that stole $20 million in SNAP benefits from individuals and families across at least 10 states. Dawood Kassim and Dia Alqalisi were charged with SNAP fraud for conducting fraudulent transactions and allowing SNAP recipients to exchange benefits for cash or non-SNAP eligible goods. Kassim, born in Yemen, is a U.S. citizen and a licensed real estate agent, while Alqalisi claims to be born in the U.S. to Yemeni immigrant parents. Oklahoma residents were significantly affected, with state authorities unable to reimburse stolen EBT funds. Secretary of Agriculture Brooke Rollins has mandated that state agencies share SNAP data to prevent illegal immigrants from receiving benefits, with 29 states complying and 21 Democratic states refusing and filing lawsuits.
