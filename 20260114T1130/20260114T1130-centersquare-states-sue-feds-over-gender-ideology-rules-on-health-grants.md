## Article summary
Twelve states, led by New York, California, and Oregon, have filed a lawsuit against the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services. The lawsuit challenges new grant conditions that the states' attorneys general argue illegally discriminate against transgender people. Oregon Attorney General Dan Rayfield stated that the policy demands recipients of health, education, and research funding certify compliance with an executive order that denies the existence of transgender people and imposes rigid definitions of sex. This policy, according to Rayfield, uses federal money to interfere with personal medical decisions. The plaintiffs contend that the new policy forces states to choose between discriminating against transgender individuals or losing vital HHS grants. California Attorney General Rob Bonta described the changes as an effort to target transgender, nonbinary, intersex, and gender nonconforming individuals, accusing HHS of overstepping its authority. The lawsuit asserts that the Trump administration cannot change a law through an executive order on gender ideology and is attempting to rewrite Title IX, which prohibits sex-based discrimination. New York Attorney General Letitia James called the directive "cruel and unjust," stating it threatens healthcare, research, and education programs by denying the dignity of transgender people.
