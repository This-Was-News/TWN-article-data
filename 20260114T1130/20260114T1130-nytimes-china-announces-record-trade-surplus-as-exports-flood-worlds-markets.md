## Article summary
China has announced a record-breaking trade surplus of $1.19 trillion for the past year, a 20 percent increase from the previous year. This surplus, driven by a surge in exports to regions like the EU, Africa, and Latin America, was achieved despite US tariffs. While US trade surplus decreased by 22 percent due to tariffs, Chinese companies rerouted goods through Southeast Asia to bypass these measures. A key factor contributing to the surplus is China's weak import market, a result of its industrial policy aimed at domestic self-reliance. Furthermore, diminished consumer spending power among Chinese families, exacerbated by a housing market crash, has led to a greater focus on exports. A weaker currency also makes Chinese goods more competitive internationally. In contrast to Western inflation, China faces deflation due to factory overcapacity and weak domestic demand. Despite warnings from the IMF about relying too heavily on exports and calls for currency appreciation, China maintains its export-focused strategy to mitigate economic harm from the housing market downturn. This massive trade surplus, exceeding historical records from Japan and Germany, has significant global implications, creating jobs in China while leading to factory closures elsewhere.
