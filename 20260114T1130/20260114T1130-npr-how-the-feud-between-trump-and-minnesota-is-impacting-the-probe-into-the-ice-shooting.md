## Article summary
A dispute has arisen between federal and Minnesota authorities over the investigation into the fatal shooting of Renee Macklin Good by an ICE agent. The FBI has declared it will solely lead the inquiry, a move Minnesota officials view as unusual and potentially unfair, advocating for a joint investigation. This disagreement is the latest in a series of conflicts between the Trump administration and Minnesota officials regarding the shooting. Legal experts suggest that deviating from typical joint investigations could undermine both federal and state probes. The handling of such sensitive cases is crucial for public trust, and a lack of cooperation can fuel skepticism. Minnesota officials have expressed doubts about receiving a fair outcome, especially after President Trump referred to Macklin Good as "radical." The FBI's exclusion of the state's Bureau of Criminal Apprehension, which has expertise in officer-involved shootings, has also drawn condemnation. Historically, Minnesota authorities have collaborated on high-profile cases, including the George Floyd murder. Joint investigations are seen as more efficient and lend legitimacy to findings. Without federal cooperation, Minnesota authorities face challenges in accessing evidence, potentially hindering any state-level prosecution. Experts warn that this situation could set a precedent for federal authorities sidelining state investigations in future cases, impacting accountability for federal officers.
