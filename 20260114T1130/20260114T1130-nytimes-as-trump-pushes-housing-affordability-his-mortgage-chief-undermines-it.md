## Article summary
Despite President Trump's stated goals of improving housing affordability, the head of his mortgage oversight agency, Bill Pulte, has implemented policies that appear to undermine this objective. Pulte, who leads the Federal Housing Finance Agency (FHFA), has overseen the repeal of rules, the dismantling of teams focused on fair lending and climate risk, and a reduced emphasis on low-income borrowers. Advocates argue these actions will increase costs for those least able to afford them, contradicting the administration's stated commitment to addressing the housing crisis. The FHFA, established to oversee Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac after the 2008 financial crisis, has seen significant personnel changes under Pulte, with numerous officials and entire teams being dismissed. These firings have affected departments responsible for fair lending, climate risk analysis, and supporting underserved groups. Simultaneously, Pulte has rescinded guidance on climate change risk management and ended the purchase of loans made through special purpose credit programs designed to assist disadvantaged demographics. The agency has also proposed eliminating penalties for fair lending violations and has weakened affordable housing goals. These shifts, coupled with a focus on reducing costs and potentially preparing Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac for a public offering, have raised concerns among former employees and housing advocates, who fear a return to the risky practices that led to the 2008 crisis. While some market factors like falling rents and the Fed's rate easing offer some relief, the overall mood within the FHFA and its related entities is described as uncertain and chilly, with ongoing firings and controversial proposals.
