## Article summary
NASA is bringing the four-person Crew-11 mission home from the International Space Station a month early due to an undisclosed medical condition of one astronaut. This marks the first medical evacuation in the ISS's 25-year history, though officials emphasize it is not an emergency as the astronaut's condition is stable. The crew, including NASA astronauts Zena Cardman and Mike Fincke, Russian cosmonaut Oleg Platonov, and JAXA astronaut Kimiya Yui, is expected to return to Earth on January 15, 2026. The decision to return early was made to allow for a thorough medical evaluation on the ground, as the full scope of medical testing equipment is available there. While the identity and nature of the medical issue remain private, all crew members appeared well during a recent ceremony. NASA has previously trained for medical emergencies and anticipated such situations might arise, although this is the first time it has occurred. The space agency has managed various medical issues in orbit before, including treating ailments like toothaches and ear pain, and even a discovered blood clot, which was managed with medication sent from Earth. A replacement crew is scheduled to launch in February, and two other astronauts will remain on the ISS.
