## Article summary
Justice Sonia Sotomayor has sharply criticized the Supreme Court's recent trend of adopting dissenting opinions as majority rulings. During oral arguments for a case concerning state laws limiting women's sports to biological females, Sotomayor questioned the Justice Department's lawyer about their brief, which advocated for the adoption of a dissent's viewpoint. She highlighted that the court has recently overturned several key precedents, citing decisions on abortion in Dobbs v. Jackson Women's Health Organization and affirmative action in Students for Fair Admissions v. Harvard. Sotomayor also noted that the court appears poised to potentially overturn the 1935 ruling in Humphreys Executor v. United States, which could expand presidential firing power. This is not the first time Sotomayor has voiced strong opinions during oral arguments, having previously alluded to corruption and criticized the Trump administration's actions. The current cases regarding transgender athletes in sports are expected to be decided by the end of June, with the liberal justices likely to side with the athletes and the conservative majority with the states. These cases are among several high-profile decisions the Supreme Court is expected to release this term, covering topics such as tariffs, birthright citizenship, mail ballots, and gun laws.
