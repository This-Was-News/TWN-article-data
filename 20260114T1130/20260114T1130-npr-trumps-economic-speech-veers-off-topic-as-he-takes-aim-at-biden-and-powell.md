## Article summary
President Trump delivered a speech at the Detroit Economic Club, initially intended to focus on the economy, but it quickly veered into criticisms of Joe Biden, Federal Reserve Chairman Jerome Powell, and other topics. Trump claimed his administration had achieved the best economic numbers on record, despite recent polling showing his handling of the economy at a historic low. He outlined plans to combat fraud, freeze federal payments to sanctuary cities, and cap credit card fees. However, a significant portion of his speech was dedicated to blaming Biden for inflation and attacking Powell, whom he referred to as "that jerk" and suggested would be "gone soon." This speech marked Trump's first domestic trip after focusing on international affairs. The economic concerns of voters, particularly high costs and affordability, remain a significant issue, impacting recent election results and expected to be crucial in upcoming midterms. Despite these concerns, the White House has highlighted falling gas prices and mortgage rates as economic successes. Trump also attributed his economic achievements to tariffs, particularly a 25% tariff on foreign automobiles, which he claimed benefits Michigan autoworkers. He visited a Ford facility before his remarks.
