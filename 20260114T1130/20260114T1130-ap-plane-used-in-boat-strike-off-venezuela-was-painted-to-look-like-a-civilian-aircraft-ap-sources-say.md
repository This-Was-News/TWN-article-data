## Article summary
The U.S. military plane involved in a drug-smuggling boat strike off Venezuela was painted to resemble a civilian aircraft, according to AP sources. This tactic appears to contradict the Pentagon's manual on the laws of war, which prohibits perfidy, or feigning civilian status to attack. The plane also carried munitions internally, raising further questions about the operation's disguise. These revelations come amid heightened U.S. military pressure on Venezuela, which culminated in the capture of President Nicolás Maduro. The Senate is set to vote on a war powers resolution to limit further military action in Venezuela without congressional authorization. President Trump is reportedly attempting to dissuade Republican senators from supporting this resolution. The administration has justified past boat strikes by asserting an armed conflict with drug cartels. However, U.S. military guidelines explicitly warn against using civilian disguises in combat. The plane in question was used in a Sept. 2 strike, the first in a series of deadly operations against suspected drug boats. Lawmakers are seeking answers regarding the legality of these actions, particularly a follow-up strike that killed survivors. Additionally, senators have reviewed a classified legal opinion justifying the use of military force to oust Maduro, though some express concerns about its secrecy and the long-term U.S. involvement in Venezuela.
