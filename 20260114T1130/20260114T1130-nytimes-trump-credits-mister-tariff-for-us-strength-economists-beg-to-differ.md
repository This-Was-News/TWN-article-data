## Article summary
President Trump has recently expressed increased enthusiasm for tariffs, crediting them for the country's strong economy, declining trade deficit, and booming stock market. However, economists largely disagree, attributing the current economic strength primarily to the artificial intelligence boom, which is driving investment in data centers and boosting stock market gains. New tax deductions are also encouraging investment, but the manufacturing sector, intended to be helped by tariffs, appears to be struggling, with surveys indicating a contraction for ten consecutive months and a slump in spending on new factories. Manufacturing jobs have been shedding, and smaller manufacturers are reportedly affected by higher input costs due to tariffs. Some economists suggest the U.S. is growing in spite of tariffs, with the AI boom offsetting their negative impact. While supporters believe tariffs will eventually benefit manufacturing, uncertainty about their longevity has hindered their effectiveness. The recent improvements in economic output and the trade deficit may be partly due to volatility caused by policy changes, such as companies importing goods in anticipation of tariffs, which temporarily reduced GDP but were later offset by a drop in imports. Economists are seeking more data to discern the true impact of tariffs from these fluctuations. The future effectiveness of tariffs as a policy tool may depend on the overall economic performance, with voters potentially basing their views on broad economic trends rather than granular policy analysis.
