## Article summary
Los Angeles County is considering the creation of ICE-free zones in response to federal immigration enforcement actions. Supervisors Lindsey Horvath and Hilda Solis have proposed an ordinance to be drafted within 30 days, aiming to prevent the use of county property for what they describe as violence and harassment by Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE). Horvath stated that the county will not allow its property to be a staging ground for violence and that public spaces should be safe havens, not places of fear. Supervisor Janice Hahn echoed these sentiments, emphasizing that the county cannot allow its property to be a tool for ICE's work. The U.S. Department of Homeland Security, however, questioned the legality and benefit of such an ordinance, asserting that enforcing federal immigration laws is a clear federal responsibility and criticizing local politicians for releasing criminals onto the streets. The department spokesperson stated that their officers will continue to arrest criminals to make Los Angeles safe again.
