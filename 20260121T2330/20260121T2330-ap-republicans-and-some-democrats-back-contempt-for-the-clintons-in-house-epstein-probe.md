## Article summary
A House committee has advanced resolutions to hold former President Bill Clinton and former Secretary of State Hillary Clinton in contempt of Congress regarding the Jeffrey Epstein investigation. This move could lead to the House using a significant punishment against a former president for the first time. The Republican-controlled House Oversight Committee approved the charges with bipartisan support, including some Democrats who emphasized the need for transparency in the Epstein probe. Chairman James Comer stated that no one can willfully defy a congressional subpoena without consequence. The Clintons have denied involvement for decades and offered to be interviewed in New York, but Chairman Comer insisted on an official transcript. Lawmakers are investigating the connections between Epstein and powerful figures, including the Clintons and Donald Trump. The Clintons' lawyer has been negotiating with Comer, proposing testimony on holidays and offering written declarations, arguing the subpoenas lack legislative purpose. Democrats have largely focused on advancing the investigation, with some suggesting Hillary Clinton be exempted due to limited personal interaction with Epstein and proposing the contempt resolution be civil rather than criminal. Democrats criticized Chairman Comer for focusing on the Clintons while the Justice Department is delayed in releasing Epstein case files. The committee plans to interview Ghislaine Maxwell, Epstein's confidant, next month. Historically, contempt proceedings are rare, used as a last resort, with recent examples including convictions of Trump's former advisers Steve Bannon and Peter Navarro for defying subpoenas. No former president has been successfully forced to appear before Congress, though some have voluntarily. Some Republicans believe the Clintons should face similar consequences to Bannon and Navarro if they refuse to testify.
