## Article summary
During oral arguments in the Supreme Court case *Trump v. Cook*, Justice Samuel Alito questioned Federal Reserve governor Lisa Cook's lawyer, Paul Clement, about the grounds necessary for a president to remove a Fed governor. Alito posed hypothetical scenarios, asking if past support for Adolf Hitler or the Ku Klux Klan would constitute sufficient cause for removal. Clement maintained that even in these extreme hypothetical situations, impeachment, rather than direct removal by the president, would be the appropriate course of action, and that such a process would likely lead to the official's resignation. The case centers on whether President Trump had the authority to fire Cook, who is contesting her dismissal. Cook was fired by Trump in August, based on allegations of mortgage fraud. A lower court had previously ruled that Cook could remain in her position while the legal challenge proceeded. The Supreme Court's decision in this case could have implications for the independence of the Federal Reserve and the president's removal powers over independent agency heads. The court is expected to rule on this emergency docket case sooner than the merits docket case, *Trump v. Slaughter*, which also concerns presidential removal powers.
