## Article summary
In a lengthy speech at the World Economic Forum, President Trump targeted familiar and new adversaries, alternating between praise and threats towards European allies. He reiterated his demand for the United States to acquire Greenland, assuring that force would not be used for this purpose. However, his mocking tone throughout the speech heightened European leaders' concerns about NATO and U.S. relations. Trump criticized NATO members directly, attacked leaders of several nations, Somali immigrants, and wind power. Regarding Greenland, he described it as poorly located and emphasized its strategic importance for national security, suggesting China and Russia could exploit it. He proposed immediate negotiations for its acquisition, while also stating he would not use force. Despite this, Trump implied a threat to European leaders, suggesting they agree to the U.S. acquiring Greenland to earn American gratitude, or face being remembered negatively if they refused. The speech underscored ongoing anxieties among European leaders regarding the stability of alliances and transatlantic relationships.
