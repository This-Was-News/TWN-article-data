## Article summary
A federal appeals court has temporarily paused a lower court's order that restricted Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) tactics in Minnesota. The U.S. Court of Appeals for the 8th Circuit lifted an injunction previously issued by U.S. District Judge Katherine Menendez. This injunction had limited ICE officers' use of arrests, tear gas, pepper spray, and other force against non-violent protesters. Attorney General Pam Bondi hailed the appeals court's decision as a victory for the Trump administration's Operation Metro Surge. Bondi criticized the district court's order as an attempt to hinder federal law enforcement. The lawsuit stemmed from protests against increased immigration enforcement in the Twin Cities, with plaintiffs alleging violations of their First and Fourth Amendment rights. Judge Menendez had barred ICE from using force against peaceful protesters, stating that actions like crowds forming around officers or following vehicles did not justify a forceful response. The appeals court's pause allows ICE to operate without these restrictions while the case is further reviewed. The dispute arises amidst heightened protests in the Twin Cities, including incidents involving ICE operations and local unrest. The ACLU filed the lawsuit on behalf of plaintiffs who claim they were unjustly assaulted or arrested by ICE officers during demonstrations.
