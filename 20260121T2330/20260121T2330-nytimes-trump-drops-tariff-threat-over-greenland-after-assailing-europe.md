## Article summary
President Trump announced he has reached a "framework agreement" with NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte regarding Greenland, leading him to drop tariff threats against European allies. The deal, which reportedly involves the U.S. gaining sovereignty over small pockets of Greenlandic land for military bases, aims to prevent Russia and China from establishing a foothold in the Arctic. Trump's announcement followed a NATO meeting where such a compromise was discussed. While details remain scarce, NATO confirmed that negotiations between Denmark, Greenland, and the U.S. will continue to ensure Arctic security. Trump's earlier demands for outright U.S. ownership of Greenland had caused significant diplomatic tension and market volatility. He also delivered a lengthy speech at the World Economic Forum in Davos, Switzerland, where he criticized European allies, repeated his claims about NATO's financial contributions, and distorted historical events. During his speech, Trump also mistakenly referred to Greenland as Iceland and confused its economic impact on U.S. markets. Meanwhile, Greenland's government advised residents to prepare for potential crises, though they did not directly link it to Trump's statements. The U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent also made controversial remarks about California Governor Gavin Newsom, who was reportedly denied entry to a U.S. venue at Davos. The broader context of Trump's visit highlighted deep concerns among European leaders about the future of the U.S.-European alliance and the U.S. commitment to global security.
