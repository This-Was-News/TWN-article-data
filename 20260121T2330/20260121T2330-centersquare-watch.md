## Article summary
Advocates and legal experts are urging the U.S. Supreme Court to establish uniform national gun laws, following arguments in a case challenging Hawaii's restrictive concealed carry law. The case, Wolford v. Lopez, questions Hawaii's statute that prohibits permit holders from entering certain public places like gas stations and beaches without property owner consent. During oral arguments, gun rights supporters gathered outside the court, emphasizing that while private property owners can prohibit firearms, government mandates to do so constitute overreach. A key debate point involved the distinction between free speech rights and gun rights on private property open to the public, with arguments that store owners can still post signs restricting guns independently. Justices also focused on historical laws to interpret the Second Amendment, with Hawaii's lawyers referencing Black codes, a move criticized by some as a weak legal strategy. Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson's apparent support for considering Black codes was noted, while Justice Clarence Thomas seemed to favor later amendments like the 14th. Hawaii's argument that its state customs do not assume widespread gun carrying was countered by the assertion that national customs and traditions should guide Second Amendment interpretation. The case has implications for similar restrictions considered in other states, and advocates seek clear Supreme Court guidance on constitutional limits for concealed carry laws nationwide.
