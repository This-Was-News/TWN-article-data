## Article summary
The Supreme Court appears hesitant to grant President Trump the authority to fire Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook. The Trump administration argues that a president's decision to fire a Fed board member for cause should not be subject to judicial review. Cook's attorney contends that such unchecked power would severely undermine the independence of the central bank. The administration's stated cause for removal involves accusations of falsifying documents related to property loans, which Cook denies. Her legal team highlights that other Trump administration officials have engaged in similar mortgage applications without facing accusations of wrongdoing. While the Supreme Court's conservative majority has previously allowed Trump to remove other agency leaders, a previous court opinion hinted that the Federal Reserve might be a distinct entity. If Trump succeeds in removing Cook, he could potentially replace other Fed governors to gain a majority on the board. The case's outcome remains uncertain, but the court's apparent skepticism suggests a potential safeguard for the Federal Reserve's independence.
