## Article summary
President Donald Trump announced at the World Economic Forum in Davos that he is withdrawing his threat to impose tariffs on European countries, citing a new "framework" agreement with NATO on Arctic security. This decision comes after Trump stated the U.S. would not use force to acquire Greenland from Denmark, though he reiterated his desire for U.S. ownership of the island for national security reasons. The U.S. stock market saw a rebound following Trump's announcement about dropping the tariffs. California Governor Gavin Newsom criticized Trump's speech at Davos as "remarkably boring" and unoriginal. Russian President Vladimir Putin commented that Trump's bid to acquire Greenland does not concern Russia. European allies expressed relief over Trump's decision to back off the tariff threat, which had caused concern among U.S. officials as well. Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney met with NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte to discuss Arctic security investments. Trump also discussed his "Board of Peace" initiative, inviting Russia to join and suggesting frozen assets could fund it. In other news, the Trump administration is increasing the stipend offered to migrants for voluntary deportation. Trump also repeated claims about his economic policies and addressed issues like credit card interest rates and housing affordability.
