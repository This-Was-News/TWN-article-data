## Article summary
President Donald Trump has proposed a $1.5 trillion budget for the Department of War, aiming to build a "Dream Military" and enhance national security. He suggested that tariff revenue, along with tariff rebate checks and debt reduction funds, could finance this significant increase in defense spending. However, an analysis by the Committee for a Responsible Federal Budget estimates this plan would actually boost defense spending by $5 trillion over the next decade, with interest raising the total to $5.8 trillion, and that tariff revenue would not be sufficient to cover the costs. Republican leaders in the Senate and House Armed Services Committees, Roger Wicker and Mike Rogers respectively, have endorsed Trump's proposal, citing escalating global threats from China, Russia, Iran, and narco-terrorists, and emphasizing the need to rebuild American military strength and leadership. They anticipate tangible outcomes such as accelerated production of ships and aircraft, modernization of the arsenal, and development of advanced technologies. Secretary of War Pete Hegseth views the budget as a global message. Conversely, some Democrats have criticized the plan, highlighting the Pentagon's ongoing inability to accurately account for its spending, with an expected inability to do so before 2027. They point to the Department of War's failure in its eighth consecutive audit in December 2025, arguing that the agency should be held to the same budgetary standards as other federal agencies and questioning the justification for increased funding amidst a lack of accountability and transparency. The Department of War had previously informed Congress of its intention to achieve full accounting of spending and assets by 2028.
