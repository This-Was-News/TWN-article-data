## Article summary
The House of Representatives has passed the final four of twelve essential government funding bills, with a deadline of January 30th to avert a government shutdown. The bills, totaling approximately $1.2 trillion, cover appropriations for Defense, Labor, Health and Human Services, Transportation, Housing and Urban Development, and Homeland Security. The Department of Homeland Security bill faced significant debate due to Democratic concerns about Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) operations. Democrats secured a $115 million funding reduction for ICE, a decrease of 5,500 detention beds, and $20 million for body cameras. Despite these concessions, key Democratic leaders and several members voted against the DHS bill, citing insufficient action on ICE. Some Republicans also withheld votes, negotiating for concessions on ethanol fuel standards, leading to the creation of an E15 Rural Domestic Energy Council. House leaders managed to secure enough votes by addressing the concerns of various GOP factions. Notably, a procedural vote to advance the bills saw some initial Republican opposition before members ultimately flipped their votes. The passage of these bills now sets the stage for a crucial vote in the Senate, which is returning from recess to address the remaining funding measures.
