## Article summary
The U.S. House of Representatives has passed the final four of its fiscal year 2026 government spending bills, sending them to the Senate for approval. These bills total $1.2 trillion and cover funding for the departments of Defense, Labor, Health and Human Services, Education, Transportation, Housing and Urban Development, and Homeland Security. House Speaker Mike Johnson highlighted the bipartisan support for most of these appropriations, noting that all 12 bills have now passed the House. The Defense bill allocates $839 billion, including a 3.8% pay raise for servicemembers. The Labor-HHS-Education bill provides $221 billion for various programs, and the Transportation-HUD bill includes $102 billion for transportation, border security, and housing assistance. The Homeland Security bill, however, faced criticism and passed with only seven Democratic votes due to concerns about its provisions regarding Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE). This bill maintains ICE funding at $10 billion and allocates $20 million for body cameras for federal immigration officers, but critics argue it lacks sufficient oversight and accountability for ICE's authority. The Senate is expected to vote on these bills, along with others already passed by the House, next week. Three appropriations bills have already become law, and three more await the president's signature.
