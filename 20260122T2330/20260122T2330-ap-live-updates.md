## Article summary
Vice President JD Vance stated that the Insurrection Act is not currently needed in Minneapolis, despite the city being a focal point for the Trump administration's immigration crackdown. Vance also addressed reports of federal authorities asserting power to enter homes without warrants, clarifying that warrants would still be required unless officers are under immediate threat. The administration is facing scrutiny over its immigration enforcement, including the arrest of protesters at a Minnesota church and the detention of a 5-year-old boy and his father. Meanwhile, former special counsel Jack Smith defended his investigations into President Trump before the House Judiciary Committee, asserting that no one is above the law. Trump himself has been active, suing JPMorgan Chase, announcing a "Board of Peace" initiative at Davos, and planning a trip to Iowa to speak with farmers. The administration is also gathering data on federal funds in predominantly Democratic states and expanding a ban on foreign aid to include groups promoting DEI and gender identity. In international affairs, Trump's "Board of Peace" aims to challenge the UN's role in global conflict resolution, and the U.S. is moving a fleet of ships toward Iran amidst tensions. The House narrowly defeated a war powers resolution aimed at checking Trump's military actions in Venezuela, and Trump has expressed confidence that both Putin and Zelenskyy are ready to end the war in Ukraine.
