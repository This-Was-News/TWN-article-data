## Article summary
Special counsel Jack Smith testified before the House Judiciary Committee, defending the two federal criminal indictments he secured against President Trump. This marked Smith's first public testimony regarding his work. Republican members of the committee, led by Chairman Jim Jordan, questioned Smith, suggesting his investigations were politically motivated to target Trump. Smith asserted his impartiality, stating, "I am not a politician and I have no partisan loyalties," and denied his office spied on anyone. He maintained that the evidence clearly showed President Trump was the most culpable in the conspiracy to overturn the 2020 election results. Smith argued that the law required accountability for Trump's actions, including his role in the Jan. 6th Capitol attack. He expressed no regrets about his work, only wishing he had shown more appreciation for his team. During the hearing, Trump posted on Truth Social, calling Smith a "deranged animal" and suggesting the Justice Department should investigate him. Smith stated he would not be intimidated and anticipated potential retaliation from the Trump Justice Department. He also addressed the classified documents investigation, noting that a volume of his report concerning that matter remains blocked from public release by Judge Aileen Cannon.
