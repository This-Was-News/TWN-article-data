## Article summary
President Trump signed the founding charter for his proposed Board of Peace at the World Economic Forum in Davos, Switzerland. This new international body aims to oversee postwar stabilization in Gaza and potentially other conflict zones. The initiative, championed by Trump's son-in-law Jared Kushner, includes a vision for rebuilding Gaza with new infrastructure like airports and seaports, contingent on Hamas disarming. While Trump presented the board as a global mechanism, some U.S. allies, particularly European nations like France, the U.K., Belgium, Italy, and the Netherlands, have publicly refused to join, citing concerns about undermining existing institutions like the United Nations and the potential participation of Russia. Slovenia also declined, emphasizing the importance of established global frameworks. Despite this resistance, countries such as Azerbaijan, Argentina, Uzbekistan, Hungary, and Israel have expressed support or agreed to participate. The board is intended to coordinate international assistance, support security, and guide post-conflict administration, operating outside formal U.N. structures. The initiative comes amid severe humanitarian conditions and unclear governance in Gaza, creating pressure for long-term solutions beyond emergency relief.
