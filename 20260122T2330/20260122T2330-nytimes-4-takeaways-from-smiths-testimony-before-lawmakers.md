## Article summary
Former special counsel Jack Smith testified before Congress to defend his decision to bring two criminal indictments against Donald Trump. His five-hour testimony to the House Judiciary Committee was his first opportunity to officially justify the charges related to Trump's efforts to overturn the 2020 election and the illegal removal of classified documents. Smith repeatedly denied acting out of partisan animus, contrasting his actions with the Trump administration's alleged use of the Justice Department against political enemies. Republican members of the committee focused their questioning on procedural aspects of Smith's investigations, such as obtaining phone records and issuing subpoenas, to suggest political motives. However, they offered little challenge to Smith's assertion that sufficient evidence existed to secure convictions had the cases gone to trial. Smith explained that the investigative tactics he employed are standard in criminal prosecutions and that he adhered to legal and Justice Department procedures. The testimony highlighted a debate over the legitimacy of the investigations and the methods used, rather than the substance of the charges themselves.
