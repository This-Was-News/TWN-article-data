## Article summary
The House of Representatives has approved a series of spending bills to fund large parts of the federal government and avert a shutdown, sending the package to the Senate. While the bills passed with broad bipartisan support, seven House Democrats voted against the funding for the Department of Homeland Security due to concerns about Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) tactics. House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries stated that ICE is "totally out of control." Despite a narrow Republican majority, the measure passed 220-207. Democrats had sought to implement stricter measures on ICE, such as prohibiting deportations of citizens and banning raids on sensitive locations, but ultimately accepted concessions including funding for ICE body cameras and increased oversight. Some Democrats, like Rep. Henry Cuellar, supported the bill as a better alternative to a continuing resolution or a government shutdown. However, others, like Rep. Rosa DeLauro, felt the gains did not outweigh the grave concerns over ICE. Republicans, such as Rep. Tom Cole, argued that Democrats were overlooking essential government services funded by the bills. The Senate is expected to vote on the measures next week, with funding set to expire on January 30th. Senator Patty Murray indicated she would vote for the bills, stating that a shutdown would remove critical guardrails on the administration's actions.
