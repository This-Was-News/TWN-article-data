## Article summary
Six state attorneys general, led by Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier, have urged the nonprofit climate organization Ceres, Inc. to cease activities they claim violate antitrust and consumer protection laws. The attorneys general allege that Ceres, founded after the Exxon Valdez oil spill, pressures companies into ESG investments and net-zero initiatives through various means, including stakeholder engagement and investor campaigns. They argue that Ceres' efforts to influence markets, reduce oil supply, and increase prices constitute anticompetitive behavior, potentially violating the Sherman Antitrust Act. The letter also suggests that companies targeted by Ceres risk greenwashing claims if they fail to meet the organization's goals. Critics like Will Hild of Consumers Research describe Ceres as a leader of a 'climate cartel' pushing radical agendas that harm consumers and the economy. Jason Isaac of the American Energy Institute accused Ceres of using 'dark money' and collusive pressure to manipulate markets and suppress competition. The attorneys general contend that Ceres' 'Global World War' to achieve net-zero emissions is an assault on American families and businesses.
