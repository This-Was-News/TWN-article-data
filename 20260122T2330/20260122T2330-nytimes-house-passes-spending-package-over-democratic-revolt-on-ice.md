## Article summary
The House of Representatives has passed a comprehensive spending package for various government departments, including Homeland Security, the Pentagon, Health, and Transportation. This legislation narrowly secured enough votes to fund the Department of Homeland Security, despite significant opposition from Democrats regarding funding for Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE). The package rejects President Trump's proposed deep cuts to agencies like the CDC and NIH, instead allocating increased funding to the NIH. It also maintains current funding levels for ICE, rejecting a requested increase. This development is notable as it represents the successful passage of individual spending bills without resorting to a large, omnibus package or a stopgap measure, thus avoiding a government shutdown. However, the bill faced strong objections from Democrats, particularly after the fatal shooting of Renee Good by a federal immigration agent, leading to a substantial number of Democrats voting against the homeland security funding component. Ultimately, the homeland security bill passed with a narrow margin, with Republican leaders allowing a separate vote on this measure to accommodate Democratic dissent without jeopardizing the entire spending package.
