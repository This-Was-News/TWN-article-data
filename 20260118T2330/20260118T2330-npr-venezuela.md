## Article summary
The capture of Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro by U.S. special forces has not led to a democratic transition, primarily because Diosdado Cabello, Maduro's enforcer, remains in power. Cabello, Venezuela's Interior Minister, has maintained control of the streets and security forces. He has been indicted in the United States on drug trafficking and narco-terrorism charges, with a significant reward offered for his arrest. Despite these charges, Cabello, a close confidant of Hugo Chávez, continues to wield considerable influence. He previously served as interim president and played a key role in closing media outlets critical of the government. Although he was seen as Chávez's potential successor, Maduro was chosen. Cabello later led the National Assembly and the ruling Socialist Party. In 2024, he was appointed interior minister to suppress protests following allegations of election fraud, leading to a crackdown. Human rights activists and opposition figures believe his continued presence, along with armed groups like the colectivos, makes returning to Venezuela unsafe and hinders democratic progress. His TV program, "Con el Mazo Dando," continues to criticize dissidents. Experts suggest Cabello might seek a deal or lie low to avoid Maduro's fate, potentially by cooperating with interim President Delcy Rodríguez, though his ambitions may persist.
