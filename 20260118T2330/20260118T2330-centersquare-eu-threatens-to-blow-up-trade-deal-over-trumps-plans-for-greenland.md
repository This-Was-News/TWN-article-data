## Article summary
A top European official has stated that President Donald Trump's recent comments regarding Greenland could jeopardize a trade deal between the U.S. and the European Union. Manfred Weber, president of the European People's Party, indicated that his party would not support a deal under the current circumstances and called for a halt to proposed 0% tariffs on U.S. products. This statement follows Trump's announcement of potential tariffs on European countries if a deal to annex Greenland is not reached. European Union Commission President Ursula von der Leyen expressed solidarity with Denmark and Greenland, warning that tariffs could escalate tensions and damage transatlantic relations. Trump had threatened tariffs ranging from 10% to 25% on goods from Denmark, Norway, Sweden, France, Germany, the United Kingdom, the Netherlands, and Finland, stating these would remain until Greenland is acquired. He emphasized the long-standing U.S. interest in purchasing Greenland, citing its strategic importance. European nations have reportedly sent troops to Greenland amid these escalating discussions. This situation arises shortly after Trump announced a separate trade deal with the EU that would impose a 15% tariff on imports from the bloc.
