## Article summary
The U.S. Supreme Court is set to hear arguments in Trump v. Cook, a case concerning President Donald Trump's authority to fire Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook. Trump accused Cook of mortgage fraud prior to her appointment, citing this as grounds for dismissal. However, the Federal Reserve Act stipulates that board members can only be fired for cause. A lower court judge reinstated Cook, arguing she has rights to notice and a hearing before termination, likening her position to that of a civil servant. The Trump administration contested this, asserting the president's "plenary power" to remove officials in multi-member commissions exercising executive power, without requiring notice or a hearing. This case is being heard alongside Trump v. Slaughter, which challenges the removal of a Federal Trade Commission member. The Supreme Court's decision could potentially overturn a nearly 90-year-old precedent limiting presidential removal powers. The case also arises amid ongoing public criticism from Trump towards Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell regarding interest rate decisions and a Justice Department investigation into Powell. The Supreme Court's ruling, expected by July, will determine whether the lower court's order reinstating Cook will stand.
