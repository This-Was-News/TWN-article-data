## Article summary
Iran has vowed to engage in all-out war if its Supreme Leader, Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, is targeted, following President Donald Trump's calls for regime change in Tehran. This strong warning came from Iran's reformist President Masoud Pezeshkian, who also blamed the United States for the hardships faced by the Iranian people due to sanctions. Pezeshkian stated that any aggression against the Supreme Leader would be considered an all-out war against the Iranian nation. These remarks represent a rare departure from Pezeshkian's usual conciliatory tone, as he defended the government's response to recent protests. Trump had previously expressed a desire for new leadership in Iran, calling Khamenei a "sick man" who mismanages his country and causes suffering. Khamenei himself has frequently accused the U.S. and Israel of instigating the protests, which erupted due to poor economic conditions. He declared that the Iranian nation had thwarted U.S. attempts to orchestrate "sedition" and demanded accountability. Despite not seeking war, Khamenei vowed to pursue both internal and international "criminals." Tensions between the U.S. and Iran have escalated since the protests began, with Trump threatening intervention against any crackdown. Human rights groups estimate a significant number of protesters were killed during the crackdown, further complicated by an internet blackout.
