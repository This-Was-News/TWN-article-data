## Article summary
A Senate Judiciary Committee hearing has exposed significant failures in the vetting process for Afghan parolees admitted to the U.S. under Operation Allies Welcome. Officials admitted that many of the nearly 100,000 evacuees were admitted without essential biographic data or biometrics, leading to a loss of track of thousands of individuals. Senator John Cornyn highlighted that 18,000 admitted individuals were known or suspected terrorists, with 2,000 having ties to terrorist organizations. Senator Josh Hawley raised concerns about tens of millions of dollars in funding allegedly going to pro-terrorist organizations that were supposed to assist in the refugee process. This comes as Congress is set to vote on nearly $6 billion in continued funding for refugee programs, which saw a dramatic increase under the Biden administration, rising from less than $2 billion in FY21 to nearly $9 billion in FY22. Despite the vetting issues, many refugees continue to qualify for numerous taxpayer-funded benefits, including SSI, SNAP, and housing assistance. Afghan refugees, in particular, face challenges with lower English proficiency, educational attainment, and labor force participation, leading to higher poverty rates and continued reliance on government programs. The total federal assistance for refugee programs has reached approximately $30 billion over four years, with significant portions allocated to Refugee and Entrant Assistance programs.
