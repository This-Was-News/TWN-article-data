## Article summary
Leaders from eight European nations, including Denmark, France, Germany, and the United Kingdom, have jointly condemned President Trump's recent threats to impose tariffs on their imports. These tariffs are linked to military actions taken by these countries in Greenland as part of a Danish-led Arctic exercise. The European leaders stated that Trump's proposed tariffs "undermine transatlantic relations and risk a dangerous downward spiral." They reaffirmed their commitment to NATO goals and to engaging in dialogue based on sovereignty and territorial integrity, rejecting Trump's repeated threats to take control of Greenland. Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen of Denmark expressed pleasure with the unified European stance, stating, "Europe will not be blackmailed." The coordinated statement represents an unusual diplomatic pushback from key U.S. allies, with individual leaders also voicing strong criticism. The tariff threats follow weeks of assertive U.S. rhetoric regarding Greenland's strategic importance. Greenlandic and Danish leaders have consistently maintained that the territory is not for sale. The tariff decision has also faced criticism from bipartisan voices in the U.S. Congress, who warn it could damage NATO cohesion and U.S. credibility.
