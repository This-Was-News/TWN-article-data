## Article summary
Bishop Rob Hirschfeld of the Episcopal Church of New Hampshire has urged his clergy to prepare for a new era of "martyrdom," advising them to finalize their wills and organize their affairs. This call to action came during a vigil honoring Renee Good, who was fatally shot by an ICE officer. Hirschfeld invoked historical figures like Jonathan Daniels, a seminary student killed while protecting a civil rights activist, suggesting that clergy may need to physically stand between "powers of this world and the most vulnerable." He emphasized that Christians should not fear death and must be prepared to build a new world. Other religious leaders, including Presiding Bishop Sean W. Rowe and Minnesota Bishop Craig Loya, have also called for protecting the vulnerable and responding to hatred with love and hope, aligning with the Episcopal Church's stance on supporting immigrants and refugees.
