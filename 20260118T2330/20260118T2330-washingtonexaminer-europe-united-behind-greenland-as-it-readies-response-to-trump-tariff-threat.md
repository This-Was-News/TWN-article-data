## Article summary
Europe stands united behind Greenland following President Donald Trump's threat to impose tariffs over its support for the Arctic island. Several European leaders, including those from Denmark, Finland, the Netherlands, the United Kingdom, France, Germany, Norway, and Sweden, issued a joint statement expressing solidarity with Greenland and signaling openness to discussing security concerns. They emphasized their commitment to strengthening Arctic security as a shared transatlantic interest and upholding sovereignty and territorial integrity. The leaders warned that Trump's threatened tariffs, set to take effect on February 1, would undermine transatlantic relations and risk a dangerous downward spiral, potentially initiating another trade war. In response to Trump's tariff threat, a key leader in the European Parliament indicated that the EU-U.S. trade deal reached last summer would not be approved. The European Union is reportedly considering retaliatory actions, including potential tariffs worth over $107 billion on the United States and barring U.S. companies from the European market. This united front from Europe signals a strong stance against Trump's acquisition efforts and potential trade disputes.
