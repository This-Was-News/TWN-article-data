## Article summary
Republicans and Democrats in Congress are attempting to curb President Donald Trump's aggressive stance on acquiring Greenland, a move that has raised concerns about the future of NATO and global alliances. Lawmakers have voiced opposition through speeches, proposed legislation, and diplomatic meetings with Danish officials. Despite these efforts, Trump continues to insist on taking control of the Arctic island and has threatened tariffs on European nations that oppose his plans. Key Republicans, while avoiding direct confrontation with Trump, have publicly stated their disapproval of forceful acquisition and the potential negative impact on international relations. Some lawmakers are exploring legislative options, such as prohibiting the use of defense funds for attacking NATO allies without consent, and have considered war powers resolutions to limit the president's unilateral military actions. However, the Trump administration's past actions suggest a reluctance to seek congressional approval for military endeavors. The effectiveness of these congressional measures remains uncertain as Trump's "go-it-alone" foreign policy continues to create global apprehension, with some Republicans expressing support for Trump's desire to possess Greenland but downplaying forceful methods. A few retiring Republican lawmakers have been more vocal in their criticism, with one suggesting an invasion could lead to Trump's impeachment.
