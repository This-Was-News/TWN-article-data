## Article summary
As antigovernment protests surge in Iran, President Masoud Pezeshkian has vowed to address economic grievances but maintained a tough stance against demonstrators, who rights groups report number nearly 200 dead, with some estimates significantly higher. The protests, initially sparked by economic distress over currency devaluation, have evolved into a widespread movement calling for the overthrow of the clerical rulers. The Iranian authorities have imposed an internet blackout, making it difficult to assess the full extent of the violence and the number of casualties, which include both protesters and security personnel. Senior officials have accused the United States and Israel of backing the protesters, while Israel has expressed support for the demonstrators' struggle for freedom. The situation is further complicated by threats of U.S. military action against Iran, which Iranian officials have warned would be met with retaliatory strikes. Government buildings have been set ablaze, and authorities claim that trained elements, not security forces, are responsible for many of the deaths, with a significant number of fatalities caused by bladed weapons and close-range gunfire. Over 10,000 people have reportedly been arrested, and there are accounts of security forces storming hospitals to apprehend injured protesters. While President Pezeshkian has previously acknowledged economic pain and offered minor reforms, his recent statements indicate a hardening of his position, accusing foreign enemies of deploying "trained terrorists" to incite riots. Meanwhile, the U.S. president has expressed readiness to help Iran achieve "FREEDOM," a sentiment dismissed by President Pezeshkian, who warned against U.S. intentions based on recent events in Venezuela.
