## Article summary
Federal agents conducted immigration arrests in Minneapolis, a city already on edge following the fatal shooting of Renee Good by an immigration officer. During one arrest, agents rammed a door and entered a home, later pepper-spraying protesters who attempted to disrupt the operation. Video footage showed a confrontation where agents pushed back protesters, and a woman emerged from the house with a document for the arrest, which was not a judicial warrant. Immigrant advocacy groups have advised people not to open their doors unless agents have a judge-signed court order. Over 2,000 immigration arrests have been made in Minnesota since December as part of a large-scale enforcement operation. The community is anxious, with Minneapolis public schools offering remote learning due to safety concerns. State Democrats are calling for state authorities to be included in the investigation of Good's shooting, questioning the federal government's objectivity. The Trump administration defended the officer involved, stating the officer acted in self-defense. The ICE acting director suggested these operations would be unnecessary if local jurisdictions cooperated more. The recent incidents, including Good's death and events in Portland, have sparked protests nationwide.
