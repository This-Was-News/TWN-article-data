## Article summary
The Department of Homeland Security (DHS) has implemented a new policy restricting congressional visits to Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) facilities, specifically impacting a visit to a Minneapolis facility. Under this new policy, lawmakers must now request visits at least seven days in advance. Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem stated that the facilities are funded by the "One Big Beautiful Bill Act," which she claims exempts them from the federal law allowing unannounced congressional visits. This policy was cited when three Minnesota congresswomen, Reps. Ilhan Omar, Angie Craig, and Kelly Morrison, were initially allowed into the Whipple Federal Building in Minneapolis but were subsequently asked to leave and blocked from touring the facility. Secretary Noem justified the policy by citing the need for adequate protection for all individuals involved and a desire to prevent "circus-like publicity stunts." The congresswomen's visit occurred in the context of heightened tensions in Minneapolis following the fatal shooting of a woman by an ICE agent. Rep. Angie Craig emphasized the congressional duty to ensure humane treatment of detainees. The deployment of over 2,000 federal immigration agents to Minnesota adds to the ongoing situation.
