## Article summary
Reza Pahlavi, the exiled son of Iran's deposed shah, has publicly endorsed U.S. intervention to support ongoing mass protests against the Iranian theocracy. Pahlavi expressed hope that President Trump would help bring the current regime "down for good." He believes that Trump's warnings against the Iranian government have emboldened protesters, who have seen their numbers swell to the hundreds over two weeks of demonstrations. Pahlavi, who has lived in exile since the 1979 revolution, directly appealed to Trump, stating that the Iranian people feel empowered by the hope that the U.S. has their back. He emphasized the need to "cut the snakes head off for good" to eliminate the threat the regime poses to Iranian, American, and regional interests. While Pahlavi has historically been a divisive figure among the opposition due to the monarchy's past repressive rule, he appears to have gained recent support within Iran. The current protests, initially sparked by a currency devaluation, have evolved into broader calls for the ouster of Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, with some protesters even chanting "long live the shah." Pahlavi and activist groups have been instrumental in accelerating the protest movement. This plea for intervention comes amid reports that President Trump has been briefed on options for military strikes against Iran, following a similar U.S. operation in Venezuela.
