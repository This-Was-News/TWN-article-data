## Article summary
Antigovernment protests in Iran have escalated, growing in size and violence as security forces crack down on demonstrations that began over economic woes and broadened into a challenge to the country's clerical rulers. Nearly 200 people may have been killed, with restrictions on the internet hindering independent assessments. The government acknowledges economic difficulties but blames external forces for unrest, vowing to address grievances while warning against destabilization. Iran's economy has been under pressure due to sanctions and a past conflict, with a recent currency plunge triggering widespread protests. Initially focused on economic issues, the rallies evolved into broader criticism of the theocratic government, with protesters chanting anti-dictator slogans. Demonstrations have spread to numerous cities, resulting in thousands of arrests. The government has hardened its stance, with the prosecutor general warning of severe legal proceedings against protesters, equating their actions to being an 'enemy of God.' Internationally, Iran faces pressure, with the U.S. president threatening military action if lethal force is used against demonstrators, though lawmakers express wariness. Iran has vowed forceful retaliation against any intervention. Israel is monitoring the protests and expressing support for demonstrators. The unrest has also brought attention to Reza Pahlavi, the son of the deposed shah, who lives in exile and urged Iranians to protest and strike.
