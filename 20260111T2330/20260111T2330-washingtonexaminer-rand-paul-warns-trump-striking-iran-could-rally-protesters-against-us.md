## Article summary
Senator Rand Paul has cautioned President Donald Trump against military strikes in Iran, warning that such actions could inadvertently rally the Iranian population around their government rather than supporting the protesters seeking freedom. While Trump has publicly stated the U.S. is ready to help Iranian protesters gain freedom, and Senator Lindsey Graham has fueled speculation of imminent U.S. intervention, Paul expressed a more reserved stance. Paul hopes for the success of the freedom movement in Iran but doubts that bombing the country would achieve the intended effect. He believes that external military intervention can sometimes backfire, causing citizens to unite against a foreign aggressor. Furthermore, Paul emphasized that it is not the U.S.'s role to involve itself in every global freedom movement and suggested that encouraging the pursuit of free elections is a more constructive approach. He also highlighted the constitutional requirement for Congress to authorize acts of war, referencing his past efforts to restrict the president's ability to engage in military actions without congressional approval, such as in Venezuela. Paul pointed to historical examples, like the votes for war following Pearl Harbor or 9/11, contrasting them with closer votes for other conflicts, underscoring the importance of congressional deliberation on matters of war.
