## Article summary
A bipartisan group of lawmakers has introduced House Resolution 981, a proposal aiming to cap annual federal deficits at 3% of the Gross Domestic Product (GDP) by 2030. This measure seeks to curb the nation's growing debt, which currently stands at approximately 6% of GDP, double the proposed target. The resolution mandates that the House Budget Committee recommend enforcement mechanisms within 180 days and that the Rules Committee suggest rule changes to facilitate deficit reduction, such as making budget rules harder to waive and requiring the Congressional Budget Office to analyze the fiscal impact of major legislation. Proponents, including Republican Bill Huizenga and Democrat Scott Peters, emphasize the urgency of addressing the national debt to ensure long-term economic security. They highlight that unchecked debt could lead to interest payments crowding out essential government services like defense and healthcare. Last year, interest costs on the national debt exceeded military spending. Budget watchdogs have expressed support for the resolution, viewing the 3% target as both achievable and sufficient to reassure markets about the sustainability of U.S. debt. They believe that reducing annual deficits is crucial to mitigating the risk of a debt-induced economic crisis.
