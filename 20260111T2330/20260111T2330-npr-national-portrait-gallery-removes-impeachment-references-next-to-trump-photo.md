## Article summary
The National Portrait Gallery has updated the wall text accompanying a new portrait of President Trump, removing references to his two impeachments and other details about his presidency. A Smithsonian spokesperson stated that this is part of a planned update to the "America's Presidents" gallery, which will feature new labels and wall text, exploring quotes or general information like the artist's name. Previously, the label mentioned Trump's Supreme Court nominations, the development of the COVID-19 vaccine, and his impeachments. The current text identifies his birth year, his terms as the 45th and 47th president, and details about the photographer. The Smithsonian noted that other museums, like the National Museum of American History, provide information on presidential impeachments. This change occurs amidst a tense period for the Smithsonian, following an executive order from Trump aimed at removing "divisive race-centered ideology" and a "comprehensive internal review" of several Smithsonian museums. The former director of the National Portrait Gallery, Kim Sajet, resigned in May after Trump attempted to remove her. The Smithsonian previously rotated two other photographs of Trump before displaying the current one, taken by Daniel Torok. A White House spokesperson described the new portrait as "iconic."
