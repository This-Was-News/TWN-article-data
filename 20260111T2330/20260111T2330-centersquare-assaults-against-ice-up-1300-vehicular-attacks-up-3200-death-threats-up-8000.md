## Article summary
Assaults against U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) officers have surged by 1,300%, vehicular attacks have increased by 3,200%, and death threats have risen by 8,000%. These alarming statistics come in the wake of several violent incidents involving ICE and Border Patrol officers. In Minneapolis, a U.S. citizen was shot and killed during an alleged vehicular attack on an ICE officer, an event federal authorities deemed justified self-defense while local officials dispute this. Shortly after, in Portland, Oregon, two Venezuelan nationals, identified as alleged Tren de Aragua gang members, were shot by a Border Patrol agent after attempting to run him over with their vehicle. The Department of Homeland Security (DHS) also described this incident as self-defense. Both individuals had illegally entered the U.S. and were released into the country by the Biden administration, with one having prior arrests and a removal order, and the other involved in a prostitution ring and a previous shooting. Previous incidents include a Mexican national shot in September after driving his car at law enforcement, and another Mexican national shot in October after ramming a law enforcement vehicle. In a separate October incident, a U.S. citizen was shot during an immigration enforcement operation in Los Angeles after allegedly accelerating towards federal agents. These events highlight a significant and unprecedented increase in violence against federal immigration enforcement officers.
