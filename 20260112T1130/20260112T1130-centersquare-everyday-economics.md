## Article summary
The recent jobs report indicates a stalled labor market, showing modest payroll growth but a loss of momentum. Key takeaways include narrow and uneven job growth across industries, a shift where labor supply now exceeds demand, and a low-hire, low-fire environment suggesting employers are hesitant to expand payrolls. Revisions to previous months' data reveal the labor market was weaker entering the year than initially reported, with 2025 experiencing the weakest job creation since the aftermath of the Great Financial Crisis, excluding the pandemic year. This data is crucial as upcoming inflation and retail sales reports will provide insights into easing price pressures and consumer spending, while housing market activity remains historically weak due to affordability constraints. The Federal Reserve is likely to hold interest rates steady at its next meeting, as inflation remains above target and the labor market, while not collapsing, is losing forward motion. Policymakers are advised to exercise patience, waiting for more convincing signs of cooling inflation or further labor market weakening before adjusting policy. The current economic environment suggests a period of stagnation rather than an imminent collapse.
