## Article summary
The expiration of enhanced Affordable Care Act (ACA) subsidies at the end of 2025 has created a cost crisis for millions of Americans relying on marketplace health insurance. For individuals like Mathew, a 40-year-old in Michigan with an autoimmune condition, the monthly premiums are set to skyrocket from $181 to over $427, an unaffordable increase. Facing this untenable situation, Mathew made the drastic decision to marry his roommate and best friend of 25 years, Christina, who has employer-sponsored health insurance. This unconventional solution, while highlighting the absurdity of the situation, allows Mathew to maintain coverage for his essential medical treatments. Experts note that while marrying for benefits was more common before the ACA, the lapse in subsidies is pushing people back towards pre-ACA workarounds. Marriage for insurance purposes is not illegal, and people often marry for various non-romantic reasons, including economic and practical ones. Mathew's new plan costs $121 per month, similar to his previous subsidized premium, but he is now navigating the complexities of transferring his care. He expresses frustration with the situation, hoping Congress will reinstate the subsidies, as not everyone has a close friend they can marry for insurance.
