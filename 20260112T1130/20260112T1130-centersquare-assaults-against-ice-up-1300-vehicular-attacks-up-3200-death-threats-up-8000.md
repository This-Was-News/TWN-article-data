## Article summary
Assaults against U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) officers have surged by 1,300%, vehicular attacks have increased by 3,200%, and death threats are up by 8,000%. These alarming statistics follow a recent incident in Minneapolis where a U.S. citizen was shot and killed during an alleged vehicular attack on an ICE officer. The Department of Homeland Security (DHS) deemed the shooting justified and in self-defense, though local and state officials dispute this. In a separate incident in Portland, Oregon, two Venezuelan nationals, identified as alleged Tren de Aragua gang members, were shot by a Border Patrol agent after reportedly attempting to run him over with their vehicle. DHS also described this shooting as an act of self-defense. Both individuals had illegally entered the U.S. and were released into the country by the Biden administration, with one having prior arrests and a removal order, and the other allegedly involved in a prostitution ring and a previous shooting. Previous incidents also highlight a pattern of violence, including a September shooting where an ICE officer was dragged by a car and fired in self-defense, and an October incident where a man was shot after ramming his vehicle into law enforcement. These escalating attacks underscore a significant increase in threats against federal immigration enforcement officers.
