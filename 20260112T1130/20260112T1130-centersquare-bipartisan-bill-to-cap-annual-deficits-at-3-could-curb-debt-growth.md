## Article summary
A bipartisan proposal, House Resolution 981, has been introduced by lawmakers aiming to cap annual deficits at 3% of the Gross Domestic Product (GDP) by 2030. This measure seeks to curb the growth of the national debt, which has seen deficits double that target in recent years, with last year's deficit at approximately 6% of GDP. The resolution mandates that the House Budget Committee propose enforcement mechanisms within 180 days and that the Rules Committee suggest rule changes to help achieve the target, including making budget rules harder to waive. Proponents, including Republican Bill Huizenga and Democrat Scott Peters, view this as a necessary step to ensure America's long-term economic security. They highlight that unchecked debt could lead to interest payments crowding out essential government services like defense and healthcare. Last year, interest costs on the national debt exceeded military spending. The resolution is supported by budget watchdogs, who believe a 3% deficit target is both achievable and sufficient to reassure markets about the sustainability of U.S. debt. They argue that reducing annual deficits is crucial to mitigating the risk of a debt-induced economic crisis.
