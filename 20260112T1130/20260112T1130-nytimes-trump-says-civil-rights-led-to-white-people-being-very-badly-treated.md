## Article summary
President Trump stated in a recent interview that he believes civil rights-era protections led to white people being "very badly treated," indicating that the concept of reverse discrimination is a core driver of his administration's stance against diversity policies. He suggested that affirmative action, for instance, unfairly prevented deserving individuals from attending college or securing jobs. This perspective aligns with grievances amplified by Vice President JD Vance and other officials who have encouraged white men to file complaints with the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC). Trump's comments reflect a broader narrative within his administration that white people are the primary victims of discrimination in America, a view he has espoused since his presidential campaign. Civil rights leaders, however, dispute this claim, with the NAACP president stating there is no evidence of discrimination against white men resulting from the civil rights movement. The Trump administration has actively dismantled diversity, equity, and inclusion (DEI) offices and halted enforcement of key Civil Rights Act provisions. EEOC Chair Andrea Lucas has notably promoted the agency's role in addressing discrimination against white male applicants and employees, a shift from its historical focus on aiding groups that have faced systemic discrimination. This strategy has been supported by figures like Stephen Miller and JD Vance, who have criticized DEI initiatives as discriminatory programs against white men. Labor and civil rights lawyers view Lucas's actions as an escalation in using civil rights laws to address perceived harms against white men, raising concerns about the agency's new direction.
