## Article summary
A massive winter storm is threatening over half of the U.S. population with snow, ice, and dangerously cold temperatures. The storm, which began in Texas with freezing rain, is expected to move across the country, bringing heavy snowfall to the Northeast. Forecasters warn of potential catastrophic damage, especially from ice, which could rival hurricane impacts. Millions are under watches and warnings, with concerns about extensive power outages due to ice-laden trees and power lines. Schools have canceled classes, thousands of flights have been grounded, and public events have been altered or postponed. Arctic air has already caused frigid conditions in the Midwest, with wind chills as low as minus 40 degrees Fahrenheit. Emergency shelters are preparing for an influx of people, and utility companies are bracing for widespread power disruptions, recalling a severe cold snap in Texas five years ago that caused major outages. Plumbing services are also anticipating a surge in calls due to the risk of frozen pipes. The Northeast is preparing for its heaviest snowfall in years, with cities like Boston declaring cold emergencies and considering travel restrictions. Many people are hunkering down, with some choosing to stay on campus at universities despite power outage concerns, preferring to be with friends during the severe weather.
