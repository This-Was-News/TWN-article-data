## Article summary
In January 2025, the prevailing thought in Washington was that President Donald Trump's push to broker a deal to keep TikTok operating in the United States was nothing more than a pipe dream. The president himself had explored banning the app during his first term, citing national security concerns, and then former President Joe Biden signed a bill into law that made TikTok go dark unless its parent company, ByteDance, sold off its top product to non-Chinese owners. However, senior Trump advisers now say that, apart from the constant public pressure to keep TikTok up and running voiced by the president himself, no one was more responsible for turning the president's dream into a reality than Vice President JD Vance. TikTok completed the Vance-brokered deal earlier this week, after both Washington and Beijing signed off on the framework late last year. The agreement will see TikTok's U.S. operations spun off into a new American joint venture, responsible for operating under defined safeguards that protect national security through comprehensive data protections, algorithm security, content moderation, and software assurances for U.S. users. Just over 80% of the joint venture will be owned by American interests, including 15% stakes each for a managing investor group comprising tech giant Oracle, private equity firm Silver Lake, and investment firm MGX. ByteDance will maintain a minority stake of 19.9% but have no operational oversight regarding U.S. operations. Multiple Trumpworld insiders told the Washington Examiner that Vance was the glue that held them together, even as Trump was forced to issue a series of executive orders last year that repeatedly delayed the banning of the app while the deal was slowly finalized. Alex Bruesewitz, a Republican operative and social media guru for the president's political arm, noted that the vice president has a great understanding of not just the political end of things, but also business and deal structure because that's his background. JD took this very seriously because he knew what was at stake, Bruesewitz said in an interview Friday. He also understood the complex issues at hand, the security concerns with Chinese influence, and he came to the table with complex solutions. Jason Miller, a longtime, out-of-government adviser to the president, scoffed at the idea of Vance serving as a bridge, suggesting the characterization makes it sound a lot easier than it was. 'This was peak diplomacy,' he told the Washington Examiner over the phone while waiting to depart Switzerland following the World Economic Forum. 'It took someone who definitely had the government experience, the Silicon Valley VC experience having been around a number of complex deals but also having the stature of being the sitting vice president to be able to pull this off.'
