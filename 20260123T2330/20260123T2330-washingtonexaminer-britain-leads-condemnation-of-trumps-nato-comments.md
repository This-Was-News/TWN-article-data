## Article summary
President Donald Trump has drawn widespread condemnation, particularly from Britain, for questioning whether NATO allies would aid the U.S. in a crisis. Trump stated in an interview that he was unsure if NATO members would be there for America, despite the alliance's Article 5, which mandates collective defense, having been invoked only once in response to the 9/11 attacks. Following the attacks, numerous NATO countries, including Britain and France, sent troops to Afghanistan and Iraq to fight alongside U.S. forces, with many sustaining casualties. U.K. Prime Minister Keir Starmer called Trump's remarks "insulting and frankly appalling," suggesting an apology was warranted. NATO officials and former military commanders highlighted the sacrifices made by allied troops in Afghanistan, emphasizing that allies have historically answered the U.S. call. French officials also recalled their commitment and losses in Afghanistan after Article 5 was activated. Retired U.S. Admiral James Stavridis noted that hundreds of NATO troops died under his command on the front lines in Afghanistan. Trump's long-standing skepticism of NATO was evident during the Davos World Economic Forum, where his demands regarding Greenland also surfaced.
