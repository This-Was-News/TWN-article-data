## Article summary
A federal court in Los Angeles has upheld California's congressional redistricting plan, which was designed to create more Democratic seats. The three-judge panel ruled 2-1 in favor of Governor Gavin Newsom and the state against a lawsuit that accused the redistricting of racial gerrymandering. The plaintiffs, including Assemblymember David Tangipa and the U.S. Department of Justice, argued the redistricting was illegal under the Voting Rights Act. However, the court found that the challengers failed to prove racial gerrymandering occurred, concluding that the gerrymandering was political-partisan, which is legal. The lawsuit challenged Proposition 50, a measure approved by nearly 65% of California voters. In a dissenting opinion, Judge Kenneth Lee suggested that racial gerrymandering was likely predominant in at least one district, citing the mapmaker's public statements about bolstering Latino voting power. Governor Newsom and Attorney General Rob Bonta praised the ruling, emphasizing that it upholds the will of the people. Tangipa indicated that the case might proceed to the U.S. Supreme Court, a possibility also acknowledged by Bonta.
