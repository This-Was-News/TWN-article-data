## Article summary
Greenlanders are expressing shock, anger, and fear in response to President Trump's offer to buy their territory. Pipaluk Lynge, a leading Greenlandic official, stated that Greenlanders are not stupid and will not sell their soul, rejecting the idea of being better off as Americans. For centuries, Greenland and its Inuit people have been largely ignored, but now Greenlanders are asserting themselves in discussions about their future. Despite reliance on Danish subsidies, Greenlanders prefer their Scandinavian welfare system, including free healthcare and education, over potential absorption into the United States. They feel a connection to Denmark, even with past colonial grievances, and are wary of being recolonized by a new power. Many Greenlanders are concerned about cultural differences, such as differing views on hunting practices, and do not want to be bought. Greenlandic officials are working to present a united front, with the prime minister stating a preference for Denmark over the U.S. if a choice must be made. While some internal disagreements exist, the overarching sentiment is a desire to maintain autonomy and avoid becoming part of the United States. One potential compromise discussed is a free association agreement, but some officials fear this could lead to Greenland becoming solely a mining island. Greenlanders are calling for international support as decisions about their fate are made far from their shores.
