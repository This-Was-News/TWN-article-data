## Article summary
Democratic Representatives Elissa Slotkin, Maggie Goodlander, and Jason Crow have stated they are under investigation by federal prosecutors for their participation in a video that advised military members to refuse illegal orders. Slotkin reported being contacted by the U.S. attorney's office for the District of Columbia, and The New York Times first reported her awareness of the investigation. A spokesperson for the U.S. attorney's office declined to confirm or deny any investigations. The video, released in November, featured six congressional Democrats with military backgrounds emphasizing that service members are not obligated to follow unlawful orders. President Trump denounced the video as "treasonous" and amplified a post suggesting the lawmakers should be hanged, though the White House later clarified he was not advocating for their death. Following the video, several lawmakers reported receiving death threats and bomb scares. Slotkin stated she has faced over a thousand threats, including a bomb threat at her home, and that her parents were victims of swatting. Crow accused President Trump of pressuring political appointees to harass him for speaking out, while Goodlander affirmed she would not be intimidated. The FBI had previously requested interviews with the lawmakers involved in the video. Additionally, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth announced the Pentagon would censure Senator Mark Kelly, another participant, leading Kelly to sue the Pentagon for retaliation. This situation occurs amidst allegations of the Trump administration using the Justice Department against political opponents.
