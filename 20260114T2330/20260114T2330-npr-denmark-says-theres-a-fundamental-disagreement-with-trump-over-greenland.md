## Article summary
Denmark's Foreign Minister Lars Løkke Rasmussen stated there is a "fundamental disagreement" with President Donald Trump regarding Greenland, following talks in Washington. Despite Trump's continued calls for a U.S. takeover of the Arctic territory, both sides agreed to form a working group to address differences. Rasmussen emphasized that this group should focus on American security concerns while respecting Denmark's sovereignty. Trump has been advocating for NATO's involvement in acquiring Greenland, citing national security and potential threats from China and Russia. In response, Denmark announced plans to increase its military presence in the Arctic and North Atlantic. Greenland's representatives have questioned Trump's assertions, with one student noting that Greenlanders benefit from their current status within the Kingdom of Denmark. Experts and some Greenlanders doubt the claims of Russian and Chinese threats, suggesting Trump's interest may stem from Greenland's untapped natural resources. A 1951 treaty already grants the U.S. broad rights to establish military bases in Greenland with Danish and Greenlandic consent. Danish and U.S. officials are continuing discussions, with a bipartisan congressional delegation also heading to Copenhagen.
