## Article summary
The U.S. State Department has announced a suspension of immigrant visa processing for citizens of 75 countries, including Afghanistan, Iran, Russia, and Somalia. This decision, led by Secretary of State Marco Rubio, stems from a November order that tightened rules for potential immigrants who might become public charges in the U.S. The suspension, effective January 21, aims to prevent foreign nationals from utilizing welfare and public benefits in the United States. This measure expands upon previous immigration restrictions imposed by the Trump administration. While immigrant visa processing is halted for these countries, non-immigrant visas, such as tourist and business visas, are not affected. However, a separate directive urges U.S. embassies and consulates to screen non-immigrant visa applicants for the possibility of seeking public benefits. Consular officials will now consider a broader range of personal details, including age, health, finances, and English proficiency, to assess an applicant's likelihood of relying on public assistance. The administration asserts this action is part of an effort to prevent abuse of the U.S. immigration system.
