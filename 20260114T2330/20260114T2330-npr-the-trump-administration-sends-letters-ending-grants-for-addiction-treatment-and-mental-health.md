## Article summary
The Trump administration has abruptly terminated hundreds of federal grants supporting mental health and addiction services nationwide, effective immediately. These cuts, potentially totaling around $2 billion, have sent shockwaves through the healthcare system, with nonprofit groups fearing severe loss of front-line capacity. Organizations report losing significant funding overnight, impacting critical services like overdose prevention and peer recovery support. The Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration (SAMHSA) indicated in termination letters that the defunded programs no longer align with the administration's priorities, aiming to reshape the national health system. Experts warn that these cuts could lead to the closure of programs and unravel the safety net for individuals facing addiction and mental health crises. This action follows previous Medicaid cuts, further straining providers. Advocates and medical professionals express grave concern, stating that these funding reductions endanger lives and dismantle essential recovery infrastructure. Programs targeted include those providing mental health training for school staff and initiatives aimed at encouraging students to enter the psychiatry field. The American Psychiatric Association and Foundation have called for the immediate reinstatement of the funding, emphasizing the catastrophic impact on millions with unmet mental health and substance use disorder needs.
