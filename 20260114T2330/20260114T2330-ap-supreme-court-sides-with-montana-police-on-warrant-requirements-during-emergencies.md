## Article summary
The Supreme Court has ruled in favor of Montana police in a case concerning warrantless entry into a home during emergencies. The unanimous decision found that officers acted lawfully when they entered William Case's home in 2021, responding to a welfare check requested by his former girlfriend. Case, who was shot during the incident, had argued that the evidence against him should be suppressed because the officers lacked a warrant. The Montana Supreme Court had previously ruled that officers only needed a reasonable suspicion of an emergency. However, the U.S. Supreme Court found this standard too low, while still upholding the officers' actions as objectively reasonable under a previous Supreme Court precedent. Justice Elena Kagan, writing for the court, emphasized that emergency entries are limited to what is necessary for aid and officer safety. The ruling clarifies the conditions under which law enforcement can enter a residence without a warrant when an emergency is suspected.
