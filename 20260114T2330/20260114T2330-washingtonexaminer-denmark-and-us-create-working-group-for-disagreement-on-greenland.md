## Article summary
Denmark and the United States have established a working group to address a fundamental disagreement regarding the future of Greenland. Danish officials met with Secretary of State Marco Rubio at the White House to discuss President Donald Trump's interest in acquiring the island, which he deems vital for national security. While Denmark acknowledges the evolving security situation in the Arctic due to Russian and Chinese activities, it firmly rejects the idea of a U.S. acquisition. Danish Foreign Minister Lars Løkke Rasmussen stated that while they share some security concerns, acquiring Greenland is "absolutely not necessary." Greenland and Denmark have emphasized their existing security pacts with the U.S., which allow for military presence and infrastructure development. The working group aims to explore ways to accommodate U.S. security concerns while respecting Denmark's sovereignty. Greenlandic Foreign Minister Vivian Motzfeldt expressed a desire to return to normalized relations and reiterated that Greenland is not for sale. Despite initial indignation from European nations, the U.S. has maintained its stance on the importance of Greenland for national security, with Trump suggesting that if the U.S. doesn't acquire it, Russia or China will. Both nations agree that dialogue is essential to navigate this disagreement and strengthen cooperation in the Arctic region.
