## Article summary
President Donald Trump has intervened in a stalled contract dispute between New York's Metropolitan Transportation Authority (MTA) and union leaders representing Long Island Rail Road workers. He signed an executive order establishing a second emergency board to mediate the negotiations, which have been ongoing for over a year. This new panel, appointed by the president, will investigate the good-faith efforts of both sides and has the power to recommend a resolution. The intervention grants both parties an additional 120 days to reach an agreement before any potential strike action can occur. A previous mediation board had recommended a 14% raise over four years, but the MTA offered a 9.5% raise over three years, which some unions accepted. Union officials have accused the MTA of delaying meaningful negotiation. Governor Kathy Hochul had previously criticized the unions for not negotiating in good faith and had also called for Trump to appoint a second emergency board. The potential strike could impact over 300,000 daily riders, as well as LIRR employees and their families. The new board will have 60 days to make non-binding recommendations, followed by a 60-day cooling-off period. If no agreement is reached after 120 days, a strike or lockout could commence.
