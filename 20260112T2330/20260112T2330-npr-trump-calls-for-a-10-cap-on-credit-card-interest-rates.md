## Article summary
President Donald Trump has proposed a one-year cap of 10% on credit card interest rates, citing abuses by credit card companies. This proposal comes as average credit card rates have surged, reaching 22.3% in November 2025, a significant increase from a decade prior. Trump aims for the cap to take effect on January 20, but it remains unclear if he has the unilateral authority to enforce it without congressional action. A bipartisan bill with a similar 10% cap has stalled in Congress, and similar legislation has been proposed in the House. Senators Bernie Sanders and Elizabeth Warren have both criticized Trump's approach, with Warren noting his administration's efforts to undermine the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB), an agency created to protect consumers from predatory financial practices. Republicans have historically accused the CFPB of overreach, and the Trump administration has curtailed its activities, although a recent court ruling mandated continued funding seeking. In response to Trump's announcement, bank stocks declined, and industry groups warned that such a cap could harm consumers by restricting credit access and driving them to riskier alternatives. They also argued that it would negatively impact families and small business owners.
