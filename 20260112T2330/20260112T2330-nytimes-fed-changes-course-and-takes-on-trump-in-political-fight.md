## Article summary
Jerome Powell, the Fed chair, has shifted from his strategy of avoiding conflict with President Trump to a more assertive stance. This change comes after the Justice Department initiated a criminal investigation into Powell, which the Fed views as a pretext to coerce them into lowering interest rates. Powell publicly condemned this action in a rare video message, asserting that the investigation is a consequence of the Fed setting interest rates independently, rather than yielding to presidential preferences. The subpoenas are related to the costly renovation of the Fed's headquarters, which Trump has used as a new avenue to criticize Powell. This unprecedented criminal investigation against a Fed chair marks a significant escalation from Trump's previous personal attacks and threats to remove Powell. The Fed, with Powell as a lawyer, perceives this as a direct assault on the central bank's autonomy, a principle designed to ensure monetary policy serves the public interest, not the president's agenda. This move has garnered widespread support for Powell from former Fed chairs and Treasury secretaries, who describe the investigation as a severe attack on the Fed's independence. The core of the conflict lies in the president's attempt to exert influence over the Fed's independent rate-setting authority, a protection established by Congress to prevent undue presidential sway over economic policy. The outcome of this confrontation could critically determine the future independence of the Federal Reserve.
