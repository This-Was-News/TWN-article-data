## Article summary
Minnesota and Illinois have filed lawsuits against the Trump administration, seeking to restrict the operations of Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) within their states. These actions are largely a response to a recent incident where an ICE agent shot and killed a Minneapolis woman. Illinois' lawsuit, joined by Chicago, alleges that ICE's tactics, particularly during Operation Midway Blitz, involved "violent and unlawful tactics" and "assaults" that violate the 10th Amendment and federal statutes. The suit describes federal agents as "military-trained personnel, carrying semi-automatic firearms and military-grade weaponry" who have "rampaged" through the state. Minnesota's lawsuit, filed in the U.S. District Court for the District of Minnesota, aims to halt or limit operations of ICE and Customs and Border Protection agents involved in Operation Metro Surge. Minnesota Attorney General Keith Ellison called the surge a "federal invasion," alleging it targets the state for its diversity and differences with the federal government, and violates the 10th Amendment. He also contends that the Department of Homeland Security's (DHS) use of "excessive and lethal force," "warrantless racist arrests," and targeting of courts, churches, and schools are arbitrary and capricious actions. The DHS reported that federal agents have arrested over 1,500 undocumented immigrants in Minnesota since Operation Metro Surge began. The lawsuits seek to prevent federal agents from arresting U.S. citizens and visa holders without probable cause and to ban agents from threatening physical force or brandishing weapons against individuals not subject to immigration arrest.
