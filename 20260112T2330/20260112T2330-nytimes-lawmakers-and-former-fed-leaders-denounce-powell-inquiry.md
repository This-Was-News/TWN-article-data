## Article summary
President Trump is exploring diplomatic options with Iran while also considering military strikes following the killing of protesters. He has threatened to impose tariffs on countries doing business with Iran, potentially affecting nations like China, India, and Turkey. Iran, facing internal unrest and mass protests, has expressed readiness for negotiations, though the specifics remain unclear. In a separate development, Minnesota and Illinois have filed federal lawsuits against the Trump administration over the deployment of immigration agents, alleging constitutional violations and infringement on states' rights. A federal judge has blocked the Trump administration's decision to halt construction on the Revolution Wind offshore project, ruling the suspension order was arbitrary. Senator Mark Kelly is suing Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth and the Pentagon over actions that could reduce his retirement rank and pension, stemming from a video he released reminding military members of their right to refuse illegal orders. The Justice Department has opened a criminal investigation into Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell regarding the renovation of the Fed's headquarters, which Powell has denounced as an attempt to coerce the central bank into lowering interest rates. This investigation has drawn criticism from some Republicans and former Fed officials who warn of the erosion of the Fed's independence. The administration is also nearing a trade deal with Taiwan that would lower tariffs and increase semiconductor investment in the U.S. Meanwhile, Mexico's President Claudia Sheinbaum reported a "very friendly" call with President Trump regarding security and trade, despite previous threats of U.S. military action. The Federal Reserve, under Powell's leadership, is reportedly preparing to fight back against the administration's pressure, marking a significant shift in its approach to defending its autonomy.
