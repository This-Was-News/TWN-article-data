## Article summary
President Donald Trump has expressed significant concern that a Supreme Court ruling against his tariffs could lead to the U.S. owing "many Hundreds of Billions of Dollars," potentially reaching "Trillions of Dollars" when factoring in investments made by companies to avoid tariffs. Trump stated on Truth Social that repaying these funds would be "almost impossible" and a "complete mess," warning that if the Supreme Court rules against the U.S. on this matter, "WE'RE SCREWED!" The federal government, in a filing with the Court of International Trade, indicated they would refund tariff revenue if required by a final court decision. However, Trump believes any quick and easy resolution would be a false claim. The Supreme Court is considering a legal challenge to Trump's authority to impose tariffs under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act of 1977. The Trump administration could be forced to refund over $133.5 billion in tariff revenue if the Supreme Court sides with the challengers, which include states and small businesses. Experts like Phillip Magness argue that Trump's claims of economic ruin are exaggerated and lack a basis in reality. Tariffs have been a cornerstone of Trump's agenda, with varied application since their initial imposition. Businesses have reported that tariffs have increased consumer prices.
