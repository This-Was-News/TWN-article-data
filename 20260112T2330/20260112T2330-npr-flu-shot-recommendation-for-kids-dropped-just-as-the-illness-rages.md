## Article summary
The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) has reversed its recommendation that all children receive an annual flu shot, shifting to a policy of "shared clinical decision-making" where parents consult with healthcare providers. This change comes at a time when flu cases and hospitalizations are surging, with doctor visits for flu-like illnesses reaching a nearly 30-year high. Pediatric deaths from the flu have already occurred this season, with most of the children who died last year being unvaccinated. This new recommendation has caused confusion and concern among doctors, parents, and public health experts, who fear it will lead to decreased vaccination rates and an increase in severe illness, hospitalizations, and deaths among children. The CDC's acting director, Jim O'Neill, stated that the decision is based on individual patient characteristics rather than population-wide factors and cited a review finding no randomized controlled trials demonstrating community transmission reduction from pediatric vaccines. However, independent experts dispute this, highlighting that last year's flu season was also severe and that childhood flu vaccination rates are already slightly behind last year. Some former CDC officials criticize the move, calling it dangerous and noting the cancellation of a successful flu vaccination campaign last year.
