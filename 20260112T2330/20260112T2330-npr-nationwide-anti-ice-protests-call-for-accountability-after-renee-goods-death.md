## Article summary
Thousands of people participated in nationwide protests over the weekend, demanding accountability following the death of Renee Good, a 37-year-old woman shot and killed by a U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) officer in Minneapolis. The protests, coordinated by the progressive grassroots coalition Indivisible, aimed to "grieve, honor those we've lost, and demand accountability from a system that has operated with impunity for far too long." Demonstrators in Minneapolis carried signs and chanted "ICE out now!" expressing concerns about potential increased violence with more ICE agents in the city. A "noise protest" the night before resulted in 29 arrests, with some individuals damaging hotel windows and injuring a police officer. Minneapolis Mayor Jacob Frey condemned property damage while supporting peaceful protesters. The Department of Homeland Security stated that the First Amendment protects peaceful assembly, not rioting, and announced plans to send "hundreds more" federal agents to Minneapolis. Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem warned that violent activities against law enforcement or interference with operations would result in accountability. Good's death occurred shortly after DHS launched a large-scale immigration enforcement operation in Minnesota. Similar protests took place in Philadelphia, Portland, Atlanta, Washington, D.C., and New York City, with some involving clashes and arrests, while others remained peaceful.
