## Article summary
A report from the Kiel Institute for the World Economy indicates that Americans are bearing the cost of nearly all of President Donald Trump's foreign tariffs, directly contradicting his claims that other nations absorb this financial burden. The German think tank's research suggests that for every $100 in tariff revenue collected by the U.S. government, $96 originates from American importers and consumers, with only $4 coming from reduced profits of foreign exporters. This finding aligns with other U.S.-based studies and reports from financial institutions and economists. The report argues that tariffs function more like a consumption tax on Americans than a tax on foreign producers. While the White House maintains that foreign exporters ultimately pay the tariffs due to their reliance on the U.S. market, the Kiel Institute suggests exporters may not lower prices if they believe tariffs are temporary. Previous projections from Goldman Sachs and studies on European wines indicated a significant portion of tariff costs would be passed on to American consumers and businesses. However, some, like former Trump trade adviser Peter Navarro, argue that bargaining power determines who truly pays. The Congressional Budget Office has also adjusted its projections, now estimating foreign businesses absorb about 5% of the tariff costs. In response to concerns about rising grocery prices, Trump has exempted certain food products from tariffs, and the Supreme Court is set to hear a case challenging his use of tariffs under a 1977 law.
