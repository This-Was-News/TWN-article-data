## Article summary
President Trump's actions suggest a departure from America's traditional role as a defender of international order, potentially placing the U.S. in the category of territorial conquerors. Unlike past U.S. foreign policy, which resisted conquest and stood against aggressors, Trump has reportedly considered using military force to acquire Greenland from Denmark, despite the territory's citizens not wishing to join the United States. This proposal, once considered delusional by a former cabinet secretary, is now debated as a serious proposition, highlighting a shift in what is considered normal political discourse. While the U.S. has a history of intervening in other nations' affairs, it has never done so against a loyal ally posing no threat, nor has it retained captured territory since the Spanish-American War. This approach contrasts sharply with the post-World War I era when America championed resistance to conquest. The article posits that Trump's worldview redefines the limits of American power, suggesting that only his personal morality and mind constrain his global ambitions. This stance challenges decades of international norms and U.S. treaty obligations, creating an "upside-down, might-makes-right world" where territorial conquest against allies is contemplated.
