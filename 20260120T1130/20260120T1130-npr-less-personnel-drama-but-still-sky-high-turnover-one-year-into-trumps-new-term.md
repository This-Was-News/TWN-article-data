## Article summary
One year into President Trump's second term, there's been a notable decrease in high-level staff and cabinet turnover compared to his first term. While turnover in the first year of his presidency was 35% for senior staff and two cabinet positions, it has dropped to 29% for senior staff with no cabinet turnover in the current term. This reduction in drama and shakeups is attributed to a focus on loyalty in hiring, with aides more closely aligned with Trump's political agenda. Unlike the first term, where many high-profile aides were fired, departures this term have largely been promotions, such as Sergio Gor becoming ambassador to India. However, a significant portion of the departures occurred within the National Security Council staff, including National Security Advisor Mike Waltz, who was promoted to ambassador to the UN. This NSC staff reduction is described as a strategic choice to streamline foreign policy, rather than internal White House conflict. The Brookings Institution analysis, which exclusively tracks high-level positions, does not account for turnover among career officials in other departments like the State, Justice, or Defense Departments, which have experienced significant changes.
