## Article summary
President Trump's second term is marked by a foreign policy that significantly deviates from traditional U.S. approaches, dismantling the post-World War II global order. This new approach, characterized by an "America First" stance, embraces elements of 19th-century U.S. foreign policy, such as a version of the Monroe Doctrine focused on the Western Hemisphere. Trump's strategy involves a more aggressive use of military force, with the U.S. having conducted operations in multiple Middle Eastern and African countries, and asserting control over territories like Greenland, often disregarding the concerns of allies. While supporters argue this "shock therapy" is necessary to combat complacency among allies, critics express concern that it undermines the credibility of alliances. Trump's administration has achieved some diplomatic successes, like a ceasefire in the Israel-Hamas conflict, but has struggled to influence major rivals like Russia and China. This shift has led U.S. allies to question Washington's reliability, prompting them to hedge their bets and reduce their dependence on the United States. As the old world order breaks down, the specifics of Trump's envisioned global system are still emerging, with a notable emphasis on unilateral action and a questioning of established international institutions.
