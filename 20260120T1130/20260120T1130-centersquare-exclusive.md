## Article summary
A congregant from Cities Church in St. Paul described a recent Sunday service disruption as feeling "surrounded" by protesters who were integrated into the congregation before the service began. The U.S. Department of Justice is investigating the protest, which was organized in part by Black Lives Matter Minnesota and targeted a pastor who works for U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE). Videos show protesters chanting and confronting congregants, leading to a tense situation. The church is standing by its pastor, emphasizing their support is based on faith rather than politics. The incident occurred amidst heightened tensions in the Twin Cities surrounding federal immigration enforcement, following recent events involving ICE agents and civilian deaths. Federal officials are considering charges, potentially under the Freedom of Access to Clinic Entrances (FACE) Act. The congregant recounted the suddenness of the protest during the service, the fear it caused among children, and the church's response of prayer and scripture reading. Political figures and commentators have weighed in, with some criticizing Democrats for silence and others defending the protesters' actions. The congregant expressed a desire for justice but also highlighted the congregation's response of love and forgiveness, hoping to show Christ to those who seem to hate them.
