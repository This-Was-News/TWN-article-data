## Article summary
President Donald Trump has invited several countries, including Israel, Russia, Belarus, Thailand, and the European Union, to join his new "Board of Peace," a body intended to oversee the next phase of the Gaza peace plan. While some nations like Morocco, Vietnam, Kazakhstan, Hungary, and Argentina have accepted, France has opted out, citing concerns about respecting the United Nations' structure. The board's mandate and decision-making processes are unclear, especially given the inclusion of countries with existing geopolitical tensions. Israeli Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich has strongly opposed the board, viewing it as detrimental to Israel's interests and advocating for military administration of Gaza. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu acknowledged differences with the U.S. regarding the board's composition but stated it wouldn't harm his relationship with Trump. The U.S. is expected to announce the official list of members during the World Economic Forum in Davos. Board members will oversee an executive committee responsible for implementing the Gaza peace plan, which includes deploying an international security force, disarming Hamas, and reconstruction. A $1 billion contribution can secure permanent membership. Meanwhile, Egypt's Foreign Minister met with the head of a Palestinian technocrat committee tasked with managing Gaza's day-to-day affairs. The UN World Food Program reported an expanded operation in Gaza, reaching over a million people monthly, but warned the humanitarian situation remains fragile. In other news, Israeli forces conducted a counterterrorism operation in Hebron, and three Palestinians were killed by Israeli forces in southern Gaza.
