## Article summary
China's declining birth rate, reaching its lowest point since the establishment of the People's Republic in 1949, highlights a long-standing struggle to manage its vast population. For millennia, a large population has been a source of national strength for China, but it has also created anxieties about resource management. The recent drop in birth rates, with the population decreasing by 3 million in the past year, presents a complex challenge for officials who aim to maintain national strength while ensuring sufficient young people for the future. The legacy of the one-child policy, implemented to curb population growth, has led to an aging population and unintended social consequences, such as a gender imbalance and increased loneliness among only children. Despite the policy's abolition, the government is now actively encouraging citizens to have more children, reversing decades of population control. This shift is evident in measures like tax exemptions for child-related services and incentives for marriage and childbearing. The declining birth rate also has international implications, particularly as India has surpassed China in population, intensifying a rivalry for regional and global influence. The situation mirrors historical challenges, echoing Mao Zedong's observation on managing contradictions among the people. Ultimately, China faces the question of whether its traditional values regarding offspring can coexist with the realities shaped by past policies and modern global life.
