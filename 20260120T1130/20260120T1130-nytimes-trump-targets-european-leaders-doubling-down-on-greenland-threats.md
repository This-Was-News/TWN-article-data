## Article summary
President Trump has intensified his controversial pursuit of Greenland, making social media posts directed at European leaders and reiterating his desire for the territory. He shared messages from French President Emmanuel Macron, who expressed confusion about Trump's Greenland ambitions and invited him to Paris. Trump also engaged with NATO's secretary general regarding Greenland's strategic importance for global peace and strength. Additionally, Trump criticized Britain's decision to relinquish sovereignty over the Chagos Islands, calling it "great stupidity" and linking it to his national security rationale for acquiring Greenland. These actions follow weeks of renewed tensions, including threats of steep tariffs on European allies. Greenland itself has stated it is not for sale and prefers remaining part of Denmark. Trump also threatened a 200 percent tariff on French wine if France refused to join his proposed "Board of Peace" for Gaza, a move described as "blackmail" by France's agriculture minister. Meanwhile, the Trump administration is battling Minnesota's lawsuit challenging a surge of immigration agents, arguing that the state is attempting to impede federal law enforcement. The administration insists that federal law supersedes local preferences, while Minnesota claims the operation violates its sovereignty and aims to provoke outrage. The legal dispute unfolds amidst protests and clashes involving Immigration and Customs Enforcement agents, including recent shootings.
